## Leading independent candidate: reusable loop geometry

Hypothetical useful result: a few explicit loop shapes give useful capacity upper
bounds across unrelated nonproduct families, with an explanation of what geometric
property makes them effective. A stronger descendant would identify a whole body
class for which a restricted loop family suffices.

The mathematical starting point is established, not a proposed inequality:
[Artstein-Avidan–Ostrover, Proposition 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.2631)
gives a variational principle over action-normalized loops. For a closed polygonal
loop gamma with positive action, its support length yields an upper bound of the
form (sum h_K(-J edge))^2/(4 action). Conventions must be checked against the
repository before implementation. A transferred loop need not remain a boundary
characteristic or retain its facet word. Thus new minimizing-orbit computations
are not a prerequisite for testing usefulness.

Preparation: inspect retained loop coordinates and verify normalization on a
source body, roughly 20–40 minutes if the coordinates are retained. Only then
consider a small off-source comparison of planar and genuinely four-dimensional
templates, including the cost of symplectic adaptation. Useful numerical bounds
seem plausible; a family theorem is substantially less certain. A failed local
template optimizer cannot prove that a loop shape is insufficient. Stop if the
advantage is source-specific, merely duplicates existing bounds, or requires
essentially resolving the original optimization problem. Interpretation and a
stable certificate interface could take substantially longer than the first check.
Jörn's valuation of the intermediate result determines whether substantial work
on this candidate is worthwhile; no implementation has begun.

## Optional theorem direction: stability under one new facet

Hypothetical result: every sufficiently shallow single-halfspace truncation of
HKO decreases its systolic ratio, with the original ten facets fixed. This would
address a boundary not covered by a theorem restricted to moving ten facets.
It would not establish unrestricted eleven-facet local maximality.

A short subagent audit reconstructed known minimizing characteristics through
23 of 25 vertices numerically (residual below 3.1e-12); simultaneous 72-degree
rotation supplies the remaining vertices. This is a reported arithmetic audit
of retained certified branches, not a newly retained exact coverage certificate.
It removes the proposed shortcut of searching for a vertex avoided by every
minimizer. Do not launch another sampled cutting campaign on that premise.

Single-cut stability remains plausible. At a simple vertex, removed volume has
fourth-order size; a second-order capacity loss would dominate. Exceptional
directions and limits toward normal-cone boundaries are the serious unresolved
issue. A proof could cost days or weeks. This direction is parked pending interest
in strengthening local maximality, rather than advertised as cheap discovery.
