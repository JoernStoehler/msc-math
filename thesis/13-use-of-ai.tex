\section{AI in the Research Process}\label{sec:use-of-ai}

The disclosure preceding the table of contents records the extent and
provenance of AI use in this thesis.  This section has a narrower purpose: it
examines what one retained case shows about the evidence needed to accept an
agent-produced code repair.  It does not estimate how much AI contributed,
whether AI accelerated the project, or who first originated particular ideas.

\subsection{Evidence boundary}

The case below is supported by an inspectable Git state and a retained replay
packet with an executable mutation contract.  Git identifies the defective
source state and the later repair, but it does not measure active working time,
establish mathematical correctness by itself, or record semantic acceptance.
The replay reconstructs the mathematical fixtures and regressions recorded for
four repair runs; the original prompt text is not recoverable.  These limits
rule out timing, productivity, model-ranking, and causal claims.

\subsection{A regression that can distinguish the defect}

A projection solver for a constrained quadratic problem contained the
historical sign defect.  Write a feasible point as
\(\beta=\beta_0+V\alpha\).  Stationarity in the retained modes then requires
\[
  H'\alpha=-V^{\mathsf T}H\beta_0.
\]
The frozen source state used the opposite sign.  Four isolated repair runs were
given this known defect.  Two received a minimal repair contract; two were also
required to construct a regression that failed before the repair and passed
afterwards.  The exact prompts are unavailable, so these descriptions are
reconstructed task contracts rather than verbatim prompt records.

The retained replay injects each reconstructed regression into the frozen
source, runs it once with the erroneous positive sign and once with the correct
negative sign, and records whether the test passes.  Table
\ref{tab:ai-sign-replay} gives the reproduced outcomes.

  \centering
  \caption{Mutation replay of four regressions for one known sign defect.  A
  discriminating regression fails when the bad sign is restored and passes
  with the correct sign.  The rows reproduce test behavior, not the original
  prompts or complete diffs.}
  \label{tab:ai-sign-replay}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
    \toprule
    Recorded condition & Bad sign restored & Correct sign \\
    \midrule
    GPT-5.5, minimal        & fail & pass \\
    GPT-5.5, verifier first & fail & pass \\
    GPT-5.6-sol, minimal        & pass & pass \\
    GPT-5.6-sol, verifier first & fail & pass \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Thus all four regressions passed with the corrected sign, but one also passed
when the defect was restored.  That test made the repaired implementation look
checked without distinguishing it from the defective implementation.  In this
case, the informative evidence was not an additional green test; it was the
demonstration that the test became red under the specific mutation it was
supposed to exclude.

This is one known-defect task with one run per condition, no randomization or
within-condition replication, and no verbatim prompt record.  The agents were
told the defect category.  The table therefore establishes neither an effect
of model generation nor an effect of the verifier-first instruction.  It also
does not estimate performance on bug discovery, proof development, or
open-ended mathematical work.  It establishes only the mutation sensitivity
of these four reconstructed regressions on the frozen source state.

\subsection{Bounded conclusion}

The case supports a local distinction between production and verification.  A
correct edit and a passing test are separate artifacts, and the test is
evidence for the repair only to the extent that it can detect the relevant
failure.  Here that ability was checked directly by restoring the bad sign.

Nothing in this case quantifies labor saved, development speed, cost, or net
productivity.  Commit counts, changed lines, session counts, and artifact
volume would not supply those missing comparisons.  The wider project record
contains heterogeneous selected episodes rather than a representative sample
or a human-only counterfactual.  Accordingly, this section offers no global
score for AI in mathematical research.  Its narrower conclusion is that an
acceptance criterion should itself be tested against the defect it is meant to
exclude.
