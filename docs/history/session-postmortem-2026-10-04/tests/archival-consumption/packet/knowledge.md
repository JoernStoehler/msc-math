# When local success leaves the larger task unfinished

This note answers four recurring questions: why a root can stop while it still knows what remains, why a supervisor may not prevent that, why more detailed/reviewed proposals can remain poorly justified, and why asking more questions can increase Jörn's work. Its purpose is to retain the investigation now so future agents do not need to reconstruct these incidents or ask Jörn to explain them again.

The source is the [3–4 October postmortem](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/REPORT.md), covering eight MSc/Codex workflow roots frozen at 23:24:26 UTC on 3 October. Its source key and selected evidence provide exact native lines and times. This note supplies conditional reasoning, not a current assignment or permission to change guidance, configuration, staffing or budget. The [output map](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/OUTPUTS.md) locates retained artifacts and distinguishes their worktree and acceptance boundaries. Current authority and waiting packages remain with [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md) and [coordination](../coordination/README.md).

## What this investigation adds to existing knowledge

The [September audit](../history/process-audit/REPORT.md) already explained that local review cannot establish whole-artifact readiness, an incumbent approach needs meaningful rivals, and communication/status can fail to provide actual coordination. [Planning history](../history/memories/planning-context.md) already established that deferring one proposed solution does not close its parent objective. October did not newly discover those maxims.

The additional evidence identifies the failed transitions. Some roots **read or wrote the unresolved parent obligation immediately before ending**. The missing task was not simply absent from their consulted record. A supervisor actually saw an ending, accepted the local runtime check and stopped; the native route also could not wake the idle parent, although this episode contained no correct warning defeated by that limit. A four-worker/root-assembly design survived multiple changes of mathematical work, even when reviews improved the content and timing. These distinctions narrow which remedies could address the observed failure.

Existing guidance was sometimes delivered and accurately repeated before recurrence. Adding another paragraph, moving a skill or improving task-memory retrieval may help a genuinely missing-context case; those interventions do not by themselves explain or repair the explicit-known-gap cases below. The value of this note is the checked distinction and its evidence, not an assumption that reading it enforces better behavior.

## If a root knows work remains but still stops

In configuration, completed accounting repairs were legitimately marked done. The broader selected ongoing-awareness question remained recorded, and the final answer explicitly named it, but the root issued no actual discussion question and did not continue independent work. In recurring information, the root read its running, owned, unblocked design task, changed it to ready while retaining its owner and unfinished original objective, then ended because its own “bounded pass” was complete. Jörn challenged both stops without supplying a new technical fact or authorization. Configuration resumed work. Recurring information remained stuck for additional minutes before Fix supplied a concrete delegated comparison. [Report: C L1609–1643](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#c-l1609-component); [R L1960–2099](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#r-l1963-next)

Distinguish three conditions: authority and information for further work exist; someone selects a valuable concrete next operation; and a process executes it. Project structure had already specified its proposed drafting package before ending. Recurring information still needed help selecting the next operation. A restart alone was not a demonstrated cure. [Report: S L1262–1365](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#s-l1262-gap); [R L2056/L2087–2099](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#evidence-r)

These are failures to connect local completion to the parent task's next action. A component's completion, an unresolved caveat, a stored `next_step` and a recorded owner can all be accurate while the work is idle. The record is not an independent guard when it reflects the same narrowing as the final answer. The evidence does not show that writing `done` or `ready` mechanically caused termination.

For a matching current case, identify the selected parent obligation and the actual transition: continued authorized work, a consequential issued request, an accepted transfer, a real blocker, or a completed/withdrawn assignment. A proposed handoff package is not an accepted transfer. Fix was receiving one recurring-information artifact; that did not transfer the remaining original design obligation. Check the scope of acceptance, not merely the presence of a receiver. “Discuss X” in a file is not a question the user has received. Explaining why no input is needed does not itself continue the work.

The boundary matters. Closing a repaired component is allowed. Configuration eventually had a genuinely complete selected local outcome; recurring information's terminal stop followed Fix's explicit resource directive. An unapproved substantial programme remains unapproved. A remedy that restarts every ending or executes every proposed next step would violate those cases. The supported counterfactual is only that certain next steps were immediately available; it is not a promise that an alternative whole programme would have passed.

## If a watcher exists but the parent still needs human rescue

Separate three questions: what obligation the observer judges, whether it sees the relevant event, and what its response can cause.

The renewed configuration supervisor directly read the root's final answer at 22:16:59. It accepted the runtime-adoption check as complete and ended at 22:17:31, even though the root had named broader ongoing work. Exact encrypted assignment wording was not recovered, so the record does not settle whether the parent supplied a narrow remit or the supervisor narrowed it. The observed coverage was nevertheless local. More frequent polling alone would not have changed an event it already saw. [Report: C L2463–2497](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#c-l2474); [supervisor L201–225](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#cs-l205)

A separate runtime limit constrains recovery. In the inspected Codex **0.160.0** native route, child finals and native messages were queued without starting a new parent turn, and native follow-up could not trigger a root. The trace shows the child's event arriving while the root remained idle; Jörn started the next turn, after which the queued report entered context. Fix successfully used a different app-server route to restart roots. This is release- and route-specific evidence, not a claim that all supervision is powerless.

No correct warning was produced and then defeated by transport in this episode. The conditional limitation still follows from the inspected route: a correct warning after termination cannot by itself resume the parent, while a working restart channel cannot correct an observer that endorses the wrong endpoint. The selected intervention has to address the actual missing link. For changed clients or transports, recheck the current [Codex reference](../codex.md) and relevant runtime contract instead of treating this historical implementation as permanent. Do not infer intervention authority merely from technical capability.

Positive controls remain: the supervisor found stale state, helped deliver usage reports and later checked a real local completion. Preserve those uses without promoting them into evidence of sustained parent-task control.

## If a plan is concrete and reviewed but still lacks a value case

Ask what the source finding actually establishes and which decision now uses it. In task design, “an evaluator and measured paths exist” supported feasibility, not priority. A writing auditor named a useful mathematical reconstruction and said it need not consume the whole five-hour cap. The root turned that into a weak rival—no individual repair justified the full window—when selecting research. The auditor corrected the cap-versus-target mistake and pointed back to a portfolio of bounded repairs; the root changed its recommendation within minutes. [Report: T L218–231](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#t-l218); [writing child L96/L128](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#wa-l96); [T L410/L450/L455](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#t-l410-allocation)

That positive repair identifies the missing operation: retain the finding's conditions while comparing actual alternatives under the user's outcome. It does not show all children were wiser than roots or all advice was ignored. Some children helped build weak initial portfolios; some precise comparative objections improved them. A risky proposal can still be worth selecting. A truthful caveat is neither proof of comparative value nor a reason to require guaranteed success before proceeding.

The staffing sequence shows a related inherited assumption. Four workers plus root assembly began as a plausible mathematical decomposition, then persisted across different proof and exposition packages. Fresh allocation review changed guaranteed work and strengthened assembly timing, with real reasons. A late proposal compared a smaller team too. What remained untested was the larger/delegated-assembly alternative, not every possible staffing comparison. [Report: T L410/L455/L717/L1114/L1787](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#t-l410-allocation); [allocation child L74/L90](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#ta-l74)

When work, remaining time or integration requirements materially change, the old decomposition is a candidate, not an established capacity constraint. Compare actual dependencies and receiving responsibilities, including delegated assembly where it could remove a bottleneck. More workers are not intrinsically better; the later sixteen-role inventory did not establish optimality or measured throughput. Reviewing several options that all retain the disputed bottleneck does not test it.

## If corrections keep coming but the proposal does not converge

The priorities root successively addressed vagueness, excessive dispatch detail, missing permission, inadequate value justification and context-poor questions. Each new object could answer the latest complaint while leaving another still-applicable requirement unsupported in the recommendation. Some caveats stayed written; the missing step was enforcing their implications in the comparison. The combined review object never became adequate merely because all the individual words—concrete, reviewed, autonomous, approved—had appeared somewhere. [Report: P L366/L414/L428/L435/L534–553](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#evidence-p)

Treat feedback as changing the current proposal while preserving the other accepted requirements. This is not a demand for a new universal checklist. The relevant conditions here were substantive: writeup value, known adverse prose/review evidence, a useful rival, sufficient mathematical context, the live human window and actual execution authority. A correction that only changes a label or final-answer wording leaves the decision untouched.

The same distinction applies to conversation. Acceptance of a messaging skill did not complete the original joint-design task. A challenge about ending with FAIL was not an instruction to give up. Correcting an unauthorized timer did not answer the original feasibility question. A later explicit withdrawal really did require stopping. Preserve what the utterance does to the active assignment rather than adopting a rule about isolated words such as “thanks,” “why” or “fail.” [Report: R L806–823](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#r-l806); [C L2938–3006](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#c-l2938); [Q L139–333](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#q-l139); [F L1227–1249](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#f-l1227)

## If a question exists but Jörn still has to manage the work

There are two different problems: inadequate content and unestablished delivery. Direct answers to particular questions show when the first is supported; a transcript alone does not prove every emitted prompt was displayed or read.

Jörn explicitly assigned comparative value work to the agent, yet received broad category menus and requests to repeat already-returned prose history. Other questions lacked the proof context or proposal referent needed to judge them. Useful open expert critique does not need predetermined answer branches, but it does need a prepared object and a consequential purpose. The agent must do retrievable source work and frame the tradeoff before asking the user to supply private knowledge, critique or authority.

For delivery, task design emitted an async question and accurately called receipt unconfirmed, then ended and waited through the known-unverified route. The handler's acceptance established server emission, not frontend rendering, persistence or receipt. At 22:49:14 the root finally repeated the full request directly in commentary and kept the turn open; the earlier unverified-route dependency should not be projected onto all later waiting. The exact historical disappearance mechanism is unresolved. Accurate uncertainty language did not make the earlier dependency reliable. [Report: T L1601–1662](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#t-l1603)

Real approval boundaries remain intact while useful independent preparation continues. When those preparations are exhausted, preserve the pending package and decision with its receiving owner in [coordination](../coordination/README.md); silence and a new owner do not authorize execution. Historical human availability and included quota are not current resources.

## If a proposed repair sounds convincing

Identify the exact failure it can bypass and the claim its evidence supports. Fix's proposed 42-run study changed after it finally inspected the live sessions and found existing trials and pending work. A broad catalogue can help discovery, but does not establish which repair advances the current task. The original request for multiple approaches remained valid; a general study was not thereby a justified prerequisite. [Report: F L62–135](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#f-l62) The installed Claude reset fallback ran the primary's same redemption script and client operation. It could recover some transient process failures; it did not bypass shared script/client faults and added authentication/model dependencies. A second interface did not establish an independent implementation. The reset result was still future at the report cutoff. [Report: Q L748/L1294/L1470](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#q-l748)

The stop-review wrapper replays changed runtime and evidence conditions, and the fresh reviewer's complete effective context was not captured. Their negative results are not a controlled estimate of general stop-review reliability. The messaging skill eventually passed one synthetic case and was accepted; that is a useful recovered component, not broad behavioral validation.

Check explanations against counterevidence too. Quota's confession that it treated an attempt as a completed reset conflicts with earlier explicit caveats; more caveats would leave the route problem unsolved. Self-written FAIL labels and copied incident lists are not independent corroboration. User rejection still establishes a failed interaction even when one literal diagnosis is wrong. [Report: Q L1041 and preceding messages](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#q-l1041); [C L2947–3006](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/report.html#c-l2947)

## What remains unproved

The causal traces support the distinctions above more strongly than a general claim about model incapacity, context overload or low speed. All historical roots requested Sol/high, while the same roots and narrower children produced useful outputs. Large inputs and delays do not isolate an internal cause. The supervised stopping case supplies a real runtime limitation alongside a decision error; neither should be generalized to unrelated cases.

The report includes a separately marked current-run example: its first version passed bounded reviews yet was submitted before further authorized investigation recovered material causal findings. This preserves a limitation of the producing system rather than implying that the authors have solved the behavior they describe.

A completed six-case paired paper test found **no robust incremental decision benefit** from the earlier frozen knowledge note. All twelve answers were adequate; both blind scorers tied five pairs, and the only difference was one scorer's debatable interpretation of custody in the sixth. Treatment added 2,137 words per case. Rich supplied facts and existing guidance produced a near-ceiling baseline. The test used current Astra agents and short hypothetical decisions, not this revision or historical Sol roots in long sessions. It did not measure savings from reconstructing these historical incidents; retained history and generic behavioral instruction are different uses. [Report: transfer-study result and retained protocol](../history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/tests/transfer/REPORT.md)

Use the relevant section and its evidence when the current decision matches it. Recheck runtime details after client/tool changes and current authority before action. The unresolved prevention questions do not require asking Jörn to reconstruct these documented incidents, and the existence of this note is not evidence that future behavior has improved.
