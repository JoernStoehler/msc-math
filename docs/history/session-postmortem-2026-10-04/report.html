Postmortem: why the MSc/Codex roots required repeated human rescue

Substantial activity produced useful components, but repeatedly failed to provide the service Jörn needed: select worthwhile work, obtain his input while it was available, continue the authorized work, and leave results with trustworthy completion boundaries. Instead, he repeatedly had to restart roots, reconstruct their questions, restore the relevant comparison and challenge their reviews. The attempted repair session reproduced those failures. No substantial unattended research/thesis programme was approved and launched during the reviewed window.

The strongest explanation is more specific than “the agents forgot the goal.” In several decisive cases they read or wrote the unfinished parent obligation immediately before ending, but did not turn it into a concrete next action. They treated completion of their latest bounded activity as sufficient to stop. Sometimes an already-described step was available; sometimes further decomposition was needed and was later supplied by another agent. Their task records preserved the gap, but did not make it actionable. A supervisor sometimes saw the ending and accepted the same narrower result. Its ordinary native communication also could not restart an idle root.

A second mechanism affected planning. Conditions on specialist findings were lost when those findings were used to select work. Corrections changed the latest criticized feature without establishing that the recommendation met other still-applicable requirements. Four workers plus root integration persisted across substantially different proposals; reviewers improved work within that design without adequately comparing a larger team with delegated integration. These defects made plans look increasingly concrete without resolving the judgments that actually justified spending the window.

Neither mechanism explains every incident. Quota setup also involved misunderstanding the requested service and a fallback with shared dependencies. Configuration eventually achieved a legitimate local accounting outcome. The earlier error-list audit appears to have completed its narrower request. Some later stops were explicitly instructed. Those distinctions matter both for recovering useful work and for avoiding repairs that would turn legitimate completion or a real approval boundary into endless activity.

This investigation is retained now to avoid paying for the same reconstruction through Jörn’s attention and future compute. Existing September guidance already covered parent-objective loss, inadequate local review and delivery problems. October adds evidence about where those principles failed to control decisions, which apparently plausible repairs did not address the mechanism, and which explanations the traces rule out. Saving another reminder is not itself evidence that behavior has improved.

Household power and host backups are excluded. Quota redemption was scheduled for 04:00 Berlin / 02:00 UTC, after both the freeze and this report’s deadline; its result remains pending. Judgment and continuation effects are separate requirements. This episode did not contain a correct warning defeated by the transport: the observer endorsed the local endpoint. Plans became more detailed without preserving the whole case

Existing evaluators and measured paths supported the feasibility of an unattended saturation investigation. What the evidence now settles

The strongest conclusions are operational and conditional, rather than six equally plausible general causes.

First, missing task memory is inadequate for the decisive stopping cases. Known gaps were immediately written or reread before ending. The supported failure lies between that knowledge, selection of a concrete next contribution, and a justified stopping decision. Some cases had the next preparation already specified; others needed further decomposition and received it later from Fix. Attention or salience may still contribute internally; the traces do not measure them. Another persistence paragraph, automatic restart or dashboard refresh would not by itself supply a valuable missing next action.

Second, local review and supervision did not consistently test the disputed transition. Some reviews were valuable and some local endpoints valid. But specialist correctness, incorporated review findings and a completed runtime check could all coexist with an unclosed parent obligation or untested allocation. Independence must concern the particular assumption and evidence, not merely a different agent name. For the inspected native route, observation after root termination also lacked an automatic parent-resume effect.

Third, task and plan constraints were not reliably preserved through refinement. The successive proposals, child correction and staffing sequence support this directly. “More detail,” “ask a question,” “compare options” and “use a reviewer” each occurred without making the combined decision adequate. A future diagnosis should inspect the meaning carried from source finding to recommendation and from correction to revised proposal, not infer success from the presence of those activities.

Fourth, authority boundaries were real but did not require all the observed inactivity. Useful analysis and preparation resumed without new permission after several protests. Large execution and hook activation nevertheless remained gated, and some stops were explicit. Removing the gate would have allowed weakly selected work to run; it would not establish its value. Changing ownership or ending a turn cannot silently convert a waiting package into approved work.

Fifth, several tempting explanations of individual incidents are contradicted or limited by the evidence. Accounting’s original twenty passing tests missed silent fork/missing-counter errors; later targeted cases and fixes were useful. Quota’s self-accusation of treating a scheduled attempt as a completed reset conflicts with its repeated earlier caveats. More caveats would not repair its missing route analysis. Fix’s literal confession of missing a 300-second deadline is not supported by the recorded 292-second interval, although its stale timing promise and unsuitable proposal remain problems. C source L1394; Q L1041 versus L197/L535/L721/L945/L1029; F L693/L706/L777

The quota fallback illustrates the required specificity. Its Claude route invoked the same redeem.py --redeem, credit and idempotency key as the primary. It could recover some transient primary-process failures, but did not bypass a shared script/client defect and introduced another authentication/model dependency. Verified login and guarded timer startup did not exercise a Claude model turn or redemption. This establishes the installed dependency structure, not that all fallback designs are useless or that this one must fail. Q L748; source L1294, L1470

The stop-review experiments likewise do not establish a universal reliability verdict. Three wrapper replays changed conditions: one failed before reading evidence, one received 25 selected messages, and one received all 71 visible messages but no explicitly passed parent instruction/tool/task stack. The fresh reviewer’s full effective context was uncaptured. Its wrong verdict cannot be attributed solely to missing context without a matched enriched comparison. The semantic runs took roughly 39–46 seconds; correct judgment and tolerable latency would both matter. No live hook was activated. R source L906/L936/L1029/L1035/L1060–1082; L1465–1505/L1653

The accepted messaging skill is a useful positive boundary. An initial general rule confused root replies with child finals; structural checks missed the scope error. Conditional routing and fresh probes exposed further receipt clutter before a revision handled one synthetic case and Jörn accepted it. That preserves a recovered local tool while limiting the behavioral claim. Discovery, suitable scope and one passing example are different from general reliability. R L590–614, L745–806

Broader model, context and speed explanations remain less established. All eight roots and the compared children requested gpt-6.1-sol at high effort; this is not a model comparison or proof of equal inputs, tools or task difficulty. Narrow children and the same roots produced useful work. The concrete allocation and continuation findings are stronger than a blanket claim of model incapacity or context overload.

Costs, validation and the remaining uncertainty

No accepted substantial programme was launched in the reviewed window. Useful preparation did occur, and its presence shows that lack of large-package approval did not prevent all progress. The record cannot determine which alternative portfolio would have passed, exactly how much work would otherwise have moved into the window, or a monetary loss. Jörn’s financial concern is his stated concern, not a measured charge. Future reset success remains unknown.

Recorded resources show concentration in roots: among 1,812 completed-response records from eight roots and 29 descendants, roots produced about 83% of output tokens and accounted for about 78% of input processing. Input repeatedly counts context, mostly cached; output includes reasoning and tool calls. Independent subprocess calls are a known gap. These quantities describe allocation, not an optimal ratio, a bill, user-attention minutes or proof of waste. Recorded aggregates and method. They make root concentration worth examining, while the event traces identify the actual work that remained serial.

The postmortem’s first version received factual, causal and fresh-reader checks. Those established useful bounded properties, including that a new reader could retrieve the intended distinctions without private transcripts. They did not prove that the available investigation had exhausted material causal questions.

Two separately frozen consumption tests constrain claims about this report’s value. The six-case generic transfer study found no robust incremental decision benefit from adding the earlier 2,133-word knowledge note to current guidance. Twelve fresh Astra/xhigh agents answered nearby hypothetical cases; two blinded scorers assessed them. Five pairs tied under both scorers. The remaining small difference depended on one scorer’s debatable custody judgment; the other scorer tied that pair too. All twelve answers were adequate under the study rubric. Treatment added 2,137 words per participant including its wrapper. Rich supplied facts produced a near-ceiling baseline. This tested generic decision support, not reconstruction of these incidents or sustained historical-Sol behavior.

The distinct four-task archival study addressed historical reuse more directly. Eight fresh Astra/xhigh consumers reconstructed actual assignments, results, authority and limitations from a bounded archive; one consumer in each pair could also consult the frozen 00:34 v2 report packet. It demonstrated no net reconstruction-cost saving. All eight answers were adequate. Packet access yielded some more explicit qualifications: across three pairs, mean scorer gains were only 0.25–0.5 on the 18-point rubric. Adequacy and the broad next-action recommendations did not change. Packet consumers read 75,940 versus 70,786 charged source words (+7.3%), made 83 versus 63 reads, and took longer in every pair, by 2.4–23.4 seconds. One consumer per condition and imperfect blinding preclude a stable effect estimate.

The reviewer-history pair’s larger score difference had unequal information. The packet’s output map exposed a separate pre-freeze Fix fresh/fork probe that the relevant baseline archive omitted. The baseline answer overgeneralized from its own available reviewer history; the packet answer preserved the additional probe. That difference receives no credit as an equal-information reasoning improvement. Locked scores remain alongside this adjudication.
