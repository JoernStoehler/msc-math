## Coverage: a proved uncertainty certificate

A continuous set of geometry-selected capacity candidates gives an upper bound $U$ and a coverage number $\eta$, with

$$\eta U\le c_{\rm EHZ}\le U.$$

This holds for polygon products generally and is exact at HKO. It is a small optimization procedure, not a fitted scalar formula or a claimed faster capacity algorithm. Six existing near-HKO cases have much tighter lower certificates than the earlier conditional relaxation; their true capacities were already known.

Adding a tiny facet can make the coverage number nearly zero even when capacity is unchanged. A geometric pruning/containment step repairs the same tested case: the certified capacity interval narrows from a factor 84.53 to **1.01806**, namely approximately **[4.88213,4.97029]**. This is a proved single-path remedy; hard random coverage remains weak.

## A precise global-bound crux survives a finite test

The combined width/chord-star ceiling stays at or below the HKO ratio on 14 retained random products whose exact width ceilings exceed HKO. On 60 constructed perturbations, width alone already suffices; the standalone star ceiling also passes all 60. An independent checker verifies one exact eligible star witness per body. These are previously exposed geometries, without a new capacity oracle or a global theorem.

The [completed analytic facet-refinement test](facet-refinement/REPORT.md) now disproves the stronger standalone star inequality. Simultaneous tiny cuts at all five vertices of both HKO factors make every star chord collapse toward the cone-volume center. Its ratio ceiling tends to $(3+\sqrt5)/4=1.25\rho_H$, while the true ratio tends to $\rho_H$. At the frozen depth $1/1000$, exact independent reconstruction gives star ceiling1.30927194 and width ceiling1.04490602; the actual ratio is at least1.04303705 by containment, without a capacity oracle. This is a failure of sharpness, not a violation of a valid capacity upper bound.

The [independent width lemma](facet-refinement/WIDTH-PROTECTION.md) proves that width stays strictly below HKO throughout the genuine ten-facet interval of this uniform-cut family. Therefore the combined minimum survives the whole family. The missing implication remains absolute: whenever widths allow a ratio above HKO, must a star response reach the volume-normalized HKO payoff? Relative coverage does not prove this. The combined proposal is **parked** without a proof mechanism or counterexample; no new cohort or automatic continuation is selected. Broad held-out prediction and global maximality remain open.
