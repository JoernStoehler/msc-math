What did the orchestration buy?
Where could it have done better?

Checked results were obtained through parallel work and staged reviews. The schedule also contains a dispatch error, a delayed independent continuation, and a final period of monitoring without selected science. We have evidence for those events; we do not have a measured optimal alternative.

Read the mathematical-outcomes report first. It tells us what the labor actually produced. This report then asks whether the organization of that labor was worthwhile. It does not repeat the theorem statements.

3. AllocationWas there a better use of labor?

A ten-minute thread estimated to produce $30 of value can be a sensible allocation. What it thinks about matters only insofar as it changes that estimate, the actions needed, dependencies, or the alternatives. Neither “ten minutes” nor “$30” is a measured value for a task in this night.

Before execution: compare expected value, including uncertainty and what a small probe could teach us. After execution: inspect actual outcomes, costs and avoidable delays. A successful outcome alone does not prove its allocation was best; a failed route can still be valuable learning.

What the work bought

Confidence in an existing local proof; broader restricted global bounds; new restricted perturbation results; a concrete unsolved reduction; and elimination of some proof routes.

What we still cannot price

Their dollar value to Jörn, calibrated chances of finishing the broad questions, task-level subscription cost, and the added value an alternative schedule would have produced.

Three concrete allocation findings

~9 minutes: a selected task did not start

The endpoint task was selected around 01:52, but a message was queued to an idle owner without starting a new turn. The actual follow-up began around 02:01. Other science continued, so this was a lane delay rather than whole-program idle.

Supported improvement: verify an actual start after dispatch, with an explicit owner and first action. No daemon outage was established.

37 minutes: prepared work stayed unselected

The P* continuation had a proposal by 02:25; its lead was observed finished at 02:29. The next science began at 03:06. The global lane was prioritized. No mathematical prerequisite requiring its completion, quota refusal or missing P* owner was found.

Counterfactual worth assessing: run the small P* census beside the global work. Shared decision/review burden was not measured. Earlier execution might have helped, but neither improved total value nor full research completion is demonstrated.

28 min 25 s: retention was complete, but monitoring continued

By 04:28 all scientific and source assignments were reported complete. Only root and supervision recorded usage afterward. Ordinary quota use was still allowed. The root had parked larger continuations as unpriced.

Unresolved choice: was the best marginal use another bounded discriminator, earlier release, or the remaining cutoff supervision? “Unpriced” identifies uncertainty; it does not by itself justify leaving the interval without science. Exact compliance with the original authorization is a separate investigation.

Rounded account-wide primary quota rose from 1% used to 8% used. That is not seven percentage points attributed exactly to this tree. Per-task quota shares and actual dollar spending are unknown. No quota refusal was observed at the recorded reads.

Root, comparison and supervision account for 32.68% of the unweighted noncached-input-plus-output total. That is a token-volume diagnostic, not a measured overhead percentage. Nothing here establishes how much wall time was spent thinking, reading, editing or waiting.

What we can reasonably conclude

The organization delivered checked restricted results and used parallel work. A dispatch mistake is established, and two allocation intervals deserve criticism and comparison. The evidence does not establish an optimal schedule, the value per dollar, or how much further a better programme would have got.

A useful next assessment would attach Jörn's value judgments to the outcomes, then compare concrete alternatives using expected returns, dependencies and uncertainty. We should not fill the missing estimates with invented scores or infer efficiency from the number of files produced.
