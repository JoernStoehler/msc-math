Viterbo's conjecture predicted that the normalized
Ekeland--Hofer--Zehnder capacity of a four-dimensional convex body satisfies
\(\operatorname{sys}(K)\leq 1\).  The Lagrangian product of two suitably
rotated regular pentagons found by Haim--Kislev and Ostrover disproves this
bound.  This thesis asks whether that body can be improved locally and whether
finite computation reveals further examples.

For polytopes, the investigation combines the Haim--Kislev finite quadratic
program for the capacity with first-order variation in normalized facet
coordinates and exact algebraic certificates.  The main theorem shows that
the HKO body is a Hausdorff-local maximizer among polytopes with exactly ten
facets.  In a sufficiently small neighborhood, equality occurs precisely
under translations, positive scalings, and linear symplectic transformations.
The proof uses \(26\) feasible capacity upper functions whose exact derivative
certificate forces decrease in all \(25\) transverse directions.

For the one-parameter family
\(K_\theta=P_5\times_L R(\theta)P_5\), the same finite machinery gives the
exact profile
\[
  \operatorname{sys}(K_\theta)
  =\frac{5+2\sqrt5}{10}\sec^2 d(\theta),
\]
where \(d(\theta)\) is the distance to the nearest multiple of \(\pi/5\).
Its maximum is exactly the HKO value \((3+\sqrt5)/5\).  A complementary
rational flow-graph algorithm is proved correct under explicit regularity
hypotheses.

The computational search gives a bounded negative result: no recorded value
exceeds \(1\) in a retained sample of \(14336\) random polytopes and random
Lagrangian products, nor among the \(1675\) selected-or-baseline rows evaluated
after a frozen screen of \(100000\) generated random products.  These finite
outcomes do not imply nonexistence.  Together, the results establish local
rigidity and an exact structured-family profile while keeping theorem,
computation, and empirical evidence distinct.
