# Quantitative Plots

## Reader Task And Comparison Contract

State what the reader should compare or assess: magnitude, ordering, trend,
variation, agreement, error, distribution, threshold behavior, or another
task-local relation. Choose axes, transforms, aggregation, normalization, and
panel scales so they support that comparison. Disclose choices that remove or
distort another likely comparison.

Use shared scales when direct magnitude comparison matters. Use independent
scales only when local structure matters more, and make the lost comparison
clear. Avoid visual precision beyond the numerical, experimental, or sampling
precision of the underlying result.

## Evidence And Uncertainty

Distinguish a representative example, diagnostic, sanity check, empirical
pattern, and evidence for a thesis claim. Show uncertainty or variation when
the claimed conclusion depends on it. Do not let selection, filtering,
aggregation, smoothing, or omitted failures silently strengthen the apparent
claim.

Make reference values, thresholds, extrapolations, and fitted quantities
visually and textually distinguishable from direct observations. A passing
producer invariant establishes only what it checks.

## Encoding

Prefer encodings that make the required comparison direct. Use labels, legends,
color, marker shape, line style, and ordering consistently. Check whether
essential distinctions survive the final thesis scale and likely print
conditions. Remove decoration and redundant precision when they compete with
the comparison the reader needs.
