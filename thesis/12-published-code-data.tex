\section{Availability of Code and Data}\label{sec:published-code-data}

Several results in this thesis depend on computation, but not all in the same
way.  For a future researcher, the important question is therefore not how the
project repository is organized.  It is which mathematical evidence remains
available after completion of the thesis, what can be checked or developed
from it, and which parts of the research process are not preserved.  The
published material is intended to support three uses: auditing the exact finite
calculations, re-examining the empirical observations, and continuing the
computational investigation.

For the theorem-bearing calculations, the release contains the complete
SageMath verifiers and their recorded outputs for the HKO local-maximum result
and the rotated-pentagon formula.  The HKO material also contains the finite
witness input.  A retained Rust program produces these finite choices, while
the SageMath verifier reconstructs the proof-facing algebraic objects and
checks the exact certificate.  The theorem therefore does not require trusting
the search procedure that found those choices.  For the rotated-pentagon
result, the retained SageMath program performs the exact finite classification
left after the mathematical reduction in
Section~\ref{sec:rotated-regular-polygons}.  These programs and their recorded
outputs do not replace the arguments in the thesis: the mathematical meaning
of the checked predicates and their implication for the theorems are given in
the corresponding result sections.  They make the finite parts of those
arguments available for direct re-execution and inspection.

The empirical material is retained for a different reason.  The release
contains the random/product data underlying the bounded data-science search,
together with row provenance and the derived feature table.  It also contains
the selected and evaluated rows from the frozen generated-candidate
experiment.  It also contains the frozen \(64\)-start local-optimizer
comparison, its per-evaluation traces, and the endpoint and continuation
diagnostics used to delimit the finite-budget conclusion.  The retained
predictor replay, HKO perturbation control, and directional KKT audit preserve
the evidence used to distinguish missing branches, affine-model error, and
remaining endpoint ascent.  Keeping these data
allows another researcher to inspect individual examples, repeat the reported
analyses, compare alternative methods on the same inputs, or use the samples
as a starting point for a broader search without first repeating the expensive
production runs.

Availability does not strengthen the conclusions drawn from these data.  The
random/product table remains a finite sample from the distributions specified
in Section~\ref{sec:black-box-datascience}; it does not represent all random
polytopes or all Lagrangian products.  The generated-candidate records support
the stated experiment for one generator and frozen rule set, not a general
candidate-selection theorem.  The optimizer comparison ranks frozen
implementations only at the stated ten-facet input distribution and compute
budget; its continuation traces show that its endpoints are not
local-maximality evidence.  Where a project-owned thesis figure
comes from an experiment, its source data and producer are retained so that the
figure can be inspected or regenerated.  This does not make an exploratory
figure part of a proof.

The release also preserves the Rust libraries, experiment producers and
analyzers, thesis sources, formal notes, documentation, and environment
definitions.  These materials go beyond checking the displayed
results: they allow later work to change an analysis, extend a sample, test a
new search method, or rebuild the thesis from its sources.  GitHub is the
living continuation repository and may change after submission:
\begin{center}
  \url{https://github.com/JoernStoehler/msc-math}.
\end{center}
Its frozen counterpart is a single Zenodo archive containing the reviewed
release state together with the checked thesis PDF.  Large data stored through
Git LFS appear in that archive as ordinary hydrated files, so access to the
frozen data does not depend on retrieving separate LFS objects.

The archive is deliberately selective rather than a complete record of every
computation or of the research process.  It excludes private material, raw
agent sessions, disposable outputs, and third-party material that cannot be
redistributed.  Some large intermediate caches are also omitted when they have
no independent interpretive value and can be regenerated from the retained
source and inputs.  Project software is made available under Apache-2.0, while
Jörn-owned thesis and research text, original figures, documentation, and
project-generated data are made available under CC BY~4.0.  These licenses do
not relicense third-party material.

Finally, availability is weaker than a promise of identical execution in every
future environment.  Exact verifier reruns are compared through the predicates
they check.  Retained datasets have explicit identities, whereas empirical
outputs containing timings or environment-dependent fields need not reproduce
byte for byte.  A small smoke run can show that a computational route still
connects, but it cannot replace either a full retained dataset or an exact
certificate.  Detailed access, build, and rerun instructions are kept with the
repository and the experiment that owns each result rather than repeated in
the thesis.
