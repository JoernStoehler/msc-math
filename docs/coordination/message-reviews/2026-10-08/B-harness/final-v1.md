The skills contain substantial relevant guidance, but we do not yet have a dependable harness. The strongest demonstrated problem is **failing to turn known obligations and useful findings into the right next decision**. That leaves you doing coordination and quality control the agents should have handled.

Four failures matter here:

- **Continuation:** agents remembered unfinished work and still stopped after a component. Another reminder would not, by itself, supply or execute the next useful action.
- **Integration:** specialist findings lost consequential conditions during synthesis. Fresh reviewers sometimes shared the same disputed assumption, so another agent’s agreement did not resolve it.
- **Questions:** agents asked you to reconstruct available facts or judge inadequately prepared objects. Sometimes useful material arrived after your review window. Both preparation and timing failed.
- **Parallelism:** agents retained a staffing/integration arrangement without adequately comparing alternatives as the work changed. That establishes a missing comparison; it does not prove that more agents would have been faster.

Those distinctions are supported by the [retained cases](https://joern-pc.tailc5e761.ts.net/files/workspaces/msc-math/docs/history/session-postmortem-2026-10-04/REPORT.md). They do not establish an internal cause such as reinforced habits, indifference or bad instruction placement. Some cases predate the revised skills; the [later synthetic success followed by live recurrence](https://joern-pc.tailc5e761.ts.net/files/workspaces/msc-math/docs/history/skill-presweep-20261006/behavior-dispositions.md) also prevents calling the behavior repaired.

The accepted message review is a concrete intervention: it can challenge a poorly framed question, lost caveat or premature completion before delivery. Its limits are consequential: review can come too late, share the sender’s assumptions, or improve the message without improving subsequent execution.

**My recommendation is to keep the current guidance and examine the accepted review through an actual decision and its resulting action before adding another general mechanism.** Assess whether it saves an intervention from you, preserves useful progress and costs tolerable walltime—not merely whether the reviewer approves. A specific failure could then justify a targeted source repair, a decision-level independent comparison or a verified runtime fix. A broad rewrite or another universal checkpoint has no established advantage yet.
