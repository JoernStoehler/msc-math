The decision is whether an overnight investment can remove enough research and writing friction to enable the human thesis within seven days—not whether agents can reorganize files and pass checks.

1. Decision and constraints D1

Proposed next step: use the design hour to test a curated agent-facing account against alternatives and prepare a cold-start execution package. This draft does not establish that the package will work.

The overnight product should explain the research coherently, with progressive disclosure and links both to and from code, datasets, figures and notes. A good file tree could supply that function; a linear account is the leading candidate, not a predetermined format.

The downstream constraint is binding. Jörn permits substantial authoring, but estimates writing everything himself at four weeks. Therefore “Jörn writes it all” is not a fallback meeting seven days. The future envelope is approximately 28 human hours and $10k API-equivalent capacity; it is not current spending authorization or a measured balance.

2. Decompose the outcome before choosing tools D2

A. Research understandingFind results, reconstruct arguments, distinguish extensions and limits, reach supporting artifacts.
B. Scientific scope and supportDecide what belongs; expose real proof, evidence or reproduction gaps; close or honestly delimit them.
C. Human expositionChoose a readable order, explain objects and reasoning, draft, revise and obtain useful human judgments.
AND: A + B + C, sustained by reliable agent operation ↓
D. Integrated acceptance
A complete readable manuscript whose mathematics, evidence, figures, references and promises agree.

Cleanup acts directly on A. It can reveal B and reduce C’s source-reconstruction burden. It cannot by itself resolve scientific choices, teach effective exposition, predict human acceptance or establish human acceptance. Reliable operation—inputs, tool knowledge, decisions, integration and recovery—is needed throughout.

The causal hypothesis is: better source access and explanations → less rediscovery and fewer wrong claims → more human time available for composition and acceptance. Each arrow can fail. We must measure downstream work saved, including maintaining the new account.

5. Use standard workflow families for different risks D5

  1. Incremental refactoring: inventory dependencies, preserve behavior with focused regression checks, change small units, integrate and retain rollback points. Add consumer tasks because preserved behavior does not establish improved usability.
  2. Evidence synthesis: define coverage, extract from authoritative sources, retain provenance, reconcile contradictions, then synthesize. Do not promote a summary above its source.
  3. Editorial production: agree purpose and structure, explain before polishing, draft a bounded passage, obtain reader feedback, revise and inspect the assembled document.
  4. Experimental validation: establish a baseline, test competing interventions, challenge the evaluator with both failures and valid outputs, record uncertainty.
  5. Ongoing execution: cold-start input check, bounded ownership, checkpoints, integration, explicit pending decisions and recovery. Verify actual tool outcomes and refresh operational knowledge when uncertain.

These are established workflow patterns, not evidence that this implementation will succeed. Human-team practices need adaptation for fresh contexts, correlated model errors, cheap parallel generation and limited human review capacity.

6. What evidence exists, and what changes the decision? D6

Observed baseline: one fresh Sol 6.1 medium reader recovered the pentagon analytic proof and several extensions across roughly sixteen files, but missed the existing centrally symmetric partner branch. That is a material omission for avoiding repeated research, not proof of general model incapacity.

Adverse evidence: Jörn reports over thirty failed workflow attempts. Retained trials already tried feedback, rewriting and separate reviews without establishing human PASS. Another small study found extra packets slowed every pair without improving adequacy. “Add a summary and reviewer” starts unproven.

Pre-mortem: anticipated failures and their disposition
  • Wrong goal passes local checks → require downstream consumer tasks and integrated acceptance.
  • Summary omits or strengthens a result → source-linked coverage and independent reconstruction; retain uncertainty.
  • All reviewers share the same blind spot → challenge cases and a targeted human judgment; approval counts are insufficient.
  • Missing input is discovered overnight → inventory access beforehand; separate absent links, missing data and human-only decisions.
  • File changes damage evidence → inspect producer side effects; worktrees do not isolate shared caches.
  • Work expands without completion → explicit selected scope, integration checkpoints and stopping rules.
  • Seven-day plan quietly depends on excessive human repair → count explanation, review, correction and authoring together.
  • Tool behavior or a lost question stalls work → verify outcomes, retain uniquely numbered pending questions and expose them in handover.
