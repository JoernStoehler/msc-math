\subsection{Numerical Precision of the Admissibility Check}
\label{sec:unknown-predicates}

The capacity algorithm uses a three-valued admissibility predicate for each
candidate orbit $(S, \sigma)$: the KKT solution $\beta$ is classified as
\emph{certified} ($\beta_i > \varepsilon$ for all~$i$),
\emph{rejected} ($\beta_i < -\varepsilon$ for some~$i$), or
\emph{uncertain} ($-\varepsilon < \beta_{\min} \le \varepsilon$),
where $\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$.
The certified capacity uses only certified orbits; the uncertain capacity
additionally admits uncertain orbits.
A gap between the two (numerical gap $> 0$) would indicate that an
uncertain orbit achieves lower action than any certified orbit, meaning
the algorithm's output depends on floating-point precision.

\paragraph{Experiment.}
We re-ran both the random-sweep dataset (70~polytopes, $F = 5, \ldots, 12$)
and the Lagrangian-products dataset (92~polytopes: pentagon $5 \times 5$ sweep
and 10 regular polygon pairs) through the production algorithms
(\texttt{ehz\_capacity} and \texttt{billiard\_capacity} respectively),
recording the certified capacity, uncertain capacity, numerical gap,
and minimum $\beta$ component for each polytope.

\paragraph{Results.}
Across all 162~polytopes, the numerical gap was exactly zero:
no uncertain orbit achieved lower action than the certified optimum.
Phase~2 (high-precision re-solve) was therefore unnecessary.

The minimum $\beta$ component $\beta_{\min}$ of the certified orbit measures
the margin from the uncertain threshold.
For random polytopes, $\beta_{\min}$ ranged from $6.7 \times 10^{-4}$
to $1.2 \times 10^{-1}$ (median $4.5 \times 10^{-2}$), well above the
threshold $\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$.
For Lagrangian products, $\beta_{\min}$ ranged from $6.2 \times 10^{-12}$
to $3.5 \times 10^{-1}$ (median $1.7 \times 10^{-1}$).
The smallest value $\beta_{\min} = 6.2 \times 10^{-12}$ occurs for the
square$\,\times\,$square product $(4,4)$ at $\theta = 18^\circ$, just
$6\times$ above the threshold.
This near-miss reflects the geometric near-degeneracy of Lagrangian products
at special rotation angles, where one dwell-time component approaches zero
as the orbit becomes tangent to a facet.

\paragraph{Conclusion.}
The algorithm is empirically exact at \texttt{f64} precision on our datasets:
no UNKNOWN predicates influenced the capacity computation.
The 8-order-of-magnitude gap between the closest Lagrangian-product margin
($6.2 \times 10^{-12}$) and the closest random-polytope margin
($6.7 \times 10^{-4}$) suggests that numerical precision concerns are
concentrated in structured polytopes with geometric near-degeneracies,
not in generic polytopes.
