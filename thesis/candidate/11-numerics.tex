\section{Numerics}\label{sec:numerics}

The computations in this thesis support two different kinds of claims.
Statements used inside the HKO and rotated-pentagon proofs are checked by
separate SageMath packets over rational or ordered algebraic fields.  The
ordinary Rust implementation instead computes the EHZ capacity of a
four-dimensional polytope supplied in binary64 coordinates.  It uses
floating-point arithmetic for speed, but it does not accept an unverified
floating-point sign or ordering decision: every such decision is enclosed by
an outward error bound or resolved in exact rational arithmetic.

\subsection{Input and output contracts}

A finite binary64 number is a dyadic rational.  The numerical capacity
contract regards the supplied numbers as these exact rationals; it does not
claim to recover unavailable source coordinates from which they may have been
rounded.  Before the QP search, exact geometry checks establish that
\[
  K=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^4:\langle a_i,x\rangle\leq1\}
\]
is bounded, nonempty, full-dimensional, and described without redundant
facets.  The exact primal vertices and vertex--facet incidence are then
constructed from the dyadic input.  Floating-point filters may discard a
subset only when a one-sided bound proves the exact conclusion; an
indeterminate filter result is resolved exactly.

The production route accepts at most sixteen facets and requires every primal
and dual vertex to have infinity norm in
\[
  [10^{-3},10^3].
\]
These bounds are a deliberately narrow applicability policy, not a theorem
about all polytopes.  They exclude scales for which the current binary64
enclosures have not been justified and tested.  The general candidate stream
is additionally limited to \(100\,000\) cyclic words.  Violating any of these
conditions returns an error rather than an uncertified capacity.

For a general polytope the result is an outward interval
\([c_{\mathrm L},c_{\mathrm U}]\) containing the exact capacity of the
dyadic input.  A scalar wrapper returns the midpoint only if this interval
certifies the relative error requested by the caller.  For a recognized
Lagrangian product the specialized route returns the exact rational capacity
of the dyadic input, together with outward binary64 endpoints for convenient
downstream use.

\subsection{Certified fixed-word decisions}

For a word \(\sigma\), write the equality-constrained stationary system in the
symmetric form
\[
  \mathcal K
  \binom{\beta}{\lambda}
  =
  \begin{pmatrix}H&C^{\mathsf T}\\ C&0\end{pmatrix}
  \binom{\beta}{\lambda}
  =
  \binom{0}{d},
  \qquad d=(0,0,0,0,1)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Here \(C\beta=d\) is closure plus normalization and \(H\) is twice the
quadratic form in \eqref{eq:quadratic-program-algorithm-hk2019-q}.  A numerical
factorization supplies an approximate solution \(\widehat x\) and approximate
inverse \(R\).  The following standard inverse-defect estimate is the central
certificate.

\begin{lemma}[Verified inverse defect]\label{lem:numerics-verified-inverse-defect}
Suppose outward arithmetic establishes
\[
  \overline r\geq\|\mathcal K\widehat x-b\|_\infty,\qquad
  \overline e\geq\|I-\mathcal K R\|_\infty,\qquad
  \overline e<1.
\]
Then \(\mathcal K\) is invertible, and its exact solution \(x^\ast\) satisfies
\[
  \|\widehat x-x^\ast\|_\infty
  \leq
  \rho:=
  \frac{\|R\|_\infty\,\overline r}{1-\overline e}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Banach lemma applied to
\(\mathcal K R=I-(I-\mathcal K R)\) gives
\(\|\mathcal K^{-1}\|_\infty\leq
\|R\|_\infty/(1-\overline e)\).  Apply this bound to the residual
\(\mathcal K\widehat x-b\).
\end{proof}

Thus \(\widehat\beta_i-\rho>0\) for every \(i\) certifies positive weights,
while \(\widehat\beta_i+\rho\leq0\) for some \(i\) certifies failure.  If
\(\xi\) is the multiplier for normalization, stationarity gives
\[
  2Q(\beta^\ast)+\xi^\ast=0.
\]
Consequently the same radius yields
\[
  Q(\beta^\ast)\in
  \left[-\frac{\widehat\xi+\rho}{2},
        -\frac{\widehat\xi-\rho}{2}\right].
\]
For \(0<q_{\mathrm L}\leq Q\leq q_{\mathrm U}\), monotonicity then gives the
action enclosure
\[
  \frac{1}{2q_{\mathrm U}}
  \leq\mathcal A\leq
  \frac{1}{2q_{\mathrm L}}.
\]

The implementation computes the residual and inverse-defect bounds with
explicit roundoff and subnormal-error terms.  It first uses a cheap induced
norm bound and retries an indeterminate system with a tighter entrywise
bound.  The proof assumes round-to-nearest binary64 arithmetic with gradual
underflow and the inspected matrix-multiplication evaluation order.  The
runtime checks gradual underflow; if the contract is unavailable, or if either
bound remains indeterminate, the decision is made by exact rational
arithmetic.  A compiler, dependency, target, or arithmetic-contract change
therefore requires the floating-point certificate tests to be repeated.

\subsection{Complete general and product routes}

The general search constructs the exact transition graph, pruned by facet
incidence and symplectic signs, then enumerates its simple cyclic words. Candidate
correctness is not obtained merely by solving every displayed KKT system.  A
fixed-word maximum must have nonpositive curvature in every tangent direction.
Conversely, a certified vector \(z\in\ker C_\tau\) with
\(z^{\mathsf T}H_\tau z>0\) rules out \(\tau\).  If \(\tau\) injects into a
longer word \(\sigma\) while preserving cyclic order, zero-extending \(z\)
preserves both its tangent constraints and positive curvature.  Hence
\(\sigma\) is ruled out as well.  Processing words by length allows these
proved obstructions to skip many later factorizations.

For every word not excluded in this way, the route uses the verified
fixed-word decision above, with exact fallback for every indeterminate case.
The resulting objective intervals are aggregated outwards over the complete
candidate family, and the reciprocal reverses the endpoints to enclose the
capacity.  Exact arithmetic is therefore a selective correctness fallback,
not the default arithmetic for every word.

For a Lagrangian product, the inverse-defect route is correct but frequently
indeterminate and unnecessarily expensive.  The specialized implementation
uses Algorithm~\ref{alg:quadratic-program-product-closure-vertices}.  It
encloses factor-closure weights and bilinear objectives in floating-point
arithmetic, rejects a support or order only when the intervals prove it cannot
win, and resolves ambiguous supports and every possible winner exactly.
Because the final comparison is rational, this route returns the exact
capacity and every tied sparse winner in the closure-vertex candidate family.
It intentionally does not enumerate all minimizing geometric branches.

\subsection{Empirical verification and limitations}

The general numerical audit compared the floating-point predicates with exact
dyadic-rational arithmetic on \(249\) KKT systems: ordinary generated systems,
scales from \(10^{-2}\) through \(10^3\), and systems approaching singularity
down to binary64 coincidence.  It found no incorrect determinate decision and
no violation of a \(\beta\)- or \(Q\)-radius.  Fifteen cases were
indeterminate and were resolved exactly.  The largest observed ratios of
actual error to certified radius were \(0.00149\) for \(\beta\) and
\(0.00081\) for \(Q\).

Complete-route comparisons used \(88\) words from three generated general
polytopes and \(1271\) words from three product controls.  Exact aggregation
found no missed capacity and no missed minimizer class; every exact capacity
lay in the returned interval.  On the general cases, the largest difference
between the reported midpoint and exact capacity was \(2.31\cdot10^{-14}\).
These finite comparisons test the implementation and exercise fallback, but
the soundness claim rests on the preceding error bounds and complete-candidate
arguments.

The product audit used \(88\) ordinary and adversarial products.  Across
\(1835\) closure weights and \(173\,496\) objective intervals it found no
interval violation, no incorrect determinate sign, and no disagreement between
the hybrid and complete exact closure-vertex computations.  Raw comparison
with zero disagreed with the exact objective sign \(23\,559\) times, mostly at
exact zero or tiny values; the certified predicate returned indeterminate
instead.  A separate retained population of \(10\,240\) random products had a
literal capacity winner of length six in every case.  This supports the
implementation and stress-tests
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-program-product-six-facet}; it is not its proof and
does not exclude longer tied minimizers.

The guarantees above concern the exact dyadic polytope represented by the
input.  They do not propagate measurement error or prior rounding uncertainty
in the coordinates, and they apply only within the stated facet, norm, and
candidate-count limits.  Proof-facing claims about algebraic parameter
families therefore continue to use the separate exact SageMath certificates.
