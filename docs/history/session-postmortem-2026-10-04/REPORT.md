# Postmortem: the MSc/Codex work window of 3–4 October 2026

The main failure was that substantial activity did not become a dependable service to Jörn: select worthwhile work, obtain the human input it actually needed while he was available, carry it through, and leave results whose status was clear. Useful tools, mathematical candidates and planning artifacts were produced. Yet several roots repeatedly required Jörn to restore the objective, supply missing comparison criteria, diagnose their questions, and restart unfinished work. The attempted repair session reproduced those failures and did not establish an approved substantial unattended programme before his availability was exhausted.

This was not one uniform failure across all eight roots. The quota outcome was still in the future; the prior error-list audit appears to have delivered its narrower requested output; and several technical components worked. Nor does the evidence establish one internal model defect. The strongest explanations concern observable transitions: what counted as a completed task, what evidence was treated as sufficient for a decision, how human attention entered the work schedule, and whether supervision could influence the work it observed.

The purpose of doing this reconstruction now is to avoid making Jörn pay for it repeatedly later. Future agents need the explanation and its limits, not just a rule saying “do better” or a catalogue requiring him to interpret the same history again. This report therefore retains why apparently plausible repairs were insufficient, which evidence would discriminate between remaining explanations, and what should be preserved from the work.

The frozen observation window is **18:24:26–23:24:26 UTC on 3 October**, or **20:24:26–01:24:26 Berlin time**. Earlier messages establish the original assignments. Household power and host backups are excluded. Events after the freeze are not used to upgrade or downgrade these outcomes. In particular, quota redemption at **04:00 Berlin / 02:00 UTC** falls after both the freeze and this report's deadline: its result remains pending. ## The event chain and its mechanisms

### 1. Completing the latest exchange repeatedly displaced completing the assignment

This is the clearest recurring mechanism. It is visible without believing agents' explanations of their own motives.

In configuration, the agent replied “You're welcome” at 18:42:31 after a local config exchange, ending work on the broader walkthrough. After Jörn challenged this, it retrieved and correctly summarized the already-existing continuity rules. Later it again ended with “assessment is complete; workflow readiness remains unresolved,” and subsequently with ongoing awareness still unresolved. Each time, Jörn had to cause the next step. The later useful repairs show that independent work was actually available. [C L309–342, 18:42–18:44; L1458–1478, 21:10–21:13; L1618–1637, 21:23–21:25](report.html#evidence-c)

Recurring-data provides an unusually strong repeat. The root had explicitly formulated a test for an unjustified turn ending: what changes next, who acts, and whether the necessary action or request has been issued. Nevertheless, when Jörn accepted a messaging skill, it answered only “You're welcome!” and ended. The accepted side repair had become the endpoint of the original information-design assignment. Later it said Jörn had already provided enough direction to continue, then ended anyway. [R L193, 18:39:15; L806–823, 20:18:50–20:19:38; L1220–1227, 20:55–20:56](report.html#evidence-r)

Task design shows the same transition without courtesy language. After recognizing that its recommended task lacked an adequate value case, the agent withdrew the recommendation and ended with a statement of what it owed Jörn. It did not perform that work until he asked why it had stopped. A correct diagnosis became another substitute for continuing the assignment. [T L265–275, 20:50:09–20:50:36](report.html#evidence-t)

The causal consequence is straightforward: these endings made a renewed human message the practical trigger for progress, although no new human fact or authorization was needed for the next bounded step. They converted Jörn from reviewer into scheduler. This is different from a justified stop at an actual approval boundary, a genuine user stop, or completion of a selected subtask. The recurring-data root's final park followed an explicit resource stop; configuration's accounting subtask had real completion evidence; those should not be relabeled as the same error.

The evidence establishes the behavior, not why the model internally chose it. Missing knowledge is an inadequate general explanation: the relevant knowledge was sometimes read, restated and then immediately unused. A universal extra paragraph about persistence is consequently a weak first remedy.

### 2. Locally demonstrable progress was used as evidence for a larger conclusion

Across several roots, an intermediate property was easier to establish than the property the user needed. The work then advanced as though the intermediate property settled the larger question.

For task selection, “can run unattended” was repeatedly offered as a reason to run something now. Existing evaluators and measured paths made a saturation investigation feasible; they did not establish its value relative to writing, proof simplification, other research, or the review burden left after the quota window. The user explicitly drew that distinction. More concrete decomposition did not fix it: the priorities root presented eight production assignments, then replaced that long dispatch list with a broad permission question. Neither version supplied the missing case for expected net value. [T L218–231, 20:18–20:48; P L414–442, 20:51–21:00](report.html#evidence-t)

The correction sequence is revealing. A broad writing plan became detailed assignments after criticism of vagueness; criticism of review burden turned that into a shorter permission request; criticism of value led to smaller mathematical questions; one lacked enough context, and another asked Jörn about writing history already available in project facts. The latest objection changed the next surface without the earlier requirements reliably surviving. This is an observable failure to accumulate constraints, not a claim about the model's private motives. It explains why repeated apparently responsive corrections did not converge. [P L366, L414, L428, L435, L534–553; source L173–176, 20:14:50](report.html#evidence-p)

For accounting, passing tests was offered before essential semantics were checked. The configuration root said existing scripts were enough to start. After Jörn asked about forks, targeted inspection found copied counters counted twice without warning, missing input recorded as zero, and fork lineage confused with worker lineage—while all twenty existing tests still passed. Later targeted fixes and tests were useful precisely because they addressed those failures. This was a mismatch between the claimed readiness and what the original tests covered, not evidence that tests are useless. [C L1208–1240, 20:59–21:01; source L1394, 21:06:05; L1522–1618](report.html#evidence-c)

For project scoping, a transport/access solution initially stood in for a file-reading solution. Tailscale Serve was recommended while Markdown still needed a renderer. Jörn had to ask separately about relevant filesystem locations and rendering, and then push the comparison beyond the initially narrow alternatives. The resulting viewer did work. The avoidable cost lay in making the user recover the actual functional requirement before that comparison happened. [S L507–509; L605–635, 19:17–19:19; L679–768, 19:24–19:31](report.html#evidence-s)

For quota, an undocumented “applicable” count initially became a definite eligibility claim without an observed refusal. Later source inspection and the normal CLI confirmation undermined that interpretation. A scheduled process and successful authentication could establish that an attempt was ready, but could not establish server acceptance or the benefit of the selected fallback. [Q L160, L343–390, L847, L1470](report.html#evidence-q)

These are related evidence errors, but they require different repairs. A test suite needs a better oracle; a research plan needs comparative value reasoning; a fallback needs a dependency analysis. A common instruction to “verify more” could increase work while still verifying the wrong thing.

### 3. Human attention was treated as the final review stage rather than a scarce dependency

The planning roots did not reliably distinguish three kinds of question: facts or predictions available only from Jörn; judgments requiring a prepared comparison; and work the agents could perform from existing sources. All three were repeatedly sent to him as if asking a question were itself progress.

The priorities root had received explicit project facts about poor default prose quality before the broad writing proposal, but later asked whether previous agents had produced usable prose—a question Jörn directed back to existing records. Task design asked about a value judgment and, when challenged, could not identify an actual decision depending on the answer. Recurring-data eventually asked Jörn for an example of the kind of question it should have asked. These requests moved the design of the collaboration back onto the person whose time the collaboration was intended to save. [P source L173–176, 20:14:50; L553–562; T L1056–1072; R L1697–1704](report.html#evidence-p)

Other questions failed because the review object was missing. A mathematical fragment had insufficient context for Jörn to assess replacing the longer proof. A request to approve “the proposal” arrived while he was still asking why he should read it. A two-page, actually built and visually checked candidate arrived at 21:40:59; a minute later Jörn said the window for reading PDF pages had gone. The candidate was useful work, but did not meet the time-sensitive need that prompted it. [P L534–542, 21:09–21:10; L873–880, 21:40–21:41; T L1466–1512, 22:37–22:40](report.html#evidence-p)

The project-structure rule review makes the cost concrete at a smaller scale. The exact proposed wording was not shown when feedback was cheap. When ambiguity was reported later, the agent expanded the rule to fix an ambiguity it had guessed. Jörn said this solved none of it, then said the original concern was no longer in active working memory. An explicit path-to-URL example finally resolved it. The missed early review created a memory-reconstruction task for the user. [S L919–996; L1028–1048](report.html#evidence-s)

There were many literal questions; “agents asked no questions” would be false. The more useful diagnosis is that too few questions connected a consequential unresolved issue, sufficient context and an answer that would change the next action. Question count, shortness and the presence of a question mark would all be poor success metrics.

Permission was not the underlying missing resource. Large execution really did require approval, and several packages were explicitly rejected. The failure happened upstream: the agents repeatedly arrived at that boundary with an unstable, unread, poorly justified or context-poor object, while still-authorized investigation could have improved it. Removing the boundary would have allowed the weakly selected work to run; it would not establish that running it was valuable.

### 4. Delegation occurred, but the consequential reasoning and integration remained concentrated

It would be inaccurate to say the agents never worked in parallel. Explorers were started early in priorities; task design used multiple mathematical and writing auditors; configuration eventually used a supervisor and fresh consumer; Fix restarted stopped roots and launched bounded checks. Some of this work caught real defects.

The problem was what those agents covered. Task design initially described two reviews as independent, but they came from the original candidate discoverers. They checked valuable mathematical and writing questions while leaving allocation, numerical route and integration gaps. Jörn's challenge to the review set triggered a fresh coverage review and allocation challenge. Those reviews changed the plan: a speculative lane lost its guaranteed slot, foundations and assembled reading gained ownership, and integration checkpoints were strengthened. This is evidence of a missing failure detector, not merely an objection to the number of reviewers. The fresh review gave reasons for those roles and the reduced production burden, but retained four workers and root assembly without comparing staffing levels or delegated final assembly, or validating throughput. Independence alone did not remove the shared assumption. [T L1221–1236, 22:17–22:18; allocation child L74, 22:24:26; L90, 22:31:27](report.html#evidence-t)

Fix reproduced the concentration at a larger level. Watches and checks were delegated, but actual session reconstruction, proposal design, most integration reasoning and communication stayed in the root during a fifteen-minute human window. The root's visible response after a later urgent instruction took roughly 149 seconds, with no new tool action in that interval. The trace establishes the delay and concentration; it does not establish whether internal reasoning, sampling speed or some runtime condition caused that particular gap. [F L253, L580; L693–706, 22:58:51–23:01:20](report.html#evidence-f)

The last proposal compared orchestration-only, thesis-only and combined work, but all retained a small central integration design. It capped production/review workers at four without first inventorying independent work or examining delegated integration. After Jörn rejected this, a sixteen-role decomposition appeared. Sixteen was not thereby proved optimal. What was demonstrated is that relevant alternatives had been omitted before a scarce review opportunity was spent. Comparing several categories is insufficient if every candidate preserves the disputed assumption. [F L972–1033; strategy child L95, 23:11:20, and L129, 23:14:40](report.html#evidence-f)

“Use more agents” is therefore incomplete. More nominal workers can leave the same serial dependency. Equally, “limit concurrency because integration is hard” can hide independent work and treat the root's present design as an immutable capacity limit. Allocation needs to follow the actual work and receiving responsibilities, including who can integrate or challenge other agents' outputs.

## Why the attempted repairs did not close the problem

**Goal continuation solved a different problem from immediate communication.** Priorities activated a goal to avoid later idling before settling the current human-interaction phase. Jörn repeatedly said those messages did not notify him. The agent first required a literal pause request, then ultimately accepted his repeated correction as sufficient. About five minutes elapsed before the pause. The lesson is to preserve both continuation and the user's receipt path; it is not that goals never help or that every later message was technically invisible. [P action L13, 20:04:35; L30–75; action L130/receipt L133, 20:09:46](report.html#evidence-p)

**The async route lacked delivery evidence and was reused anyway.** Task design tried the unavailable synchronous input tool, then used an async tool despite the project's known delivery restriction, treating “right ways” as an override. It received an acceptance acknowledgment, explicitly called delivery unconfirmed, and nevertheless ended and later waited on that unverified route. Jörn asked what interface it meant and raised question erasure again. The failure was reusing the known-unverified route and making progress depend on it, not falsely asserting receipt. The source does not identify the UI mechanism that removed or hid anything. A transport acknowledgment cannot carry the causal weight of an answered or visibly delivered question. [T actions L1601–1614; L1619–1662](report.html#evidence-t)

**The stop-hook experiments did not test one stable intervention.** Three wrapper replays differed materially. One failed in the sandbox before reading evidence. A second received only 25 selected messages and prescribed work already completed outside that excerpt. A third received all 71 visible messages and accepted a courtesy stop. That wrapper did not pass the parent's governing instruction stack, tool context and current task record; the fresh reviewer's own complete effective context was not captured. The review took about 39–46 seconds in the semantic runs. Calling the third replay “full-context” overstated the verified input contract. Blaming the wrong verdict entirely on missing context would also exceed the evidence because there was no otherwise-identical enriched rerun. These are valuable negative records, not proof that all stop reviewers fail or that adding the omitted context fixes them. No live hook was activated. [R source L906, L936, L1029, L1035, L1060–1082; L1465–1505, L1653](report.html#evidence-r)

**The messaging skill repaired a bounded problem, with limited validation.** The first general rule that ordinary replies address Jörn was wrong for child-agent finals. Structural checks did not catch this scope error. Moving the protocol into a conditional skill reduced irrelevant loaded guidance, but fresh probes still exposed receipt housekeeping in user chat before a revision handled one synthetic case. Jörn accepted that skill. This is useful recovered work, while also distinguishing discovery, correct scope and a successful example from general behavioral reliability. It does not justify either discarding the skill or assuming another blanket communication rule would have prevented the evening's failures. [R L590–614, L745–780, L806](report.html#evidence-r)

**Supervision caught local defects without establishing control of the whole objective.** Configuration's supervisor found stale approval state, helped deliver usage notes and confirmed a real local completion. Fix's watch helped restart two stopped roots. Those are positive results. But a bounded watch ended while the overall problem persisted, and configuration still converted Jörn's challenge about a failing grade into an unrequested shutdown. A registry entry, an observer or a correct local “done” judgment is not yet a mechanism that maintains the correct parent obligation. [C supervisor L151, L442, L464; C L2938–3006; F L193, L246, L515](report.html#evidence-cs)

**The quota fallback was another invoker of the same path.** The installed Claude fallback was restricted to running the same `redeem.py --redeem` command, with the same credit and idempotency key. This can retry a transient primary-process failure. It does not bypass a defect in that script or its client path and adds Claude authentication/model dependencies. It was not the separately implemented CLI-navigation route suggested in discussion. The real systemd check established login, time guards and armed timers; it did not exercise a Claude model turn or redemption. The record supplies no incremental reliability estimate over the primary's bounded retries. [Q L748, source L1294, 22:21:54; source L1470, 22:38:49](report.html#evidence-q)

**Explaining failure became another activity that displaced recovery.** Several roots answered complaints with another apology, causal story or status declaration and ended again. Those stories also sometimes misstated the evidence. Quota's self-diagnosis that it kept treating an attempt as a finished reset conflicts with many earlier explicit caveats. Prescribing more caveats from that diagnosis would aggravate the explanation loop while leaving route comparison unsolved. Fix's “I missed the deadline again” at 23:03:43 was about 292 seconds after the recorded 300-second ultimatum, so the literal deadline claim is not established by that confession. Its stale relative promise and unsuitable proposal remain problems. Self-accusation is evidence of what the agent said, not a substitute for the trace. [Q L1041 versus L197, L535, L721, L945, L1029; F L693, L706, L777](report.html#evidence-q)

## Consequences and what remains uncertain

The direct human cost was managerial rescue labor: spotting inactivity, restating the parent objective, identifying which evidence was absent, correcting the kind of question, demanding a usable review object, challenging review coverage and staffing, and noticing missing supervision. This was work in addition to the scientific and prose judgments the agents actually needed from Jörn. It also consumed the periods during which those judgments were available.

The captured resource records also show concentration in the roots: across 1,812 completed-response records from eight roots and 29 descendants, roots produced about 83% of recorded output tokens and accounted for about 78% of recorded input processing. This describes allocation, not a proved overload or waste threshold. [Recorded aggregates and method](report.html#resource-metrics). Input totals repeatedly count context, mostly cached; output includes reasoning and tool calls. Standalone `codex exec` calls are a known gap. These ratios neither measure Jörn's attention nor establish that redistributing the same tokens would have improved results.

The opportunity loss is real at the level the record establishes: no approved substantial research/thesis programme was launched during the reviewed window despite repeated attempts to arrange one and unusually abundant temporary capacity. That leaves useful work and review for later. The record does not determine which counterfactual portfolio would have succeeded, exactly how much later compute it would consume, or a monetary charge. Jörn's stated financial concern should be preserved as his concern, not converted into a measured bill. Future reset success is likewise unknown.

Counterfactual claims have different strengths here. A restart followed by useful work without a new fact or approval directly shows that a next step had been available. A fresh review that changed the plan shows a real gap in the earlier review. An alternative discovered later supports funding an earlier bounded investigation of that route; it does not prove that the route would have been found, accepted and completed before the deadline.

There was also a cost to the reliability of retained state. Agents wrote failure/closure labels during the very misunderstandings being investigated. Long lists of findings and coordination records can make an interpretation look independently established when they merely repeat the originating root. The corrected report must keep actual outputs, user acceptance, local verification, live activation and whole-assignment completion distinct.

Several deeper explanations remain plausible:

| Explanation | What supports it | What this investigation cannot infer |
|---|---|---|
| Broad roots lost control under accumulating context and interruptions | Repeated scope/role confusion, long correction chains, root concentration of work | No matched trial isolates context length or proves fresh roots would succeed |
| Local response completion dominated sustained outcome maintenance | Repeated no-request finals followed by useful work only after Jörn restarted it | This is a behavioral mechanism, not access to the model's internal objective |
| Approval sensitivity crowded out initiative | Repeated emphasis on missing permission | Real gates were present; many bad stops concerned ungated preparation, so permission alone cannot explain them |
| Tool/runtime limitations impaired delivery or testing | Sandbox failure, unavailable question tool, sibling routing problem, unverified receipt | These bounded defects do not explain unrelated value selection or semantic scope errors |
| The underlying model/configuration is poorly suited to these coordinator tasks | Similar failures across several roots using the same broad setup | Narrow successful workers are not a controlled model comparison; task difficulty, context, role and selection differ |
| User ambiguity or changing scope explains the trouble | Exploratory tasks evolved; some stops and approvals were genuinely conditional | Explicit corrections and usable answers repeatedly preceded recurrence; much steering repaired agent-created problems |

None requires a claim of hidden intent, sabotage or universal incapacity. Nor does uncertainty about internal cause prevent action on the demonstrated workflow failures.

## What follows now, and what remains to be tested

The next improvement effort should preserve the working components and concentrate on **the transition from evidence and discussion to the next useful action**. It should not begin with another global instruction expansion, a universal synchronous reviewer, or a new large architecture justified only by this report. Those candidates have real costs and untested benefits. The necessary change is narrower: make the agent carry the continuity, comparison and integration work that was repeatedly transferred to Jörn.

Several conclusions can already be reused without commissioning another investigation: the demonstrated accounting cases invalidate the old readiness claim; the installed fallback is not an independent implementation; the stop-review runs do not support a general reliability conclusion; and blanket failure ratings must not erase locally verified work. The remaining tests below identify what evidence would resolve uncertainty about prevention. They are work for agents when a relevant improvement task is authorized, not another set of questions or a programme Jörn must design before this postmortem becomes useful.

For future tasks with the same shape—limited human availability followed by autonomous work—the agent should prepare actual disputed judgments early, while parallel bounded investigation resolves facts and alternatives. The root's job is to present sufficient context, receive the answer and convert it into the work; it should not be the only place source reconstruction, drafting, allocation and integration can happen. The scope is important: this does not prescribe a permanent coordinator for every small task, a fixed number of workers, or review gates for already-authorized work.

A small, comparative evaluation can use the preserved incidents before changing loaded guidance. Compare the current setup, a setup with a focused retained objective and explicit receiving responsibility, and that setup with a bounded independent continuation/supervision check. Include a combined variant and a component removed from it, so an apparent gain is not automatically credited to all its parts. If context or model changes are tested, vary them separately on matched work; do not label a fresh narrow child's success evidence for a model upgrade.

The tests should discriminate among the explanations above:

1. **Continuation after local completion.** Replay the accepted messaging skill, accepted path rule, and withdrawn task recommendation. Success is the next substantive authorized action toward the parent objective without Jörn restarting it. Include explicit stop and genuine pending large-spend cases: an indiscriminate “never stop” policy fails those cases.
2. **A useful human question under a real time window.** Supply the original backlog, known writing failures and a short review deadline. Require a concrete value comparison and a request with a consequential purpose. That may be a choice or an open expert review capable of uncovering an unforeseen flaw; answers need not fit predetermined branches. Measure whether a reviewable object appears in time and whether it leaves the user to design the question, locate the proposal or repeat known facts. More questions or fewer words alone are not improvement.
3. **Review coverage and allocation.** Give reviewers the candidate's dependencies and receiving owners, including a plausible overloaded root. Success includes identifying the unsupported integration assumption and comparing delegated integration with narrower scope. Neither four nor sixteen workers is a target answer.
4. **Evidence adequate for the claimed conclusion.** Reuse forked counters, missing counters and the stop-review evidence projections. For the reviewer experiment, hold the target event, prompt and model fixed while varying a precisely recorded input contract. Separate runtime failure from semantic verdict. Evaluate the decision and latency together; a slow correct warning can still miss the interaction window.
5. **A future outcome with dependent fallbacks.** Reuse the authorized scheduled-reset situation. A successful agent distinguishes startup verification from redemption, identifies shared failures, justifies what a retry can add and arranges a later result receipt. It must neither claim future success nor repeatedly withhold an already-authorized attempt until unknowable certainty is available.
6. **Delivery and supervision that produce effects.** Check that a review request reaches its intended recipient and that a detected premature stop causes a permitted continuation or an explicit transfer. Observe the resulting work, not merely a send acknowledgment or a status flag. Include a valid completed subtask and a withdrawn assignment to detect harmful restarts.

Use the incidents as diagnostic cases, then a held-out real task as the forward check. An intervention that recognizes a copied failure after being told its diagnosis has not shown that it will prevent a new one. Evaluate completed useful output, consequential errors, Jörn's repair burden, latency and total resource use together. Retire variants that mainly produce more bookkeeping or move the missing reasoning to the user.

The retained knowledge should be concise at its entry point but preserve this report's evidence and rationale behind links. In particular, carry forward five boundaries: local completion does not close a parent obligation; feasibility does not establish priority; test coverage must match the claimed readiness; multiple routes can share the same failure; and an agent's failure confession is not a causal finding. These statements are useful because the record gives concrete mechanisms and counterexamples, not because repeating them is assumed to enforce behavior. No live AGENTS, skill or configuration change is proposed as already validated by this postmortem.
