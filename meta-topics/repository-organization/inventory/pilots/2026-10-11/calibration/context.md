# Common review context

These are two complete candidate subsections for a reader-facing MSc mathematics thesis. The intended reader knows EHZ capacity, Lagrangian products and systolic ratio, but may not know the data-science methods. Judge whether each subsection gives a usable account of the ridge-area observation, its restricted mathematical interpretation, and what the subsequent candidate-selection experiments establish. The subsections can refer to an earlier capacity proof; neither is meant to introduce the full DS chapter.

Source facts available to both candidates:

- The historical table contains 4,096 generic polytopes and 10,240 polygon products from specified generators. Its recorded numerical capacity values lack original execution records establishing current certified guarantees. The pooled ridge-area/systolic-ratio rank correlation is about -0.94; that alone does not establish a population law or a within-stratum effect.
- For rotated regular pentagon products, the normalized sum of absolute symplectic two-face areas has an exact inverse-square relation with systolic ratio. The proof concerns this family. Two deliberately designed product paths numerically reverse the proposed general improvement direction at every retained step.
- A frozen fresh-pool comparison within low-ridge products found a larger mean numerical systolic ratio for the half with less concentrated ridge area: difference 0.0339924696, positive in 8 of 10 polygon-size buckets. Its prespecified descriptive sign criterion required positive overall and in at least 7 buckets; it was not a significance test.
- On one changed, separately area-normalized polygon source, a frozen target-free ridge selector enriched mean numerical systolic ratio over disjoint controls in the 4x6 and 6x6 buckets. This is finite-design evidence from one source/seed and two buckets. It is not proof of mechanism or of recurrence under other generators.

This context is a compact factual reference, not a quality label. Both candidate files are full subsections; relative organization, emphasis, and selection of detail are part of the assessment.
