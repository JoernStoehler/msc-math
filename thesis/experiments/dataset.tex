\subsection{The Dataset}
\label{sec:dataset}

We compute the EHZ capacity (the minimum symplectic action over all generalized
Reeb orbits, see Section~\ref{sec:ehz-capacity}) for 3,206 convex 4-polytopes
using two algorithms:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{HK2017}: Exhaustive search over cyclic orderings of facets.
    Works on any convex polytope. Exponential cost in facet count. Applied to
    all 3,206 polytopes. (Details: Section~\ref{sec:main-result})

  \item \textbf{Billiard}: Exploits the product structure $K_q \times_L K_p$
    (Cartesian product with Lagrangian embedding). Works only when both $K_q$
    and $K_p$ are convex polygons. Polynomial cost. Applied to 2,874 Lagrangian
    products. (Details: Section~\ref{sec:billiard})
\end{itemize}

For the 2,874 Lagrangian products, we run both algorithms and verify they agree
within $10^{-8}$ relative error (chosen to exceed double-precision roundoff).

\subsubsection*{Polytope groups and algorithm application}

The 3,206 polytopes are organized into groups by experimental purpose. Some
polytopes appear in multiple groups (e.g., a known pentagon appears in both
\textsc{LiteratureKnown} and \textsc{PentagonSweep}), so group sizes sum to
more than 3,206.

\textbf{Details by group:}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textsc{LiteratureKnown}: 9 polytopes with published capacity values
    (simplex, hypercube, crosspolytope, HKO pentagon (Haim-Kislev-Ostrover
    counterexample), two Lagrangian triangle products, two Lagrangian
    triangle-square products, one symplectic triangle product (embedded via
    symplectic rather than Lagrangian map)). File:
    \texttt{experiments/data/polytopes.jsonl} (first 9 entries).

  \item \textsc{RandomGeneric}: 330 random convex 4-polytopes with facet counts
    $F = 5, \ldots, 12$ (generated via random halfspace sampling). Not Lagrangian
    products, so billiard does not apply. File: \texttt{experiments/data/polytopes.jsonl}
    (remaining 330 entries).

  \item \textsc{RandomProducts}: 234 random Lagrangian products $K_q \times_L K_p$
    where $K_q$ and $K_p$ are random convex polygons with 3--12 sides. Used to
    verify HK2017 and billiard produce identical results across diverse polytopes.
    File: \texttt{experiments/data/random\_products.jsonl}.

  \item \textsc{PentagonSweep}: 361 polytopes of the form $P_5 \times_L R(\theta) P_5$,
    where $P_5$ is a regular pentagon and $\theta \in [0°, 90°]$ with 0.25° steps.
    Detailed analysis of one Lagrangian product family. File:
    \texttt{experiments/data/pentagon\_sweep.jsonl}.

  \item \textsc{PolygonGrid}: 2,272 polytopes $P_n \times_L R(\theta) P_m$ for
    all pairs of regular polygons $(n, m)$ with $n + m \le 12$, swept over rotation
    angles $\theta$ in the fundamental domain $[0°, 180°/\mathrm{lcm}(n,m)]$.
    Parameter sweep across Lagrangian product families. File:
    \texttt{experiments/data/polygon\_grid.jsonl}.

  \item \textsc{TestFixture}: 33 polytopes used for algorithm correctness tests.
    Includes 11 base polytopes ($F \le 8$: simplex, hypercube, triangle product,
    and 8 small random polytopes), plus 11 scaled variants (testing $c(\alpha K) = \alpha^2 c(K)$),
    plus 11 symplectomorphic variants (testing $c(MK) = c(K)$ for random $M \in \mathrm{Sp}(4)$).
    File: \texttt{crates/hk2017/tests/fixtures/capacity\_dataset.json} (stored
    with test code, not experimental data, because it is regenerated by
    \texttt{cargo test}).
\end{itemize}

For HK2017, we use an adjacency-pruned search that skips cyclic orderings where
non-adjacent facets appear consecutively (see Section~\ref{sec:main-result} for
pruning correctness). To verify pruning does not introduce errors, we additionally
compute the unpruned HK2017 variant (no adjacency check, exponential blowup) for
the 11 base polytopes in \textsc{TestFixture}. Pruned and unpruned results agree
to machine precision.
