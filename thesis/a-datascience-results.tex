\section{Data-Science Experiment Results}\label{app:datascience-results}

This appendix records the experiment packets behind
Section~\ref{sec:black-box-datascience}.  It is not a separate search surface.
The active scope is the retained random/product data-science slice, the
bounded generated scalar-proposer follow-up, and the frozen ten-facet local
optimizer comparison.  Other ascent and local-behavior packets are historical
context only for this chapter.

\subsection{Finite-budget local optimizer comparison}

The optimizer packet freezes \(64\) held-out random ten-facet starts, seven
methods, a \(1000\)\,ms measured serial-compute start-new-work cutoff, and a
cap of \(128\) full historical-evaluator calls per run.  A round begun below
the cutoff is completed atomically, so the terminal values include its
possible overrun rather than imposing a strict \(1000\)\,ms truncation; there
is no extra terminal evaluation.  Of the \(448\) traces, \(310\) have terminal
compute above the cutoff (at most \(67.525\)\,ms above), and all \(305\) traces
with an evaluated overrun round began that round below the cutoff.  The traces
contain \(28824\) full calls.  With the notation and trust boundary of
Section~\ref{subsec:black-box-datascience-finite-budget-optimization},
four-anchor branch history has the largest held-out median best value,
\(0.9848\), followed by transition prediction at \(0.9816\) and the
finite-gap affine model at \(0.9772\).  No run reaches
\(\widehat{\operatorname{sys}}=1\).

\paragraph{Frozen optimizer steps.}
All local methods propose in the \(25\)-dimensional Euclidean slice transverse
to translations, common scaling, and the linear symplectic action.  Every
outer proposal is checked by a fresh geometry reconstruction and the
configured full orbit-word search.  The implementations below are frozen
comparison points; their parameters are not claimed to optimize the
corresponding algorithm families.

The companion endpoint packet does not test local maximality.  Signed polls
along the transverse basis find an improving direction at seven of \(16\)
one-second branch-history endpoints and two of the corresponding five-second
endpoints.  Separate validated continuation paths show that even an endpoint
with no improving basis direction can retain appreciable ascent between the
tested axes.  These observations diagnose insufficient finite budgets; they
do not estimate the distance to a local maximum.

\paragraph{Detailed HKO calibration.}
On \(16\) known perturbations along four development directions from HKO, the
multi-branch continuation accepted no move at HKO and improved every
perturbation.  One step recovered \(93.4\%\)--\(100\%\) of the known evaluator
gap and removed \(77.9\%\)--\(100\%\) of the distance to HKO.  In contrast,
the gradient of one current minimizing branch recovered
\(-22.1\%\)--\(41.4\%\) of the gap.

\paragraph{Selected finite-step failure audit.}
At the eight highest four-anchor tuning endpoints, five admitted one validated
improvement, by \(1.57\cdot10^{-6}\) to \(4.61\cdot10^{-5}\).  Across the
\(80\) multi-branch proposals, \(52\) decreased the recomputed evaluator value
despite a represented target winner and positive affine prediction; \(40\) of
these losses retained determinate, unchanged geometry.  All \(40\)
current-branch gradient proposals decreased.

The three-proposal audit compared two such failures with one positive control.
At normalized radius \(10^{-5}\), the failures perturbed their named KKT
matrices by \(218\) and \(67\) times the smallest base eigenvalue magnitude
and crossed zero; the control had ratio \(1.53\), did not cross zero, and kept
the correct sign.  At radius \(10^{-8}\), finite differences converged to the
implemented analytic derivative.  Binary64 and exact-arithmetic geometry
reconstruction agreed at all \(39\) audited points, and relative volume
discrepancies were below \(10^{-15}\).  For these selected cases the observed
failure is finite-distance nonlinearity of an ill-conditioned KKT branch.

\subsection{Retained random/product input}

All retained-table method packets use the same trusted random/product input
filter.  The retained table has \(14336\) trusted rows: \(4096\) generic random
polytopes and \(10240\) random Lagrangian products.  It has no duplicate
polytope rows, no excluded-label hits, maximum observed systolic ratio about
\(0.863\), and no row with systolic ratio larger than \(1\).  Its median
systolic ratio is about \(0.311\), and its \(99\)th percentile is about
\(0.752\).

The generic random rows use facet counts \(F=5,\ldots,12\), with \(512\)
accepted samples for each facet count.  The random Lagrangian-product rows use
polygon-pair buckets \(3\leq k\leq m\leq 6\), with \(1024\) accepted samples
for each bucket.  Both use seed \(42\), height interval \([0.8,1.2]\), and
rejection until a valid polytope is produced.

\subsection{Retained-table diagnostics}

The retained-table diagnostics ask what can be learned after the systolic ratio
has already been computed for every retained row.  They can explain the sampled
table and suggest future rules, but they do not by themselves validate a
candidate proposer.

\paragraph{Trusted input filter.}
The input-filter packet fixes the data slice: \(14336\) trusted random/product
rows, no duplicates, and no retained row with systolic ratio \(>1\).  This row
audit does not validate the capacity or volume computations by itself.

\paragraph{Direct scan and tail EDA.}
The direct scan and tail summaries support the finite-table negative statement.
The largest retained value is about \(0.863\), and no obvious filtered slice
contains a positive row.  The filtered slices are overlapping views of the same
rows, not independent confirmations.

\paragraph{Scalar associations.}
The scalar-association packet screens active invariant scalar covariates
against the systolic ratio with Pearson and Spearman correlations.  The
strongest absolute Spearman correlation in the current run is about \(0.938\).
This is explanatory evidence for engineered invariant features, not a
generated-row proposer.

\paragraph{Projection, clustering, and anomaly checks.}
The projection packet runs PCA, clustering, anomaly scoring, and
source/facet/product overlays on active invariant features.  The current PCA
first coordinate has correlation about \(0.464\) with the systolic ratio, while
the top \(25\) anomaly rows do not overlap the top \(2\%\) systolic-ratio tail.
No cluster, projection, or anomaly score is a proposer unless it is promoted to
a pre-evaluation rule.

\paragraph{Supervised prediction and ranking.}
The prediction packet compares ridge and random-forest predictions from active
invariant features with provenance baselines.  The current invariant-feature
random forest has \(R^2\) about \(0.885\), while the metadata-only random
forest has \(R^2\) about \(0.009\).  This supports a geometry-signal diagnostic,
not search success.

\paragraph{Tail-rule mining.}
The tail-rule packet studies single-feature filters, shallow decision-tree
filters, grouped holdout diagnostics, and residualized feature attribution on
retained rows.  It records that ridge symplectic-area summaries are useful in
retained-table high-tail diagnostics.  Because every retained row already has
its systolic ratio computed, these rules are not generated-candidate
validation.

The common boundary is important.  These diagnostics can justify statements
such as ``the current invariant features explain part of the retained table''
or ``ridge symplectic-area summaries are visible in the high tail.''  They do
not justify statements such as ``random search cannot find a counterexample,''
``the retained table validates a new candidate proposer,'' or ``the listed
features exhaust the useful invariants of the input polytopes.''

\subsection{Generated-candidate scalar proposer}

The generated scalar-proposer packet tests a stronger interface.  It generates
random Lagrangian-product candidates, computes cheap scalar features, freezes
selected candidates and a matched baseline before evaluating the systolic
ratio, and then evaluates only that selected-or-baseline union.

In the current \(100000\)-candidate packet, the frozen rule set produced
\(485\) unique selected rows and \(1195\) unique baseline rows, for \(1675\)
unique selected-or-baseline rows.  No evaluated candidate had systolic ratio
larger than \(1\).  The maximum evaluated value, and the maximum selected
value, was about \(0.868\).

This is real pre-evaluation evidence for the configured random-product
generator and scalar rule set.  It does not validate a proposer for finding
\(>1\) examples, does not support a near-counterexample claim, and does not
generalize to all scalar rules or all random-product generators.

\subsection{Mechanism and reference diagnostics}

The mechanism and reference packets are interpretation aids, not independent
positive search results.  The ridge-mechanism diagnostic compares explanations
for the observed high-tail behavior.  Its current interpretation is that low
ridge-area magnitude is a bucket-local empirical signal, product and
combinatorial confounding remains substantial, and generated low-ridge rules
can improve mean systolic ratio over matched baselines without moving selected
maxima above \(1\).

The HKO reference and ridge-source smoke packets compare the current feature
language with the known Haim--Kislev--Ostrover example.  In the reference
coverage packet, the HKO example has systolic ratio about \(1.047\), above the
retained random/product maximum, but it is not far outside the retained
invariant-feature cloud.  The ridge-source smoke packet recomputes HKO and a
small deterministic perturbation set from source and sees the perturbations
lower the systolic ratio while increasing the ridge-area sum.  These packets
help interpret why ridge-area quantities are plausible diagnostics.  They do
not own the HKO local-maximality claim and do not turn the random/product table
into a proof about nearby examples.
