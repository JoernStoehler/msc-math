# Empirical routes to mathematical discovery

Design contribution, 2026-09-18. No new computations or theorem verification performed. This is a portfolio of scientific approaches, not a promise to execute all of them. Prepared for the broader empirical-Viterbo design task.

## Aim and source boundary

An empirical study can produce more than a predictor of a scalar objective: a new construction, an exact identity, a mechanism explaining an observed pattern, a falsified plausible principle, a classification within a family, or a computational strategy with demonstrated usefulness. These outcomes can be valuable even when no new body with sys > 1 is found. Conversely, beating one on a historical numerical objective is not automatically a new mathematical counterexample: known HKO constructions already do that, and candidate-family completeness matters.

The closure reports are synthesis sources; their linked proof/experiment owners control actual claims. No external novelty claim is made here.

Important existing context:

- The accepted pentagon analytic argument is a concrete example of a proof route much simpler than numerical branch enumeration; it is not a reason to restrict future discovery to that family.
- Within rotated regular pentagon products, the checked account gives `sys R² = 16(3+sqrt(5))` for normalized absolute ridge symplectic-area sum R. Other explicit paths lower R and sys together. Thus universal descriptor minimization is already refuted, whereas conditional mechanisms remain interesting.
- Existing product analysis defeats two appealing stories: class labels do not differ simply in active-facet count, and high-target rows are not generally enriched in close two/three-bounce ties. Do not resurrect these as untested hypotheses.

## A loop, not a fixed table pipeline

Start with a scientific question or exploratory collection. Compute inexpensive geometry first; acquire costly targets or branch traces only where needed. Observe structure, formulate competing explanations, then request new bodies/features that make those explanations disagree. Extract a simpler family or witness. Attempt a mathematical argument. Use proof failures to request the next data. Keep a distinct frozen evaluation set when claiming predictive/search performance; exploratory reuse itself is not forbidden.

There are two kinds of acquisition: selecting a body whose target is unknown, and selecting an additional measurement on an existing body. A request for all near-active branches, a support perturbation, an exact certificate, or a two-bounce geometric value can be much more informative than one more scalar capacity.

## Portfolio of routes

| Route | Concrete project-specific investigation | Mathematical output / informative failure |
|---|---|---|
| Controlled geometric interventions | Perturb polygon support heights, rotate one factor, truncate a facet, or interpolate selected normals while monitoring validity. Choose paths that hold volume or another descriptor fixed where feasible. | A directional law, a counterexample to an alleged monotonicity, or evidence that the apparent relation is confined to a stratum. Covariate matching in a table does not substitute for such constructions. |
| Exact low-dimensional families | Use regular pairs, triangle products, or a two-parameter deformation of a selected high-value body. Record branch changes and fit candidate formulas only after identifying intervals. | A piecewise exact capacity formula, extremizer classification, or sharp bound under explicit assumptions. Failure to extend a formula identifies which geometric degree of freedom matters. |
| Branch/word discovery | Track active and near-active facet words, admissibility, action gaps, and transition locations along a path rather than storing only the winning scalar. | A branch-completeness conjecture, minimal witness family, or combinatorial explanation of a transition. Unstable words can expose mistaken coordinate labels or algorithm omissions rather than genuine geometry. |
| Reverse engineering high-value bodies | Cluster selected near-extremal bodies after quotienting known symmetries; inspect support balances, facet coverage and geometric normal forms. Try to recognize simple exact coordinates. | A new construction or a candidate necessary extremality condition. If all candidates reduce to familiar geometry, that is an observed basin property, not a classification of all extremals. |
| Disproof-oriented feature science | Search explicitly for pairs with essentially equal proposed descriptors but very different sys, or opposite descriptor/target trends. | Limits of sufficient-statistic claims; a reason to add a geometric feature. Matching only within solver tolerance can create false examples, so validate selected pairs. |
| Residual-guided geometry | Subtract a proved family formula or a simple mechanistic baseline, then investigate the largest signed residuals and their geometric differences. | A missing term or a revised conjecture with identifiable hypotheses. Residuals often reveal support-lineage or feature failures first; that is useful but must not be written as geometry. |
| Necessary-condition guided search | Use the existing support-variation/active-orbit balance framework to measure which conditions candidate local maximizers violate. Generate deformations designed to repair or exploit the imbalance. | An obstruction to maximality or a structurally motivated search family. Finite sampled directions do not certify local maximality; a found improving direction does disprove it at the measured point if verified. |
| Degeneration and boundary behavior | Follow controlled paths toward redundant facets, thin factors, combinatorial changes or loss of regularity; separate geometrical limits from numerical breakdown. | Limiting formulas, boundary exclusions, or hypotheses needed for a regularity theorem. A solver failure is a datum about the solver unless bounded geometrical values independently support a claim. |
| Dual/polar and operation-based experiments | Test relevant polarity, Minkowski addition, product formation, or symmetrization operations on carefully defined body classes, deriving what is already forced by scaling first. | An operation inequality, preserved phenomenon, or a sharp counterexample to it. Applicability and capacity conventions must be checked before importing any literature relationship. |
| Dimension/family transfer | Carry a mechanism from regular to irregular polygons, products to controlled nonproducts, or one facet class to another while retaining a comparison family. | A more general conjecture or a clean boundary of validity. Start with a small design where alternatives predict visibly different outcomes, not a large indiscriminate sweep. |
| Certificate discovery | Mine candidate small orbit supports or simple inequalities that give useful bounds; use the computation to find a short human-checkable certificate. | A lemma/proof technique rather than a statistical result. A successful certificate at isolated examples is still useful, but is not a uniform family certificate. |
| Invariant construction | From an explanatory mechanism build normalized symplectic pairings, distributional ridge summaries, or branch-gap descriptors; test their transformation laws and discriminating power. | A geometrically meaningful quantity or a conjectured inequality. A predictor that is accurate only through target-derived inputs does not serve as a cheap proposer. |

These routes are not twelve independent expensive projects. A single diagnostic body can connect several: anomalous residual → controlled deformation → branch transition → simple exact formula. Teams should hand off bodies, branch traces and competing hypotheses, not only CSV summaries.

## Descriptor design needs a transformation contract

“Symplectic invariant” is stronger than “numeric feature of a polytope.” Record whether a quantity is invariant under translation, common scaling after normalization, linear symplectic maps, more general symplectomorphisms when meaningful, and facet relabeling. Product-coordinate descriptors may be useful without being intrinsic invariants. Euclidean quantities may encode the sampling distribution and still be useful predictors; the interpretation changes.

A cheap metamorphic panel can check implementation consistency under transformations whose laws are known. It is also a scientific control: an alleged geometric distinction disappearing under a symmetry is a coordinate artifact. Symplectic images of the same original body must not leak across train and test as independent geometries. Descriptor dimension and computational cost belong in its contract; a capacity-derived “feature” cannot cheaply prioritize capacity evaluation.

Three different outputs deserve separate names: (i) a proved invariant and its implemented estimator; (ii) an empirical feature useful on named populations; (iii) a mechanistic quantity appearing in a conjecture or theorem. They can coincide but need not.

## Three concrete next-design candidates

### A. Two-bounce geometry versus three-bounce takeover

Existing closure evidence says the two-bounce width term explains a capacity component, while the lower-envelope takeover remains unresolved. Ask: which geometric inputs distinguish products where the three-bounce class improves substantially over the two-bounce value?

First inspect existing triangle proof and the independently computed width fixtures; do not assume the historical class labels alone supply ground truth. Select products matched in two-bounce geometry and facet bucket but separated in the ratio of the class minima. Extract pairings, support geometry and candidate words. Build a few controlled paths connecting contrasting cases. This can motivate a simple condition in triangles before attempting arbitrary polygons.

Possible outcomes: a known triangle criterion already explains the cases (cheap synthesis); a new interpretable condition survives designed contrasts (proof lead); no low-complexity distinction emerges (document unresolved mechanism, avoid generic classifier inflation). The experiment has value without sys > 1.

### B. Transverse tests around the exact pentagon identity

The identity is established along rotation and symmetry-equivalent copies, not arbitrary product deformation. Ask which departures from that family break `sys R² = constant`, and whether its first variation vanishes for a meaningful restricted class.

Derive what is forced by translations, symplectic changes and factor scaling before evaluating anything. Choose independent non-symmetry support perturbations, include both signs and multiple step sizes, and retain active-branch data where possible. A visible linear change refutes local extension; a small numerical change suggests cancellation to investigate symbolically but does not establish it. The result could be a local identity/inequality candidate or a concise explanation of why scalar extrapolation fails.

This is not a recommendation to prioritize pentagons because they are familiar. It is an inexpensive hypothesis-separation experiment only if the current branch/evaluation machinery supports the chosen perturbations.

### C. Geometrically distinct high-value constructions

Ask whether observed high-value outputs are genuinely different from known examples after removing the transformations we understand. Construct a small comparison set including HKO images/perturbations and high-value product/general outputs. Use invariant signatures to reject easy equivalences, then inspect possible exact maps rather than treating clustering as an equivalence decision. Generate candidates targeted at missing signatures or balance patterns.

Outcomes: a plausible new construction deserving exact validation; all observed gains lie in known neighborhoods; or current invariants cannot distinguish families. Each changes the next acquisition rule. Absence of a new construction in a sampled collection is not a theorem of uniqueness.

## Promotion from observation to mathematics

For each lead preserve: the exact question, discovered versus held-out examples, parameterization, feature transformation contract, evaluator route, uncertainties, falsifying examples, and the proposed mathematical statement. Then distinguish:

1. Numerical pattern on specified inputs.
2. Reproducible designed contrast supporting or refuting an empirical explanation.
3. Conjecture with explicit domain, quantifiers and known boundary cases.
4. Exact finite verification with its finite-to-general implication still separate.
5. Proof, including completeness and endpoint/degenerate cases needed by the statement.

A successful statistical test never automatically moves a claim to step 4 or 5. Conversely, a single exact counterexample can decisively refute a universal claim, and a short proof may remove any need for thousands of additional evaluations.

## Execution recommendation

The next design pass should solicit competing scientific questions from both table-first exploration and mechanism-first mathematics. Choose one cheap discriminating continuation plus one genuinely different route, rather than automatically extending the ridge program. For each require a small pilot with an explicit decision: continue, switch to proof, acquire another feature/body, or stop. Preserve exploratory findings even when they do not win a predictive benchmark.

Planning should estimate end-to-end cost: making legal geometries, computing features/targets, interpreting failures, mathematical follow-up and exposition. The best near-term return may be extracting and checking the existing triangle/width argument, but that is a hypothesis about effort/value, not an instruction to close DS prematurely. New findings should be included because they answer useful questions, not because a method ledger needs another row.
