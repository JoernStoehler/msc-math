\phantomsection
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Disclosure of AI Use}

Generative artificial intelligence systems were used materially throughout the
research and production of this thesis.  The interaction records examined for
this disclosure principally document Codex and Claude Code agents.  Their
contributions included, but were not limited to, organizing work; writing or
editing Rust, Python, and Sage code; designing, running, and interpreting
experiments and data-science analyses; developing and reviewing mathematical
arguments; searching and inspecting literature; and drafting or revising thesis
prose.  Their role was therefore not limited to proofreading or language
correction.

In my interactions with the agents, I specified goals, constraints, priorities,
and success criteria; posed mathematical and technical questions; corrected
errors, proof gaps, notation, and misleading framing; rejected inadequate
proposals; and approved selected next actions.  Agent output was not accepted
merely because it had been generated or reported as complete.  As the submitting
author, I take responsibility for the mathematical and factual claims retained
in the final thesis.

Different tasks used different checks, including tests and regressions, exact
certificates and executable verifiers, source inspection, agent-assisted
mathematical and proof-risk reviews, and reviews of prose and the AI workflows
themselves.  These checks are not interchangeable and were not applied
uniformly to every claim or artifact.

The account of who did what is informed by an LLM-authored aggregate synthesis
of sampled Codex and Claude interaction records.  The underlying non-public
logs are not reproduced here.  They do not exhaust offline work or settle the
first origin of every idea.  Interaction records do not by themselves establish
acceptance or mathematical correctness.
