# Four-dimensional simplex claim: bounded novelty and source pinpoints

Checked 29 October 2026. **Correction to the initial 28 October assessment:** Haim-Kislev's 2019 MSc thesis already proves the universal four-simplex bound. The first version of this note missed that thesis and incorrectly described the bound itself as a potential new theorem. This remains a bounded source comparison, not an exhaustive priority search or proof audit.

## Decisive earlier theorem

- **Haim-Kislev (2019), MSc thesis, Theorem B and §4 (pp. 4–5, 22–34):** [library record](https://tau.primo.exlibrisgroup.com/permalink/972TAU_INST/quev9q/alma9932955590304146), [public digital viewer](https://tau.alma.exlibrisgroup.com/view/delivery/972TAU_INST/9932955590304146). The thesis defines `Sys(S)=c_EHZ(S)^2/Vol_4(S)` (without the factor `2` in the denominator) and proves `Sys(S) <= 3/2` for **every** four-dimensional simplex. This is precisely `rho(S)=c_EHZ(S)^2/(2 Vol_4(S)) <= 3/4` in the project's convention. The proof writes each simplex as `T S_std` for arbitrary `T∈GL(4,R)`, normalizes to `SL(4,R)`, invokes Theorem A's explicit closed-characteristic upper bound, then proves a uniform lower bound for a permutation expression. Remark 1.3 combines Theorem B with Nir's standard-simplex exact capacity to state that the standard simplex is a maximizer. The checked statement/proof does **not** classify every equality case; a new equality classification or a different short proof must be compared separately. Local primary copy: `simplex-novelty/hk-msc-2019.pdf`, extracted text `hk-msc-2019.txt`, particularly lines 184–208 and 1325–2069. The university catalog says online access is unrestricted; its digital-viewer API supplied the PDF during this check.

## Later direct statements

- **Chaidez–Hutchings (2021), §1.5, pp. 12–13:** [arXiv:2008.10111v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.10111v2) defines `sys(X)=c_EHZ(X)^n/(n! vol(X))`. Under “5-vertex polytopes (4-simplices),” it calls `sys(X) <= 3/4` **experimental**; footnote 7 suggests an analytic proof via Haim-Kislev's formula. It gives the standard simplex and another equality family, including a simplex without Lagrangian two-faces. Remark 1.20 explains its own computational method's issue with Lagrangian two-faces. This later empirical wording does not erase the earlier MSc Theorem B; the source does not explain the omission. Local extraction: `simplex-novelty/chaidez-hutchings.txt`, lines 515–561.
- **Leipold (2026), accepted dissertation §12.4, p. 122, Table 1:** [university PDF](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/80566/1/Dissertation_karla_leipold_.pdf) says its computations “empirically corroborate” Chaidez–Hutchings: in dimension four the standard simplex appears to maximize among simplices. Its table gives `max c_EHZ=0.25` for its volume-normalized `n=2` family, improvement factor 1, and root-normalized `sys_2=0.8660254037`; squaring yields `3/4`. §11.4 studies Clarke stationarity of the standard simplex. These passages do not discuss the earlier Theorem B. The dissertation also treats simplex capacity as a linear-ordering integer program (§9.2), so a proposed proof should distinguish its mathematical ingredient from the already-known capacity reformulation. Local extraction: `simplex-novelty/leipold.txt`, lines 4493–4667 and 5093–5146.
- **Haim-Kislev (2025), Example 1.10:** [arXiv:2511.16644v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16644v1) explicitly attributes the four-simplex `3/4` maximum to her own MSc thesis [HK19a]. This pointer led to the primary-thesis retrieval above.
- **Zediker (2025), arXiv:2509.19083v2:** [withdrawal/status page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19083v2) advertises an all-dimensional sharp simplex bound in the old abstract, but the 30 September 2025 author comment states that a possible `R^6` counterexample to its Theorem 1.1 was raised, all remaining new results depend on that theorem, and the paper is withdrawn pending correction. Do not cite the advertised result as an established theorem. This specifically concerns the withdrawn 2025 preprint; it does not withdraw Zediker's distinct 2023 MSc work on rotated hypercubes.

## Four-item linear-ordering polytope boundary

For six edge variables `x_ij` on four ordered items, the standard linear-ordering polytope has 24 permutation vertices and **20 facets**: 12 pair bounds `0 <= x_ij <= 1` and 8 triangle inequalities (two for each triple). The independent exact enumeration and completeness argument are recorded in `/tmp/msc-math/restart/combinatorial-frontiers/rank-order/lop4-certificate.md`, with checker `lop4_vertices.py`. Thus identifying this polytope, its 20 inequalities, or its centered polar's 20 vertices alone is not a novelty claim. A new global symplectic inequality would have to come from the reduction and a valid bound over the resulting feasible set, with normalization and equality handled explicitly.

## Calibrated conclusion

The universal four-simplex bound and standard-simplex attainment are established in Haim-Kislev's 2019 MSc thesis. A proposed new proof may offer a simpler route, a stronger equality classification, or a reusable geometric reduction; those are separate potential contributions requiring comparison to the thesis's §4 argument and other literature. Cite Haim-Kislev's Theorem B prominently, then situate the later numerical work. Do not present the `3/4` inequality or the four-item ordering polytope itself as new. The withdrawn Zediker paper is not support for an additional theorem.
