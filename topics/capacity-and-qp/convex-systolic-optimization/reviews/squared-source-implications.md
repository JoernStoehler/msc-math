## Correct conditional transfer

Let `P(r)` mean every squared planar convex body `L` obeys `area(L-L)/(4 area L) <= r`. Chaidez–Tanny define squared **domains** up to an affine isomorphism (Def. 2.9). Their Finsler construction attaches to an origin-containing convex `I`-invariant body `X` a possibly non-reversible Finsler metric `F` on `RP^2` whose **unit codisk fibers** are squared domains (Theorem 4.4, Cor. 4.7, Remarks 4.3, 4.6, Lemma 4.14). This is the right object for `P(r)`: the cotangent fiber `D_x^*F`, not the tangent unit ball. The affine equivalence causes no loss because the difference-body area ratio is affine invariant.

Reversibilization is on the tangent norm: `Fbar(v)=(F(v)+F(-v))/2` (Def. 2.17). The dual support-function identity gives `D_x^*Fbar = (D_x^*F-D_x^*F)/2` (Lemma 2.19 proof). Thus `P(r)` gives the fiberwise inequality `area(D_x^*Fbar) <= r area(D_x^*F)` and, by integration, `Vol_HT(Fbar) <= r Vol_HT(F)`. No polar or reciprocal body belongs in this step; replacing a codisk with the tangent unit ball would be an unjustified shortcut.

The exact four-sheet strict contact cover of `S^*F RP^2` by `∂X` (Theorem 5.1, (5.1)) yields `sys(X) <= 4 sys(F)` as in (5.2). A noncontractible geodesic need not lift closed in one traversal; four traversals suffice. The same cover and Stokes give `Vol_4(X)=4 Vol_HT(F)` as in (5.3), with **their unnormalized** Holmes–Thompson convention (Def. 2.14). By Lemma 3.8, `sys(F)<=sys(Fbar)`, and Ivanov–Pu (Theorem 3.7) gives `sys(Fbar)^2 <= (1/2) Vol_HT(Fbar)`. Hence

`sys(X)^2 <= 16 sys(F)^2 <= 16 sys(Fbar)^2 <= 8 Vol_HT(Fbar) <= 8r Vol_HT(F) = 2r Vol_4(X).`

For convex `X`, Chaidez–Tanny identify `sys(X)=c_EHZ(X)` (Introduction and Theorem 5.7 proof). Therefore **if** the proposed `area(L-L)<=5 area(L)` is proved, `r=5/4` and `c_EHZ(X)^2 <= (5/2) Vol_4(X)`. In the thesis ratio convention this is `c_EHZ(X)^2/(2 Vol_4(X)) <=5/4`. In Chaidez–Tanny Theorem 11's normalized square-root convention, it is `C=sqrt(5/4)=sqrt(5)/2 ≈1.1180`, better than their existence statement `C<sqrt(3/2)≈1.2247`. Their `C` is not explicitly evaluated in v1, so this is an explicit conditional improvement, **not** the Viterbo bound and **not** a claim that 5/2 is optimal for `I`-invariant four-dimensional bodies. Their Theorem 5.5 / (5.5) gives the older explicit strict `c_EHZ^2 < 3 Vol_4` in the smooth class; Theorem 5.7 obtains a universal but unspecified improvement for all convex `I`-invariant bodies.

The paper's `I` is **right multiplication** by quaternion `I` on `H`, with symplectic form determined by `I`; it is not an arbitrary involution. Since `I^2=-1`, `I`-invariance entails central symmetry. Under the cotangent action, `I` maps `(x,p)` to `(-x,-p)` (Def. 4.2), the cotangent lift of the antipodal map, so the metric descends to `RP^2`; right `J` reverses `p` and is the extra symmetry that makes it reversible. The proposed claim must retain this precise action.

The standalone constant 5 is a valid *lower bound on any universal planar upper constant*, independently of the unproved upper inequality. For `K_ε=[-1,1]×[-ε,ε]`, apply the invertible output map `(u,v)↦(u,v/(2ε))` to `σ(K_ε)`; as `ε→0` the bodies converge to `C={(x²,xy): |x|,|y|≤1}={(u,v):0≤u≤1, |v|≤√u}`. Its area is `∫_0^1 2√u du=4/3`. At horizontal difference `s∈[0,1]`, the two vertical half-widths of `C-C` are each `1+√(1-s)`, so its area is `4∫_0^1(1+√(1-s))ds=20/3`, giving `(20/3)/(4/3)=5`. This proves limiting sharpness only; it does not prove the universal upper bound.

For nonsmooth convex `X`, work first with smooth convex `I`-invariant approximants (for example smooth and average an `I`-invariant support function, adding a small ball), apply the smooth-cover argument, then use Hausdorff continuity of convex-body volume and EHZ capacity. Chaidez–Tanny explicitly authorize this route in Remark 5.4 and use continuity in Theorem 5.7. Their Remark 4.6 also gives Lipschitz `F` and squared codisk fibers directly for arbitrary convex `X`; the approximation route avoids needing to reprove nonsmooth Reeb/contact-cover details. If `X` is only a compact set without convexity/interior, neither result is asserted.

## Prior-art boundary

The Chaidez–Tanny v1 text asks whether squared Finsler metrics have further applications (Remark 8), proves squared domains strongly convex (Lemma 2.11), and uses only the general planar Rogers–Shephard `area(L-L)<=6 area(L)` in Lemma 2.19 / Proposition 3.9; Theorems 5.5 and 5.7 improve the four-dimensional constant via strictness and compactness, not an explicit 5 planar bound. There is no `5/4` squared-domain difference-body estimate in the inspected paper text. MMR Lemma 18 and Proposition 19 provide the parabolic representation of reciprocals but Section 5's inequalities concern flowers and reciprocal operations, not this difference-body ratio. MMR explicitly note in their introduction that no polytope is a reciprocal body, consistent with the proposed thin-rectangle **limit** rather than an attained planar equality.

Bounded web searches (2026-10-28) for `"squared" "difference body" "5" convex body`, `"reciprocal bodies" "difference body" area Rogers Shephard`, `"parabolic" "difference body" "5" planar convex`, `"squared bodies" Rogers Shephard inequality`, `"squaring map" "Rogers-Shephard" convex`, and `"squared domains" "Rogers-Shephard"` did not locate a primary paper proving the proposed sharp 5 bound. These searches are not a priority or novelty certificate; terms like `squared` overwhelmingly retrieve unrelated uses, and I did not inspect an exhaustive bibliography or citation graph. A new proof should be described as a proposed result pending wider prior-art review, not categorically novel.

## Material caveat

The transfer is mathematically conditional on the planar estimate for **all** squared codisk fibers. The reduction in `reduction.tex` itself labels its final scalar inequality unproved. Neither Chaidez–Tanny's nonconstructive `C<sqrt(3/2)` nor the bounded searches establish `area(L-L)<=5 area(L)`. Sharpness of the standalone planar factor 5 does not imply sharpness of `5/2` for four-dimensional systolic inequality, because the fibers of one global `X` and the four-sheet systole comparison have further compatibility/equality constraints.
