We determine how its capacity changes when one pentagon rotates relative to the other. The
volume stays constant, so this also determines which relative positions maximize the systolic

9.1    Symmetries and the Haim–Kislev–Ostrover position
We first reduce the angles to [−π/10, π/10] and identify the capacity at its endpoints. The
pentagon’s rotational symmetry gives Kθ+2π/5 = Kθ . If S is reflection in a symmetry axis of P5 ,
the simultaneous reflection (q, p) 7→ (Sq, Sp) is symplectic and sends Kθ to K−θ . Thus capacity
is even as a function of θ.
    Exchanging the two factors gives a further symmetry. The symplectic map (q, p) 7→ (p, −q)
sends Kθ to R(θ)P5 ×L (−P5 ). Rotate both factors through −θ and use −P5 = R(π/5)P5 to
obtain Kπ/5−θ . Together with evenness, this proves that capacity has period π/5. Every angle
can therefore be reduced to [−π/10, π/10] without changing capacity; evenness will give the
final expression in terms of d(θ).
