# Easy production routes: prospective protocol

Coordinator received ~30 minutes, parallel work authorized. Four operations chosen: source-preserving copy-edit, explanatory dialogue followed by transcription, overexplicit draft followed by deletion, full draft with first-obstacle reader repair. These vary source transformation, interlocutor, and revision operation rather than prescribed narration order. All use one contemporary DS scientific packet, with exact family interpretation and empirical counterevidence. This is one substantial explanatory section, not whole-chapter performance or generalization across text types.

The independent agents receive the shared brief and frozen scientific material. They may see its proposed exposition order, explicitly optional. This common scaffolding is a source of convergence; success cannot be credited wholly to production operation. Historical human objections are supplied, not discovered anew. Different routes may consume different costs and output lengths; no causal ranking or efficiency estimate from one realization.

Selection rationale: DS is an already-problematic explanatory task with now-available scientific content. HKO local edits have already been tried and would add little. These four routes exercise heterogeneous operations within a short bound. Incremental/global and independent-combination comparisons are deferred to avoid ten superficial variants. Recovering the historical accepted process needs provenance work and is not silently replaced with speculation. None of these trials tests editing human-authored notes: the scientific packet is agent-produced and checked.

Evaluation planned before candidate inspection: source fidelity and numerical/inference scope; whether the route visibly performed its operation; concrete unexplained reader dependencies; retained or newly introduced obstacles with quoted loci. A fresh evaluator will see candidates and scientific packet but no route names, if practical. Review uses exact findings rather than forced preference or PASS prediction. Word limits are targets, not quality tests. Authors preserve omissions and intermediates; reviewer findings are retained even if disputed. No silent correction of generated candidates.

No new human review, no new apparatus, no production source edits. Surviving candidates are options for the desk, not validated passing prose. No reviewer consensus counts as human evidence.
