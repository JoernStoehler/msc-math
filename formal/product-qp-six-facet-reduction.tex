\subsection{Setup and factorization}
\label{subsec:product-qp-six-facet-setup}

Let \(K_q,K_p\subset\mathbb R^2\) be full-dimensional convex polygons
containing the origin in their interiors.  Write their dual vertices as
\[
  u_1,\ldots,u_r\in\mathbb R^2,
  \qquad
  v_1,\ldots,v_s\in\mathbb R^2.
\]
The corresponding facet dual vertices of the Lagrangian product
\(K_q\times_LK_p\subset\mathbb R_q^2\oplus\mathbb R_p^2\) are
\[
  \widehat u_i=(u_i,0),
  \qquad
  \widehat v_j=(0,v_j).
\]
We use the active-word Haim--Kislev convention from
Subsection~\ref{subsec:hk2017-active-word-qp-convention}.

Fix an ordered word \(\sigma\) with distinct facet labels and a feasible
weight vector \(\beta\).  Let \(I_q\) and \(I_p\) be its sets of \(q\)- and
\(p\)-positions and put
\[
  t=\sum_{k\in I_q}\beta_k.
\]
The closure equation splits in the direct sum:
\[
  \sum_{k\in I_q}\beta_k u_{\sigma(k)}=0,
  \qquad
  \sum_{k\in I_p}\beta_k v_{\sigma(k)}=0.
\]
When \(0<t<1\), define normalized factor weights
\[
  \alpha_k=\frac{\beta_k}{t}\quad(k\in I_q),
  \qquad
  \gamma_k=\frac{\beta_k}{1-t}\quad(k\in I_p).
\]
They lie in the normalized factor-closure polytopes
\[
  C_q(\sigma)=
  \left\{
    \alpha\geq0:
    \sum_{k\in I_q}\alpha_k=1,\ 
    \sum_{k\in I_q}\alpha_k u_{\sigma(k)}=0
  \right\}
\]
and
\[
  C_p(\sigma)=
  \left\{
    \gamma\geq0:
    \sum_{k\in I_p}\gamma_k=1,\ 
    \sum_{k\in I_p}\gamma_k v_{\sigma(k)}=0
  \right\}.
\]

The symplectic form vanishes on either Lagrangian factor, while
\[
  \omega_0(\widehat u_i,\widehat v_j)=\langle u_i,v_j\rangle,
  \qquad
  \omega_0(\widehat v_j,\widehat u_i)=-\langle u_i,v_j\rangle.
\]
Consequently there is a bilinear functional
\[
  B_\sigma:C_q(\sigma)\times C_p(\sigma)\longrightarrow\mathbb R
\]
such that
\begin{equation}
  Q_\sigma(\beta)
  =
  t(1-t)B_\sigma(\alpha,\gamma).
  \label{eq:product-qp-factorization}
\end{equation}
Explicitly, the coefficient of
\(\alpha_k\gamma_\ell\langle u_{\sigma(k)},v_{\sigma(\ell)}\rangle\)
is \(+1\) if the \(q\)-position \(k\) precedes the \(p\)-position \(\ell\)
in the chosen linear representative of \(\sigma\), and \(-1\) otherwise.

\begin{lemma}[Cyclic-cut invariance]
\label{lem:product-qp-cyclic-cut-invariance}
On the Haim--Kislev feasible set, \(Q_\sigma\), and hence the factorization
in~\eqref{eq:product-qp-factorization}, is unchanged by cyclically rotating
the ordered word and its weights.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to move the first weighted dual vertex \(a_1\) to the end.
Only the terms involving \(a_1\) change sign.  The difference between the two
objectives is
\[
  -2\beta_1\sum_{k>1}\beta_k\omega_0(a_1,a_k).
\]
Closure gives
\[
  \sum_{k>1}\beta_k a_k=-\beta_1a_1,
\]
so the displayed difference equals
\[
  -2\beta_1\omega_0(a_1,-\beta_1a_1)=0.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{The six-facet reduction}
\label{subsec:product-qp-six-facet-theorem}

\begin{lemma}[Support of a planar normalized-closure vertex]
\label{lem:planar-closure-vertex-support}
Every vertex of
\[
  C=\left\{
    x\in\mathbb R_{\geq0}^N:
    \sum_i x_i=1,\ 
    \sum_i x_iw_i=0
  \right\},
  \qquad w_i\in\mathbb R^2,
\]
has at most three positive coordinates.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write the three displayed scalar equalities as \(Ax=b\), where \(A\) has
three rows.  Suppose a feasible \(x\) has more than
\(\operatorname{rank}(A)\) positive coordinates.  The columns of \(A\)
indexed by those coordinates are linearly dependent, so there is a nonzero
vector \(d\), supported on the positive coordinates of \(x\), with \(Ad=0\).
For sufficiently small \(\varepsilon>0\), both
\(x+\varepsilon d\) and \(x-\varepsilon d\) remain nonnegative and feasible.
Thus \(x\) is not a vertex.  Since \(\operatorname{rank}(A)\leq3\), every
vertex has support size at most three.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Six-facet maximizer for a four-dimensional Lagrangian product]
\label{thm:product-qp-six-facet-maximizer}
Let \(K_q,K_p\subset\mathbb R^2\) be full-dimensional convex polygons
containing the origin in their interiors, and let
\(K=K_q\times_LK_p\).
In the complete Haim--Kislev quadratic-program family for \(K\), there exists
a global maximizer whose active word contains at most three \(q\)-facets and
at most three \(p\)-facets.  Its total \(q\)-weight and total \(p\)-weight are
both \(1/2\).

In particular, the capacity value is represented by an active word of length
at most six.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The complete Haim--Kislev family is finite and each feasible weight polytope is
compact, so choose a global maximizer \((\sigma,\beta)\).
Its value \(Q_{\max}\) is positive.  Because the symplectic form vanishes on
each factor separately, a word supported on only one factor has value zero.
Thus \(\sigma\) has positive total weight in both factors, and the number
\(t\) above lies in \((0,1)\).

Fix \(\gamma\).  The map
\[
  \alpha\longmapsto B_\sigma(\alpha,\gamma)
\]
is linear on the compact polytope \(C_q(\sigma)\), so it has a vertex
maximizer \(\alpha^\ast\).  Replacing \(\alpha\) by \(\alpha^\ast\), while
keeping \(t\), \(\gamma\), and the word fixed, cannot decrease \(Q_\sigma\).
It cannot increase it strictly because \((\sigma,\beta)\) was already a
global maximizer.  Hence the replacement gives another global maximizer.

Now fix \(\alpha^\ast\) and choose a vertex maximizer
\(\gamma^\ast\in C_p(\sigma)\) of
\[
  \gamma\longmapsto B_\sigma(\alpha^\ast,\gamma).
\]
The same argument shows that this second replacement preserves the global
maximum.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:planar-closure-vertex-support},
\(\alpha^\ast\) and \(\gamma^\ast\) each have at most three positive
coordinates.

The resulting bilinear value
\(B_\sigma(\alpha^\ast,\gamma^\ast)\) is positive, because its product with
\(t(1-t)>0\) is \(Q_{\max}>0\).  Keeping
\(\alpha^\ast,\gamma^\ast\) fixed and varying only the total factor mass gives
the feasible objective
\[
  \tau(1-\tau)B_\sigma(\alpha^\ast,\gamma^\ast),
  \qquad 0\leq\tau\leq1.
\]
This is maximized at \(\tau=1/2\).  Global maximality again implies that this
replacement preserves \(Q_{\max}\).

Finally delete every zero-weight position from \(\sigma\).  Such positions
contribute neither to normalization and closure nor to any term of
\(Q_\sigma\).  The relative order of all retained positions is unchanged, so
the shorter active word has the same objective value.  The complete
Haim--Kislev family contains every ordered distinct-facet support word.
The retained word therefore represents \(Q_{\max}\), has at most three facets
of each type, and has total length at most six.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Closure-vertex capacity enumeration]
\label{cor:product-qp-closure-vertex-enumeration}
For each factor, enumerate the vertices of its normalized closure polytope.
For every pair of such vertices, enumerate all cyclic orders of the union of
their supports and evaluate \(Q_\sigma\) with each factor carrying total
weight \(1/2\).  The largest value obtained is \(Q_{\max}\), and
\[
  c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}(K_q\times_LK_p)=\frac{1}{2Q_{\max}}.
\]
Every enumerated support contains at most six facets, so after fixing one
facet as the cyclic starting point there are at most \(5!=120\) orders for a
support pair.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(C_q\) and \(C_p\) denote the normalized closure polytopes formed using
all facets of the respective factors.  The polytope \(C_q(\sigma)\) in the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:product-qp-six-facet-maximizer} is naturally the
face of \(C_q\) obtained by setting every coordinate omitted from \(\sigma\)
equal to zero; likewise for \(C_p(\sigma)\).  A vertex of a face is a vertex
of the ambient polytope.  Thus the enumeration contains the two factor
vertices selected in the theorem, and enumerating their cyclic orders contains
the shortened maximizing word.

Conversely, every enumerated candidate is feasible for the complete
Haim--Kislev family, so none can exceed \(Q_{\max}\).  The capacity formula is
the Haim--Kislev formula recorded in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:hk2017-active-word-qp-convention}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Scope of the reduction]
\label{rem:product-qp-six-facet-scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:product-qp-six-facet-maximizer} is an existence and
capacity-value statement.  It does not say that every capacity-minimizing
orbit or every maximizing Haim--Kislev weight vector has support size at most
six.  In particular, replacing a solver which returns all maximizing or
near-maximizing branches by the closure-vertex enumeration requires a separate
analysis of the consumer's output semantics.

The corollary also does not by itself certify a floating-point implementation.
A certified implementation must resolve the closure-support predicates, the
factor weights, the objective evaluations, and comparisons between candidate
values without making an incorrect sign or ordering decision.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Adversarial review record}
\label{subsec:product-qp-six-facet-adversarial-review}

The following stronger or less precise claims are \emph{not} used:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The theorem does not follow by replacing the twelve-facet billiard
    bound with an unsupported six-facet bounce bound.  Its source is bilinear
    extremality in the two normalized closure polytopes.
  \item Deleting zero-weight positions need not visibly preserve the syntax of
    a particular block enumerator.  The proof only needs the complete
    Haim--Kislev family to contain the shortened word.
  \item The theorem does not assert that every maximizer is sparse.  The HKO
    exact witness bank already contains a maximizing word of length seven.
  \item A vertex chosen in the support-restricted closure polytope must also be
    found by the proposed full-factor enumeration.  This is why the proof of
    Corollary~\ref{cor:product-qp-closure-vertex-enumeration} explicitly uses
    the fact that coordinate restriction defines a face.
  \item No general-position hypothesis is used.  Rank-deficient closure
    equations only decrease the support bound in
    Lemma~\ref{lem:planar-closure-vertex-support}; a certified implementation
    must nevertheless handle the corresponding zero determinants exactly.
\end{itemize}

\end{unverified}
