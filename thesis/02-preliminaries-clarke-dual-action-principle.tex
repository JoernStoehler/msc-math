\subsection{Clarke dual action principle}
\label{subsec:preliminaries-clarke-dual-action-principle}

The later finite arguments alter and rearrange velocities.  It is therefore
more useful to optimize over curves whose position is unconstrained than to
work directly with curves on \(\partial K\).  Clarke's dual action principle
makes this change of viewpoint precise.  Three facts are needed below: the
dual minimum is attained, a dual minimizer satisfies a weak Euler--Lagrange
inclusion, and this inclusion reconstructs a generalized characteristic.  We
cite the nonsmooth variational input and derive the convention changes and
reconstruction in the normalization used in this thesis.

The dual integrand comes from convex conjugacy.  Recall that
\(H_K=g_K^2\), and write \(H_K^*\) for its Legendre--Fenchel conjugate.

\begin{lemma}[Fenchel duality for the gauge]
\label{lem:preliminaries-fenchel-duality}
For every \(y\in\mathbb R^4\),
\[
  H_K^*(y)=\frac14h_K(y)^2.
\]
Consequently
\[
  H_K(x)+H_K^*(y)\geq\langle x,y\rangle,
\]
with equality if and only if
\[
  y\in\partial H_K(x)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  x\in\partial H_K^*(y).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For fixed \(y\), write \(x=\lambda u\), where \(\lambda\geq0\) and
\(g_K(u)=1\).  Since \(H_K(\lambda u)=\lambda^2\),
\[
  H_K^*(y)
  =\sup_{\lambda\geq0}\sup_{g_K(u)=1}
    \{\lambda\langle u,y\rangle-\lambda^2\}
  =\sup_{\lambda\geq0}\{\lambda h_K(y)-\lambda^2\}
  =\frac14h_K(y)^2.
\]
The remaining statements are the Fenchel inequality and its equality case.
\end{proof}

For a closed curve \(z\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb R^4\), define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preliminaries-dual-functional}
  I_K(z)
  \coloneqq
  \int_0^T H_K^*(-J_0\dot z(t))\,dt
  =\frac14\int_0^T h_K^2(-J_0\dot z(t))\,dt.
\end{equation}
The free-period dual problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preliminaries-dual-problem}
  d(K)\coloneqq
  \inf\left\{I_K(z):
    \begin{array}{l}
      z\in W^{1,2}([0,T],\mathbb R^4),\ z(0)=z(T),\\[-2pt]
      T>0,\ A(z)=T
    \end{array}
  \right\}.
\end{equation}
The constraint \(A(z)=T\) normalizes action relative to the chosen period; it
does not fix the period or a translation of the curve.

In Haim--Kislev's fixed-period normalization, the curves \(w\) have domain
\([0,1]\) and satisfy \(A(w)=\frac12\) \cite[Lemma~2.1]{HK2017}.  The two
normalizations have exactly the same minimization content.  If \(z\) is feasible in
\eqref{eq:preliminaries-dual-problem}, then
\[
  w(s)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2T}}z(Ts)
\]
has \(A(w)=\frac12\) and \(I_K(w)=I_K(z)/2\).  Conversely, for such a
fixed-period curve \(w\), put \(c=2I_K(w)>0\) and
\[
  z(t)=\sqrt{2c}\,w(t/c),
  \qquad 0\leq t\leq c.
\]
Then \(A(z)=c\) and \(I_K(z)=c=2I_K(w)\).  Thus the free-period infimum is
twice the Haim--Kislev fixed-period minimum.

For completeness, the attainment result of Artstein--Avidan and Ostrover uses
a different fixed-period convention.  For a feasible \(z\) of period \(T\),
set
\[
  \bar z=\frac1T\int_0^Tz(t)\,dt,
  \qquad
  u(\tau)=-\frac1{\sqrt T}J_0
    \left(z\!\left(\frac{T\tau}{2\pi}\right)-\bar z\right),
  \qquad 0\leq\tau\leq2\pi.
\]
Then \(u\) has mean zero,
\[
  A(u)=1,
  \qquad
  \frac\pi2\int_0^{2\pi}h_K^2(\dot u(\tau))\,d\tau=I_K(z).
\]
The construction is invertible after choosing \(T>0\).  Thus
\cite[Proposition~2.5]{AAO2014} gives attainment of the free-period problem
with every rotation, centering, time change, and objective factor accounted
for.  The earlier rescaling is also how one chooses an attained representative
with \(I_K(z)=T\).  Other free-period minimizers in its scaling family
generally have a different period.

The variational equation is most conveniently stated before restricting to
minimizers.

\begin{definition}[Weak dual critical curve]
\label{def:preliminaries-weak-dual-critical}
A feasible curve \(z\) of period \(T\) is \emph{weak dual critical} if there
are \(\nu\in\mathbb R\) and \(b\in\mathbb R^4\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preliminaries-dual-euler-lagrange}
  \nu z(t)+b
  \in\partial H_K^*(-J_0\dot z(t))
  \quad\text{for almost every }t.
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
  -J_0\dot z(t)\in\partial H_K(\nu z(t)+b)
  \quad\text{for almost every }t.
\]
\end{definition}

In Haim--Kislev's fixed-period convention the same equation is
\[
  8I_K(w)w+a\in\partial h_K^2(-J_0\dot w).
\]
Dividing by four and applying the preceding rescaling gives
\eqref{eq:preliminaries-dual-euler-lagrange}.  More generally, pairing that
inclusion with \(-J_0\dot z\) shows that its multiplier must be
\begin{equation}\label{eq:preliminaries-dual-multiplier-value}
  \nu=\frac{I_K(z)}{T}.
\end{equation}
The next lemma derives this equation for a minimizer and records the signs and
factors in the thesis convention.

\begin{lemma}[Euler--Lagrange equation in thesis convention]
\label{lem:preliminaries-dual-euler-lagrange}
Every minimizer of the free-period dual problem is weak dual critical.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let \(z\) be a minimizer of period \(T\).  It also minimizes \(I_K\) among
closed curves of this fixed period satisfying \(A(z)=T\).  The action
constraint is regular at \(z\), since
\[
  DA(z)[z]=2A(z)=2T\neq0.
\]
The nonsmooth Lagrange-multiplier and Euler--Lagrange argument used in
\cite[Lemma~5.2]{AAO2014}, based on Clarke's nonsmooth multiplier theory
\cite[Ch.~5]{Clarke1983}, therefore gives a scalar \(\alpha\) and a measurable
selection
\[
  p(t)\in\partial H_K^*(-J_0\dot z(t))
\]
such that, in the distributional sense,
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(J_0p(t)+\alpha J_0z(t)\bigr)
  =-\alpha J_0\dot z(t).
\]
Here the coefficient of the objective has been normalized to one using the
regularity of the constraint; \(\alpha\) includes the factor two between
\(\int\langle-J_0\dot z,z\rangle\) and \(A(z)\).  The quadratic growth of
\(H_K^*\) gives \(p\in L^2\), so the displayed identity gives it an absolutely
continuous representative.  Since \(J_0\) is invertible,
\[
  \dot p(t)=-2\alpha\dot z(t),
\]
and hence \(p(t)=b-2\alpha z(t)\) for a constant \(b\).  This is
\eqref{eq:preliminaries-dual-euler-lagrange} with \(\nu=-2\alpha\).

It remains to identify the multiplier.  Since \(H_K^*\) is
two-homogeneous, every \(p\in\partial H_K^*(y)\) satisfies
\(\langle p,y\rangle=2H_K^*(y)\).  Pairing the inclusion with
\(-J_0\dot z\), integrating, and using closedness gives
\[
  2I_K(z)
  =\int_0^T\langle\nu z(t)+b,-J_0\dot z(t)\rangle\,dt
  =2\nu A(z)
  =2\nu T.
\]
This proves \eqref{eq:preliminaries-dual-multiplier-value}.
\end{proof}

Weak criticality is preserved by the symmetry that changes the period.

\begin{lemma}[Scaling and translation]
\label{lem:preliminaries-dual-symmetry}
Let \(z\) be weak dual critical on \([0,T]\).  For every \(\alpha>0\) and
\(q\in\mathbb R^4\),
\[
  z_{\alpha,q}(t)=\alpha z(t/\alpha^2)+q,
  \qquad 0\leq t\leq\alpha^2T,
\]
is weak dual critical and satisfies
\[
  A(z_{\alpha,q})=\alpha^2T,
  \qquad I_K(z_{\alpha,q})=I_K(z).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Closure is immediate, and the translation term in the action integrates to
zero.  Substitution of \(s=t/\alpha^2\) gives the two displayed identities,
using the two-homogeneity of \(H_K^*\).  Finally,
\(\partial H_K^*(\lambda y)=\lambda\partial H_K^*(y)\) for \(\lambda>0\).
If \(\nu z+B\) realizes \eqref{eq:preliminaries-dual-euler-lagrange} for
\(z\), then
\[
  \frac{\nu}{\alpha^2}z_{\alpha,q}
  +\left(\frac{B}{\alpha}-\frac{\nu q}{\alpha^2}\right)
  \in\partial H_K^*(-J_0\dot z_{\alpha,q}).
\]
\end{proof}

We can now state the dual action principle in the form used later.

\begin{theorem}[Clarke dual action principle in free-period form]
\label{thm:preliminaries-clarke-dual-action}
Let \(K\subset\mathbb R^4\) be a convex body with
\(0\in\operatorname{int}(K)\).  The dual infimum \(d(K)\) is attained and
\[
  c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}(K)
  =\min_\gamma A(\gamma)
  =d(K),
\]
where the middle minimum is over contact-normalized generalized
characteristics on \(\partial K\).

More precisely, every such characteristic is a weak dual critical curve and
satisfies \(I_K(\gamma)=A(\gamma)\).  Conversely, the scaling-and-translation
family of every weak dual critical curve contains a contact-normalized
generalized characteristic with the same value of \(I_K\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Attainment was established by the fixed-period comparison above.
Let \(\gamma\) be a contact-normalized generalized characteristic of period
\(T\).  Then
\[
  -J_0\dot\gamma(t)\in\partial H_K(\gamma(t)),
  \qquad H_K(\gamma(t))=1.
\]
Fenchel duality gives
\(\gamma(t)\in\partial H_K^*(-J_0\dot\gamma(t))\), so \(\gamma\) is weak
dual critical with \(\nu=1\) and \(b=0\).  Moreover, two-homogeneity and the
Fenchel equality give
\[
  H_K^*(-J_0\dot\gamma(t))=H_K(\gamma(t))=1
\]
almost everywhere.  Hence \(I_K(\gamma)=T=A(\gamma)\).

Conversely, let \(z\) be weak dual critical of period \(T\), and choose
\(\nu,b\) as in Definition~\ref{def:preliminaries-weak-dual-critical}.  Put
\[
  y(t)=-J_0\dot z(t),
  \qquad x(t)=\nu z(t)+b.
\]
The multiplier calculation \eqref{eq:preliminaries-dual-multiplier-value}
applies to every weak dual critical curve.  Since \(A(z)=T>0\), the curve is
not constant; since \(H_K^*\) is positive away from the origin, this gives
\[
  \nu=\frac{I_K(z)}{T}>0.
\]
Fenchel duality gives \(y(t)\in\partial H_K(x(t))\).  The chain rule for a
convex function along an absolutely continuous curve yields
\[
  \frac{d}{dt}H_K(x(t))
  =\langle y(t),\dot x(t)\rangle
  =\nu\langle-J_0\dot z(t),\dot z(t)\rangle
  =0
\]
almost everywhere.  Thus \(H_K(x(t))=c^2\) for some \(c\geq0\).  On the
Fenchel equality set, two-homogeneity gives
\[
  H_K^*(y(t))=H_K(x(t))=c^2.
\]
After integration, \(I_K(z)=c^2T\), so \(c^2=\nu>0\).

Define, on \([0,c^2T]\),
\[
  \gamma(t)
  =c\,z(t/c^2)+\frac{b}{c}.
\]
Then \(H_K(\gamma)=1\).  Since
\(\partial H_K(x/c)=c^{-1}\partial H_K(x)\),
\[
  -J_0\dot\gamma(t)
  =-\frac1cJ_0\dot z(t/c^2)
  \in\partial H_K(\gamma(t)).
\]
Thus \(\gamma\) is a contact-normalized generalized characteristic.  It is
the member \(z_{c,b/c}\) of the family in
Lemma~\ref{lem:preliminaries-dual-symmetry}, and hence
\(I_K(\gamma)=I_K(z)\).

For every generalized characteristic \(\gamma\), the first part gives a
feasible dual curve with \(I_K(\gamma)=A(\gamma)\); therefore
\(d(K)\leq\min_\gamma A(\gamma)\).  Conversely, let \(z\) attain \(d(K)\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:preliminaries-dual-euler-lagrange} makes it weak dual critical,
and the reconstruction above gives a generalized characteristic \(\gamma\)
with
\[
  A(\gamma)=I_K(\gamma)=I_K(z)=d(K).
\]
This proves the reverse inequality and the equality of the minima.  The
identification of the minimum action with \(c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}(K)\) is the
nonsmooth least-action characterization recalled above; see also
\cite[Proposition~2.7 and Lemma~5.1]{AAO2014} and
\cite[Lemma~2.1]{HK2017}.
\end{proof}

The primal and dual problems therefore have the same minimum and corresponding
minimizing curves, but the intermediate gates should not be conflated:
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.23\linewidth}p{0.32\linewidth}p{0.36\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
dual status & information available & justified conclusion \\
\midrule
feasible & closed and \(A(z)=T\) & candidate for minimizing \(I_K\); no
boundary realization yet \\
weak dual critical & additionally satisfies
\eqref{eq:preliminaries-dual-euler-lagrange} & a scaling and translation
reconstruct a generalized characteristic, preserving \(I_K\) \\
dual minimizer & additionally \(I_K(z)=d(K)\) & reconstruction is a
capacity-minimizing generalized characteristic \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The primal curve lies on \(\partial K\) and satisfies a differential
inclusion.  The dual curve has no position constraint, and its functional
depends only on its velocity.  For a polytope, this is the freedom used in the
next section to replace a capacity minimizer by one with a finite, simple
facet word.  For a modified curve known only to be feasible, reconstruction
is not yet justified.  Once it is shown to minimize, the Euler--Lagrange lemma
supplies weak criticality and reconstruction applies.
