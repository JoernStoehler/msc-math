\section{Numerical Implementation}\label{app:numerical-v2}

% --- Intro paragraph (NEW, unreviewed) ---

The algorithms in Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} are stated for exact
real arithmetic: the KKT system is solved without error, and
predicates such as $\beta_i > 0$ are decided exactly.
An exact implementation over~$\mathbb{Q}$ is possible
(Section~\ref{app:v2-input}), but prohibitively slow for the
exponential enumeration in Algorithm~\ref{alg:ehz}.
We therefore use \texttt{f64} floating-point arithmetic for the
continuous computations (SVD, KKT solve) and reserve exact
arithmetic for discrete decisions (combinatorial data,
sign predicates).

This introduces two difficulties.
First, \emph{branching decisions} based on numerical values
(e.g.\ ``is $\beta_i > 0$?'') can go either way when the true
value is near the threshold; the algorithm must handle
the resulting uncertainty.
Second, \emph{numerically unstable steps}---in particular,
the SVD solve of near-singular KKT systems---can produce
large errors in the solution vector~$\hat\beta$, though we
show that the error in the objective value~$\hat Q$ remains
controlled.
Since we seek only an approximate minimum action
$A_{\min} = 1/(2\,Q_{\max})$, controlled~$Q$ error suffices.

The remainder of this appendix discusses these issues following
the algorithm's pipeline: input representation
(Section~\ref{app:v2-input}), transition feasibility and pruning
(Section~\ref{app:v2-pruning}), the KKT solve and its numerical
error (Section~\ref{app:v2-kkt}), and the accumulator that
combines all candidates into a final answer
(Section~\ref{app:v2-answer}).

\subsection{Input Representation}\label{app:v2-input}
% ===================================================================

% --- Rational halfspace data (from approved A.1) ---

\paragraph{Rational halfspace data.}
The algorithms in Section~\ref{sec:algorithms} operate on a polytope
$K = K(n, h) = \{x \in \mathbb{R}^4 : \langle n_i, x \rangle \leq h_i,
\; i = 1, \ldots, F\}$.
Our input polytopes have rational halfspace data:
normals $n_i \in \mathbb{Q}^4 \setminus \{0\}$
(not necessarily unit vectors, unlike the unit normals
in Section~\ref{sec:algorithms})
and heights $h_i \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$.
This allows several combinatorial quantities to be
computed exactly over~$\mathbb{Q}$, eliminating
numerical predicates from all discrete decisions.

% --- Exact quantities (from approved A.1, simplified per Jörn) ---

\begin{remark}[Exact quantities from rational coordinates]%
\label{rem:v2-exact-quantities}
Given a polytope $K = K(n, h)$ with rational halfspace data,
the following quantities are computed exactly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Vertices.}
  For each 4-element subset $S \subset \{1, \ldots, F\}$
  with $\det(N_S) \neq 0$ (where $N_S$ is the $4 \times 4$
  matrix with rows~$n_i^\top$, $i \in S$),
  the candidate vertex $v_S = N_S^{-1} h_S$
  (where $h_S = (h_i)_{i \in S}$) is computed
  via Cramer's rule over~$\mathbb{Q}$.
  The subset~$S$ defines a vertex of~$K$ if
  $\langle n_i, v_S \rangle \leq h_i$ for all $i \notin S$.
\item \emph{Vertex--facet incidence.}
  The equality $\langle n_i, v_j \rangle = h_i$
  is exactly decidable over~$\mathbb{Q}$.
\item \emph{Symplectic signs.}
  For each pair of facets $i, k$,
  the value $\omega_0(n_i, n_k) \in \mathbb{Q}$
  is exact (since $n_i \in \mathbb{Q}^4$),
  so $\operatorname{sign}(\omega_0(n_i, n_k))
  \in \{+, -, 0\}$ is determined without
  numerical error.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

% --- Perturbation trick (NEW, unreviewed) ---

\paragraph{Generic perturbation.}
% [TODO: JÖRN - verify the continuity claim for c_EHZ on polytopes.

We may assume without loss of generality that
$\omega_0(n_i, n_k) \neq 0$ for all pairs of facets $i \neq k$.
This is achieved by perturbing the rational normals:
replace each~$n_i$ by $n_i + \delta n_i$, where the
$\delta n_i \in \mathbb{Q}^4$ are chosen randomly with
$\|\delta n_i\| / \|n_i\| < 2^{-64}$.
Since $2^{-64}$ is well below the \texttt{f64} machine
epsilon~$\approx 2^{-52}$, the floating-point representations
of the normals and heights are unchanged:
all \texttt{f64} computations produce identical results
on the original and perturbed polytopes.

The condition $\omega_0(n_i, n_k) = 0$ defines an algebraic
hypersurface of codimension~$\geq 1$ in the space of normal
vectors.
A random rational perturbation avoids this hypersurface with
probability~$1$, so after perturbation
$\omega_0(n_i, n_k) \neq 0$ for all pairs $i \neq k$.

Since $\operatorname{vol}$ and $c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}$ are continuous
functions of the halfspace data, the perturbation changes
the systolic ratio by a negligible amount.
The sign pattern
$\operatorname{sign}(\omega_0(n_i, n_k)) \in \{+1, -1\}$
(with no zeros) is now fully determined from the rational
data, simplifying the enumeration of candidate
pairs~$(S, \sigma)$ and the transition feasibility check below.

% --- F64 conversion (from approved A.1) ---

\begin{remark}[Floating-point conversion]%
\label{rem:v2-f64-conversion}
A rational polytope $K = K(n, h)$ is converted to
floating-point by normalizing each normal to a unit
vector:
\[
  \hat n_i = \frac{n_i}{\|n_i\|}, \qquad
  \hat h_i = \frac{h_i}{\|n_i\|}.
\]
The halfspace $\langle n_i, x \rangle \leq h_i$ is
equivalent to
$\langle \hat n_i, x \rangle \leq \hat h_i$.
Both the rational-to-floating-point conversion
and the normalization introduce rounding errors.
The capacity algorithm uses:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \emph{exact} rational data for discrete
  decisions (which facet subsets to enumerate,
  the signs of~$\omega_0(n_i, n_k)$,
  and the exact part of transition pruning),
\item the \emph{rounded} floating-point coordinates
  for continuous computation (the SVD-based
  KKT solver and the LP feasibility check below).
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Transition Feasibility and Pruning}\label{app:v2-pruning}
% ===================================================================

% --- Transition feasibility lemma (from approved A.1) ---

\begin{lemma}[Transition feasibility]%
\label{lem:v2-transition-feasibility}
Let $K \subset \mathbb{R}^4$ be a convex polytope with
facets $F_1, \ldots, F_m$ and outward normals~$n_k$
(not necessarily unit).
A \emph{transition} $F_i \to F_j$ is a segment of a
Reeb orbit that travels with velocity~$R_i$
(Definition~\ref{def:reeb-vectors}) for positive time,
then immediately with velocity~$R_j$ for positive time.
A transition $F_i \to F_j$ exists if and only if
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \ge 0$, and
\item $\exists\, x \in F_i \cap F_j$ with
  $\langle n_k, x \rangle < h_k$
  for all $k \notin \{i,j\}$ satisfying
  $\omega_0(n_i, n_k) < 0$ or
  $\omega_0(n_j, n_k) > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A transition $F_i \to F_j$ exists if and only if
there is a point $x \in F_i \cap F_j$ and
$\varepsilon > 0$ such that
$x + t\, J_0 n_i \in K$ for $t \in [-\varepsilon, 0]$
and $x + t\, J_0 n_j \in K$ for $t \in [0, \varepsilon]$:
since $R_i = (2/h_i)\, J_0\, n_i$ with $h_i > 0$,
traveling with velocity~$R_i$ is equivalent to
moving in direction~$J_0 n_i$.

\emph{Necessity.}
Let $x \in F_i \cap F_j$ realize the transition.
For the approach ($t < 0$) to satisfy
the~$F_j$ constraint:
$\langle n_j, x + t\, J_0 n_i \rangle
= h_j + t\,\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \leq h_j$,
which forces $\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \geq 0$
(since $t < 0$).
For the departure ($t > 0$), the~$F_i$ constraint gives
$\langle n_i, x + t\, J_0 n_j \rangle
= h_i - t\,\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \leq h_i$,
again requiring $\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \geq 0$.
This proves~(1).

For~(2), suppose $k \notin \{i,j\}$ satisfies
$\omega_0(n_i, n_k) < 0$.
Then
$\langle n_k, x + t\, J_0 n_i \rangle
= \langle n_k, x \rangle + t\,\omega_0(n_i, n_k)$.
Since $t < 0$ and $\omega_0(n_i, n_k) < 0$,
their product $t\,\omega_0(n_i, n_k) > 0$, so
$\langle n_k, x + t\, J_0 n_i \rangle
> \langle n_k, x \rangle$.
If $\langle n_k, x \rangle = h_k$, this
exceeds~$h_k$, violating $x + t\, J_0 n_i \in K$.
Hence $\langle n_k, x \rangle < h_k$.
The case $\omega_0(n_j, n_k) > 0$ is analogous
for the departure.

\emph{Sufficiency.}
Given $x \in F_i \cap F_j$ satisfying~(1) and~(2),
we construct $\varepsilon > 0$ witnessing
the transition.
For $k \notin \{i,j\}$ with
$\omega_0(n_i, n_k) \geq 0$ and
$\omega_0(n_j, n_k) \leq 0$:
$\langle n_k, x + t\, J_0 n_i \rangle
\leq \langle n_k, x \rangle \leq h_k$
for $t \leq 0$,
and
$\langle n_k, x + t\, J_0 n_j \rangle
\leq \langle n_k, x \rangle \leq h_k$
for $t \geq 0$.
For the remaining $k \notin \{i,j\}$
(those with $\omega_0(n_i, n_k) < 0$ or
$\omega_0(n_j, n_k) > 0$),
condition~(2) gives
$\langle n_k, x \rangle < h_k$.
Let $\delta$ be the minimum of
$h_k - \langle n_k, x \rangle > 0$
over these~$k$ (or any $\delta > 0$ if there are none),
and choose
$\varepsilon < \delta / \max_k(
|\omega_0(n_i, n_k)| + |\omega_0(n_j, n_k)|)$.
Then all halfspace constraints hold for the
approach and departure, so $x$ together
with~$\varepsilon$ witnesses the transition
$F_i \to F_j$.
\end{proof}

% --- Numerical pruning (from approved A.1, reframed) ---

\paragraph{Early-exit heuristic.}
The transition feasibility lemma provides a sound
early-exit rule: if we can certify that a transition
$F_{\sigma(i)} \to F_{\sigma(i+1)}$ does not exist for
some consecutive pair in the cycle~$\sigma$, then no
closed Reeb orbit with combinatorics~$(S, \sigma)$ exists,
and the pair is skipped.

Condition~(1) is checked exactly using the rational
symplectic signs (Remark~\ref{rem:v2-exact-quantities}).
After the generic perturbation, $\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \neq 0$
for all pairs, so condition~(1) reduces to the strict
check $\omega_0(n_i, n_j) > 0$, which is exact.

Condition~(2) is an LP feasibility problem:
does there exist $x \in F_i \cap F_j$ strictly inside
the halfspaces indexed by the critical~$k$?
The numerical LP uses floating-point arithmetic and
checks feasibility with a slack
$\langle n_k, x \rangle \leq h_k - \varepsilon_{\mathrm{LP}}$
for a small tolerance~$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{LP}} > 0$.
% [TODO: JÖRN - double check direction of the tolerance.
%  The intent is: if the LP is infeasible even with tolerance,
%  we are confident the exact LP is also infeasible, so
%  pruning is safe. If the LP is feasible, we keep the pair
%  (conservative). The tolerance makes the pruning less
%  aggressive but never incorrect.]
This makes the heuristic conservative:
it may keep pairs that a more precise check would prune,
but it never prunes a pair that admits a valid transition.
\begin{itemize}
\item Pruned transitions are correctly pruned
  (the heuristic is \emph{sound}).
\item Transitions not pruned may still be infeasible in
  exact arithmetic; these pairs are processed anyway,
  and the KKT solve in Section~\ref{app:v2-kkt} will
  find that no admissible critical point exists
  (equivalently, $\hat\beta$ will have negative components).
  The extra work is a performance cost, not a
  correctness issue.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{KKT System and Numerical Error}\label{app:v2-kkt}
% ===================================================================

% --- Intro: SVD solve (NEW framing, unreviewed) ---

For each candidate pair $(S, \sigma)$ that survives
the pruning of Section~\ref{app:v2-pruning},
the algorithm solves the KKT linear system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-kkt-system}
  \begin{pmatrix} N^\top & 0 & 0 \\ H & -N & -\eta \\ \eta^\top & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix} \beta \\ \lambda \\ \nu \end{pmatrix}
  = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
(cf.\ equation~\eqref{eq:linear-system})
via SVD in double-precision floating-point arithmetic.
The SVD provides:
\begin{itemize}
\item a numerical solution
  $(\hat\beta, \hat\lambda, \hat\nu)$,
\item the singular values
  $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_{|S|+5}$
  of the KKT matrix, and
\item the right singular vectors, whose span for small
  $\sigma_j$ approximates the null space of the system.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Near-singularity.}
By the generic perturbation (Section~\ref{app:v2-input}),
the exact KKT system is non-singular with probability~$1$.
However, it can be \emph{near}-singular:
the smallest singular value~$\sigma_{\min}$ may be close
to zero, causing large numerical error in the
solution~$(\hat\beta, \hat\lambda, \hat\nu)$.

% --- Error decomposition (NEW, partially TODO) ---

\paragraph{Error decomposition.}
The numerical error in~$\hat\beta$ decomposes into two
qualitatively different contributions:
\begin{description}
\item[Near-null directions.]
  Along the near-null space of the KKT system (directions
  corresponding to small singular values), the
  error in $(\hat\beta, \hat\lambda, \hat\nu)$ can be
  large---of order $O(1/\sigma_{\min})$.
  However, the error in the objective value
  $Q = \hat\beta^\top H\,\hat\beta$ is only \emph{quadratic}
  in the perturbation along these directions, because the
  gradient of~$Q$ on the constraint surface vanishes at the
  critical point: any constraint-preserving
  perturbation~$\delta\beta$ contributes
  $\delta\beta^\top H\,\delta\beta$ to~$Q$, not a
  linear term (the cross term
  $2\,\delta\beta^\top H\,\beta_0$ vanishes by the
  KKT conditions; see the proof of
  Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-walk-to-boundary},
  Part~3).

\item[Well-conditioned directions.]
  Along directions corresponding to large singular values,
  the SVD gives accurate results and the
  error in~$\hat\beta$ is of order
  $O(\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\,\sigma_1 / \sigma_j)$,
  which is small.
  The resulting~$Q$ error is also small
  (bounded by standard perturbation theory).
\end{description}

tools for bounding the~$Q$ error in near-singular cases,
and describes the algorithm's handling of each case.

% --- Walk-to-boundary lemma (from approved A.2) ---

\begin{lemma}[Walk to boundary]%
\label{lem:v2-walk-to-boundary}
Let $(S, \sigma)$ be a pair for which the linear
system~\eqref{eq:v2-kkt-system} has a solution
$(\beta_0, \lambda_0, \nu_0)$ with $\beta_{0,i} > 0$ for all
$i \in S$.
Let $\delta\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{|S|} \setminus \{0\}$ satisfy
the constraint equations
\[
  N^\top \delta\beta = 0, \qquad \eta^\top \delta\beta = 0.
\]
Define the walk-to-boundary quantities:
for each~$i$ with $\delta\beta_i < 0$, set
$\alpha_i = \beta_{0,i} / |\delta\beta_i|$; then
$\alpha^* = \min\{\alpha_i : \delta\beta_i < 0\}$,
$k$ is an index achieving this minimum,
$\beta^* = \beta_0 + \alpha^*\,\delta\beta$,
and $S' = S \setminus \{k\}$.

Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\delta\beta$ has mixed signs
  (at least one positive and one negative component).
\item $\beta^*_k = 0$, $\beta^*_j \geq 0$ for all $j \in S$,
  and $\beta^*|_{S'}$ is exactly feasible:
  $N^\top \beta^* = 0$ and $\eta^\top \beta^* = 1$
  (which, since $\beta^*_k = 0$, are equivalent to
  the constraints for~$(S', \sigma|_{S'})$).
\item The action value satisfies
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-walk-q-formula}
    \beta^{*\top} H\, \beta^*
    = \nu_0 + (\alpha^*)^2\,
      \delta\beta^\top H\, \delta\beta.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the $(S', \sigma|_{S'})$ algorithm finds value
at least $\nu_0 - (\alpha^*)^2\,
|\delta\beta^\top H\, \delta\beta|$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Part~(1).}
Since $\eta_i = h_{\sigma(i)} > 0$ for all~$i$,
$\sum \eta_i\,\delta\beta_i = 0$ with $\delta\beta \neq 0$
forces at least one positive and one negative component.
In particular, $k$ and~$\alpha^*$ are well-defined
(the set $\{i : \delta\beta_i < 0\}$ is non-empty).

\emph{Part~(2).}
The walk-to-boundary construction and non-negativity argument
are identical to Steps~3--4 of
Lemma~\ref{lem:rank-deficiency-dismissal}
(they use only $\beta_0 > 0$, $\delta\beta$ having mixed signs,
and the definition of~$\alpha^*$).
Exact feasibility follows from the constraint equations:
\begin{align*}
  N^\top\beta^*
  &= N^\top\beta_0 + \alpha^*\,N^\top\delta\beta
  = 0 + 0 = 0, \\
  \eta^\top\beta^*
  &= \eta^\top\beta_0 + \alpha^*\,\eta^\top\delta\beta
  = 1 + 0 = 1.
\end{align*}
Since $\beta^*_k = 0$, these equalities pass to the
restriction~$\beta^*|_{S'}$.

\emph{Part~(3).}
Expanding the quadratic form:
\[
  \beta^{*\top} H\,\beta^*
  = \beta_0^\top H\,\beta_0
    + 2\alpha^*\,\delta\beta^\top H\,\beta_0
    + (\alpha^*)^2\,\delta\beta^\top H\,\delta\beta.
\]
The first term equals~$\nu_0$.
For the cross term, the KKT condition
$H\,\beta_0 = N\,\lambda_0 + \eta\,\nu_0$ gives
\[
  \delta\beta^\top H\,\beta_0
  = (N^\top\delta\beta)^\top\lambda_0
    + (\eta^\top\delta\beta)\,\nu_0
  = 0 + 0 = 0. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

% --- Constraint projection lemma (from approved A.2) ---

\begin{lemma}[Constraint projection of approximate null directions]%
\label{lem:v2-constraint-projection}
Let $(\delta\beta, \delta\lambda, \delta\nu)$ be a unit vector
($\|(\delta\beta, \delta\lambda, \delta\nu)\|_2 = 1$) with
residual
\[
  \begin{pmatrix}
    N^\top & 0 & 0 \\
    H & -N & -\eta \\
    \eta^\top & 0 & 0
  \end{pmatrix}
  \begin{pmatrix}\delta\beta\\\delta\lambda\\\delta\nu\end{pmatrix}
  = \begin{pmatrix}r_\beta\\r_\lambda\\r_\nu\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad
  \varepsilon
  := \bigl\|(r_\beta, r_\lambda, r_\nu)\bigr\|_2.
\]
Let $C = [N \mid \eta] \in \mathbb{R}^{|S| \times 5}$
and suppose $C$ has full column rank with smallest singular
value $\sigma_C > 0$.
Define the corrected direction
\[
  \delta\beta'
  = \delta\beta
    - C\,(C^\top C)^{-1}
      \begin{pmatrix}r_\beta \\ r_\nu\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $N^\top\delta\beta' = 0$ and
  $\eta^\top\delta\beta' = 0$.
\item $\|\delta\beta - \delta\beta'\|_2
  \leq \varepsilon / \sigma_C$.
\item $\delta\beta' \neq 0$ whenever
  $\|\delta\beta\|_2 > \varepsilon / \sigma_C$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $\Delta = C\,(C^\top C)^{-1}(r_\beta, r_\nu)^\top$.
Since $N^\top\delta\beta = r_\beta$ and
$\eta^\top\delta\beta = r_\nu$, and
$C^\top\Delta = (r_\beta, r_\nu)^\top$ by construction:
\[
  C^\top\delta\beta'
  = C^\top\delta\beta - (r_\beta, r_\nu)^\top
  = (r_\beta, r_\nu)^\top - (r_\beta, r_\nu)^\top = 0,
\]
giving~(1).
For~(2):
$\|\Delta\|
= \|C\,(C^\top C)^{-1}(r_\beta, r_\nu)^\top\|
\leq \sigma_C^{-1}\,\|(r_\beta, r_\nu)\|
\leq \varepsilon / \sigma_C$.
Part~(3) follows by the triangle inequality.
\end{proof}

% --- Approximate dismissal proposition (from approved A.2) ---

\begin{proposition}[Approximate dismissal of near-singular systems]%
\label{prop:v2-approximate-dismissal}
Let $(S, \sigma)$ be a pair for which the linear
system~\eqref{eq:v2-kkt-system} has a solution
$(\beta_0, \lambda_0, \nu_0)$ with $\beta_{0,i} > 0$ for all
$i \in S$.
Let $(\delta\beta, \delta\lambda, \delta\nu)$ be a unit vector
with residual~$\varepsilon$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-constraint-projection},
and suppose $\|\delta\beta\|_2 > \varepsilon / \sigma_C$
(so that the corrected direction~$\delta\beta'$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-constraint-projection} is non-zero).

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-walk-to-boundary}
with~$\delta\beta'$: the walk-to-boundary gives
$\beta^*|_{S'} \in M(K)|_{S'}$ with $|S'| = |S| - 1$,
and the $(S', \sigma|_{S'})$ algorithm finds value at least
\[
  \nu_0
    - (\alpha^*)^2\,\|\delta\beta'\|\,
      \Bigl(1 + \frac{\|H\|}{\sigma_C}\Bigr)\,\varepsilon,
\]
where $\|\cdot\|$ denotes the spectral norm.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-constraint-projection} gives $\delta\beta'$
with $N^\top\delta\beta' = 0$,
$\eta^\top\delta\beta' = 0$, and $\delta\beta' \neq 0$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-walk-to-boundary} then gives a
feasible boundary point $\beta^*|_{S'}$ with
\[
  \beta^{*\top} H\,\beta^*
  = \nu_0
    + (\alpha^*)^2\,\delta\beta'^\top H\,\delta\beta'.
\]

It remains to bound
$|\delta\beta'^\top H\,\delta\beta'|$.

Write $\Delta = \delta\beta - \delta\beta'$
with $\|\Delta\| \leq \varepsilon/\sigma_C$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-constraint-projection}).
The second block row of the residual equation gives
$H\,\delta\beta = N\,\delta\lambda + \eta\,\delta\nu + r_\lambda$,
so
\[
  H\,\delta\beta'
  = H\,\delta\beta - H\,\Delta
  = N\,\delta\lambda + \eta\,\delta\nu + r_\lambda - H\,\Delta.
\]
Since $N^\top\delta\beta' = 0$ and $\eta^\top\delta\beta' = 0$:
\begin{align*}
  \delta\beta'^\top H\,\delta\beta'
  &= \underbrace{(N^\top\delta\beta')^\top}_{=\,0}\delta\lambda
    + \underbrace{(\eta^\top\delta\beta')}_{=\,0}\,\delta\nu
    + \delta\beta'^\top(r_\lambda - H\,\Delta) \\
  &= \delta\beta'^\top(r_\lambda - H\,\Delta).
\end{align*}
Therefore
\[
  |\delta\beta'^\top H\,\delta\beta'|
  \leq \|\delta\beta'\|\,
       \bigl(\|r_\lambda\| + \|H\|\,\|\Delta\|\bigr)
  \leq \|\delta\beta'\|\,
       \Bigl(1 + \frac{\|H\|}{\sigma_C}\Bigr)\,\varepsilon,
\]
using $\|r_\lambda\| \leq \varepsilon$ and
$\|\Delta\| \leq \varepsilon/\sigma_C$.

\emph{Conclusion.}
Since the $(S', \sigma|_{S'})$ algorithm maximises over
all of $M(K)|_{S'}$, it finds value at least
\[
  \beta^{*\top} H\,\beta^*
  \geq \nu_0
    - (\alpha^*)^2\,\|\delta\beta'\|\,
      \Bigl(1 + \frac{\|H\|}{\sigma_C}\Bigr)\,\varepsilon.
\]
\end{proof}

% --- Near-singular handling algorithm (from approved A.2) ---

\begin{algorithm}[Near-singular system handling]%
\label{alg:v2-near-singular}
For each pair $(S, \sigma)$, the numerical algorithm solves
the KKT system~\eqref{eq:v2-kkt-system} via SVD.

\begin{description}
\item[Setup.]
  Let $C = [N \mid \eta] \in \mathbb{R}^{|S| \times 5}$
  be the constraint matrix, with smallest singular
  value~$\sigma_C$.
  Let $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_{|S|+5}$ be the singular
  values of the KKT system matrix.
  A singular value~$\sigma_j$ is \emph{small} if
  $\sigma_j < \sigma_1 \cdot \tau$ for a tolerance~$\tau$;
  the corresponding right singular vectors
  $v_j = (\delta\beta^{(j)}, \delta\lambda^{(j)},
  \delta\nu^{(j)})$ span the \emph{near-null space}.
  We say that a near-null direction~$v_j$ has
  \emph{significant $\beta$-component} if
  $\|\delta\beta^{(j)}\| > \sigma_j / \sigma_C$.

\item[Normal.]
  If no singular value is small, the system is
  well-conditioned and the algorithm uses $\hat\beta$ directly.

\item[Dismiss.]
  If $C$ has full column rank ($\sigma_C > 0$) and some
  near-null direction~$v_j$ has significant
  $\beta$-component,
  the algorithm skips the pair without computing~$\hat\beta$.
  This is justified because:
  if the system has a solution with $\beta_{0,i} > 0$ for
  all~$i$, then
  Proposition~\ref{prop:v2-approximate-dismissal} guarantees
  the value is covered (up to the error bound of
  Remark~\ref{rem:v2-dismissal-error}) by a pair
  $(S', \sigma|_{S'})$ with $|S'| = |S| - 1$;
  if not, the pair has no admissible critical point and
  contributes nothing.

\item[Otherwise.]
  The algorithm uses $\hat\beta$ directly,
  covering two sub-cases:
  (a)~$C$ is rank-deficient ($\sigma_C = 0$), so the
  constraint projection
  (Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-constraint-projection}) is not available.
  The implementation flags this case for investigation,
  since it is unclear whether~$\hat\beta$ is well-determined;
  (b)~every near-null direction has
  $\|\delta\beta^{(j)}\| \leq \sigma_j / \sigma_C$,
  meaning the near-null space is confined to the
  multiplier block~$(\lambda, \nu)$ and $\hat\beta$ is
  well-determined despite the near-singularity.

\item[Implementation note.]
  The choice of~$\tau$ is an implementation decision.
  The Rust code uses a condition-number threshold
  $\sigma_j < \sigma_1 \cdot \tau$.
  The mathematical argument
  (Remark~\ref{rem:v2-dismissal-error}) is valid whenever
  the dismiss condition holds, regardless of the threshold.
\end{description}
\end{algorithm}

\begin{remark}[Error bound for dismissal]%
\label{rem:v2-dismissal-error}
When Algorithm~\ref{alg:v2-near-singular}
dismisses via a near-null direction~$v_j$ with singular
value~$\sigma_j$, the error bound is given by
Proposition~\ref{prop:v2-approximate-dismissal} with
$\varepsilon = \sigma_j$ (the SVD residual).
The quantities in the bound are computable from the SVD
output and the exact solution $(\beta_0, \lambda_0, \nu_0)$:
the corrected direction~$\delta\beta'$ from
Lemma~\ref{lem:v2-constraint-projection},
$\alpha^* = \min\{\beta_{0,i}/|\delta\beta'_i|
: \delta\beta'_i < 0\}$,
and the bound itself:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:v2-dismissal-error-bound}
  \nu_0
    - (\alpha^*)^2\,\|\delta\beta'\|\,
      \Bigl(1 + \frac{\|H\|}{\sigma_C}\Bigr)\,\sigma_j.
\end{equation}

% --- Positivity check (from approved A.2, ad-hoc framing) ---

\paragraph{Admissibility check.}
For pairs that are not dismissed,
Algorithm~\ref{alg:ehz} requires
$\beta_i > 0$ for all $i \in S$.
Since~$\hat\beta$ differs from the true~$\beta^*$ by
numerical error, a component~$\hat\beta_i$ near zero is
ambiguous: the true value might be positive, negative,
or exactly zero.
The implementation therefore returns a three-valued verdict
for each component:
\[
  \tilde P_{>0}(\hat\beta_i) =
  \begin{cases}
    \textsc{true}
      & \text{if } \hat\beta_i > \varepsilon_\beta, \\
    \textsc{false}
      & \text{if } \hat\beta_i < -\varepsilon_\beta, \\
    \textsc{unknown}
      & \text{if } |\hat\beta_i| \leq \varepsilon_\beta,
  \end{cases}
\]
where $\varepsilon_\beta$ bounds the solve error.
If all components return $\textsc{true}$, the candidate
is certified admissible.
If any component returns $\textsc{false}$, the candidate
is inadmissible and discarded.
Otherwise, some component is $\textsc{unknown}$ and
the candidate's admissibility is uncertain:
\begin{itemize}
\item If $0 < \beta^*_i < \varepsilon_\beta$:
  the pair is genuinely admissible but may be incorrectly
  discarded ($\hat\beta_i$ could round negative).
\item If $-\varepsilon_\beta < \beta^*_i < 0$:
  the pair is genuinely inadmissible but may be incorrectly
  kept ($\hat\beta_i$ could round positive).
\item If $\beta^*_i = 0$:
  the pair is covered by a smaller
  pair $(S \setminus \{i\}, \sigma|_{S \setminus \{i\}})$,
  which the algorithm also examines.
\end{itemize}

The algorithm processes all candidate pairs
$(S, \sigma)$ and maintains an accumulator of candidates,
each annotated with an interval $[Q_{\min}, Q_{\max}]$
that contains the true objective value.
The interval width reflects the numerical uncertainty
from the KKT solve (Section~\ref{app:v2-kkt}).

\paragraph{Candidate classification.}
Each pair is classified by the algorithm into one of the
following categories:
\begin{description}
\item[Skipped.]
  Transition pruning
  (Section~\ref{app:v2-pruning})
  determines that no Reeb orbit with
  combinatorics~$(S, \sigma)$ exists.

\item[Dismissed.]
  The KKT system is near-singular and
  Algorithm~\ref{alg:v2-near-singular} certifies
  that its value is covered by a shorter pair,
  up to a bounded error
  (Remark~\ref{rem:v2-dismissal-error}).

\item[Accepted with interval.]
  The KKT solve produces~$\hat Q$ and the error analysis
  produces an interval
  $[Q_{\min}, Q_{\max}] \ni Q_{\mathrm{true}}$.
  The candidate is stored in the accumulator.

\item[Majorized.]
  A candidate whose $Q_{\max}$ is below the $Q_{\min}$
  of some other accepted candidate.
  It cannot be the global maximum regardless of
  where the true values fall within their intervals,
  so it is discarded from the accumulator.
\end{description}

\paragraph{Final answer.}
After all pairs are processed, the accumulator contains
the surviving (non-majorized) candidates.
If a single candidate's $Q_{\min}$ exceeds all others'
$Q_{\max}$, the capacity is determined:
$c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}(K) = 1 / (2\, Q_{\min})$
(up to the interval width).
If multiple candidates have overlapping intervals,
the algorithm resolves the remaining uncertainty by
re-evaluating only the surviving candidates using exact
rational arithmetic, turning each interval into an
exact value.

\begin{remark}[Empirical error tracking]\label{rem:v2-empirical-errors}
In practice, we track error bounds empirically in the Rust
implementation rather than deriving tight analytic bounds
for every operation.
The implementation tracks the best certified and best
uncertain candidates separately.
The error tracking happens after the SVD step and does not
require modifying the SVD computation itself.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Parameter scales in practice]\label{rem:v2-parameter-scales}
For polytopes generated by our random sampling
(Section~\ref{sec:random-sweep}),
the relevant quantities have the following typical magnitudes:
the symplectic inner products
$\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \in [-1, 1]$ (unit normals),
with $\omega_0(n_i, n_j) \approx 0$ occurring primarily
for near-Lagrangian 2-faces;
heights $h_i \in [h_{\min}, h_{\max}]$ with typical range
$[0.1, 10]$;
vertex norms $\|v\| \lesssim 100$;
and empirically $\beta_i = O(\|v\|)$, though the precise
scaling of $\beta$ with the polytope geometry (via the
constraints $N^\top \beta = 0$, $\eta^\top \beta = 1$)
deserves further investigation.
Before the normalisation $\eta^\top \beta = 1$,
the un-normalised period~$T$ of the orbit is bounded below
by the inradius (determined by $\min_k h_k$) and above by
the circumradius (determined by $\max_k \|v_k\|$);
the normalisation rescales $\beta$ by $1/T$.

These scales determine how aggressively the three-valued
predicates can distinguish $\textsc{true}$ from
$\textsc{unknown}$:
tolerances that are small relative to these scales yield
few $\textsc{unknown}$ verdicts.
\end{remark}
