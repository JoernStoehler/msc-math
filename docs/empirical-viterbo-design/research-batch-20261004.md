## 3. Global pentagon cover: are boundary contact patterns reducible?

**Question/payoff.** Can every minimum-area convex cover of the ten fixed
normal triangles be reduced to a controlled set of contact types? That could
make the conjectural HKO global minimum-area/equality problem approachable,
or expose a remote competing cover. This is a direct consequential inequality
attack, not a prerequisite model of every fitting vector.

**Baseline/status.** [Partner README](desk/pentagon-arbitrary-partner/README.md)
locates the source covering problem and distinguishes the solved symmetric
subclass. [Local rigidity](desk/pentagon-arbitrary-partner/local-cover-rigidity.md)
gives an exact finite local certificate plus a supplied continuum estimate;
it explicitly does not solve the global arbitrary-partner problem. The
[smooth-cell lemma](desk/pentagon-arbitrary-partner/smooth-cell-reduction.md)

boundary strata to finitely many rigid patterns.

**Discriminating probe.** Choose a concrete codimension-one boundary pattern
not equivalent to the known HKO fixture. A synthetic pattern to test puts one
template vertex on a hull edge generated by vertices of two other templates,
with other hull-order inequalities strict; first check that it is realizable.
Derive its remaining translation
degrees of freedom and the area polynomial on that stratum. Does another
constant-area escape to a lower-dimensional contact stratum always exist,
or is there a genuine stationary competitor? An explicit obstruction is as
valuable as a reduction lemma. Begin with the analytic cell argument rather
than installing exhaustive contact enumeration.

**Alternatives/cost.** Proposed first-probe cap: 45 minutes to state one
realizable boundary contact contract and its remaining translation variables,
assuming the smooth-cell argument supplies reusable coordinates. Return an
exact realizability obstruction instead if found. Deriving a complete stratum
inequality or global reduction has no reliable effort forecast. Faster: seek a
direct area inequality or a counterexample without
classification. Cheaper: independently validate the continuum/local interface
before using it as a global exclusion. Parallel: inequality and contact-reduction
approaches can exchange only their concrete obstruction/candidate. Skip a
complete fitting-vector model. Risk and review load are high, but so is the
mathematical payoff if a decisive argument appears.

**Useful failure/objection.** Noncompact strata, artificial anchoring boundaries
and nonlinear coupled contacts can defeat the reduction. A single boundary
failure must not be sold as an obstruction to every area-inequality proof.
Global success probability is presently unknown; local rigidity is not evidence
of globally absent competitors. Independent review rates this the strongest
direct difficult inequality attack, but warns that finite contact labels still
leave continuous optimization and completeness. I agree: compare a direct

## 6. Nonlinear Hamiltonian control: capacity does not fix bulk covariance

**Question/payoff.** Which geometric information can vary even when intrinsic
symplectic capacity and volume are exactly fixed? A small convexity-preserving
Hamiltonian deformation gives a control population for distinguishing true
symplectic invariance from linear-symplectic invariance. This could rule out a
sufficient-description claim before acquiring another capacity dataset, while
leaving useful population correlations and one-sided bounds open.

**Baseline/status.** A bounded Luna brainstorm recovered three neglected
alternatives from [discovery toolbox](desk/discovery-toolbox.md): self-polar
primal/dual geometry, higher-capacity spectra on toric controls, and nonlinear
Hamiltonian deformations. The last was already a parked representation-audit
idea, not a new discovery. The concrete shear below is this leaf's elementary
analytic deduction, independently checked by a Sol6.1 reviewer; no external
novelty or new numerical evidence is claimed. It uses continuous uniform-volume
covariance, not extreme-vertex covariance.

**Completed cheap discriminating derivation.** Write the unit four-ball B in
standard coordinates `(q1,p1,q2,p2)`. A compactly supported Hamiltonian equal to
`q1^3/3` on `[-2,2]^4` has, for `|epsilon|≤1/4`, the time-epsilon map on B

```
Phi_epsilon(q1,p1,q2,p2) = (q1,p1-epsilon*q1^2,q2,p2).
K_epsilon = {F_epsilon≤1},
F_epsilon = q1^2+(p1+epsilon*q1^2)^2+q2^2+p2^2.
```

All trajectories remain in the constant-cutoff region. Therefore capacity and
volume equal those of B exactly by Hamiltonian invariance. To verify convexity,
the q1,p1 block of the Hessian is

```
[[2+4*epsilon*p1+12*epsilon^2*q1^2, 4*epsilon*q1],
 [4*epsilon*q1,                    2]],
det = 4+8*epsilon*p1+8*epsilon^2*q1^2.
```

The whole sublevel lies in the convex box `|q1|≤1, |p1|≤1+|epsilon|` (and
`|q2|,|p2|≤1`). There the determinant is at least
`4[1-2|epsilon|(1+|epsilon|)]>0`, so the Hessian is positive definite.
F is strictly convex on that box, hence K is convex; its smooth boundary is
the diffeomorphic image of the sphere.

Uniform measure is pushed forward because the map preserves volume. Ball
moments are `E q1^2=1/6`, `E q1^4=1/16`; the transformed p1 mean is
`-epsilon/6`, and odd mixed moments vanish. Thus its *centered* covariance is

```
diag(1/6, 1/6+5*epsilon^2/144, 1/6, 1/6).
```

The two Williamson eigenvalues, without an ordering convention, are
`1/6` and `sqrt[(1/6)(1/6+5*epsilon^2/144)]`. One changes for nonzero epsilon
despite fixed capacity and volume. This proves that capacity+volume do not
determine this bulk spectrum and that the spectrum is not invariant under all
symplectomorphisms of convex bodies. It does **not** invalidate a one-sided
spectral bound, empirical predictive utility, or any specific vertex feature.

**Next probe/alternatives/cost.** The first analytic discrimination is already
obtained without a producer; choose which actual claimed relation it tests
before doing more. Proposed further-probe cap: 15 minutes to name
one actual claimed relation and calculate its two sides symbolically, assuming
it is expressible from this family. If no relation is selected, there is no
further execution recommendation; the completed control already stands.
If the goal is only this invariance boundary, skip polytope approximation and
new capacity calls. To test a concrete bulk-feature inequality, calculate its
two sides symbolically on this family. A polytope transfer could take hours or
more because approximation error and true-capacity bounds need separate
control; no end-to-end cost is justified yet. Parallel work is useful only if
one owner tests the relation and another bounds the representation transfer.

**Useful failure/objection.** Without a named downstream inference this is a
pleasant illustration of a known invariance distinction, not a strong research
endpoint. The control becomes informative when it falsifies or restricts an
actual proposed relation. I prefer retaining it as the genuinely different
calibration option, not declaring it more valuable than cards 1–5.
