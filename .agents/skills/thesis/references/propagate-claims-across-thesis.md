# Propagate Claims Across The Thesis

Use this reference when writing or revising the abstract, introduction,
conclusion, overview passages, or any surface that summarizes claims owned
elsewhere.

Start from the settled wording and support strength of the owning result,
method, or evidence chapter. Preserve its hypotheses, quantifiers, caveats,
and epistemic category while adapting emphasis to the summary surface. Do not
let the abstract, introduction, conclusion, or a central claim map become an
independent source of stronger wording.

Draft the abstract after the retained central results stabilize. Draft the
introduction late enough to describe the thesis that actually exists while
still using it to test the reader path. Draft the conclusion from final result
owners and distinguish established contributions, bounded empirical findings,
limitations, and future work.

When an owner changes, locate and reassess every propagated occurrence. Keep a
temporary or durable propagation map only when it reduces a real risk of
inconsistent claim strength; preserve current mappings with the relevant
chapter or central claim owner rather than in this skill.
