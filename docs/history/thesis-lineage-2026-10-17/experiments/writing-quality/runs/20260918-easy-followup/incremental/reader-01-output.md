I understand this as the opening of an empirical candidate-search study. The study compares a geometric descriptor with previously computed capacity-based targets, then asks whether the association is useful for choosing new candidates. The opening promises an exact explanation on rotated pentagon products and later tests of the search idea; it has not supplied either yet.

The data consist of 4,096 generic four-dimensional polytopes grouped by facet count and 10,240 polygon products grouped by polygon sizes. These are accepted outputs of specified random generation procedures, not a distribution-independent survey of convex bodies. The highest recorded target is about 0.863. No recorded target exceeds one, but the numerical values are historical evaluator outputs rather than certified values of the mathematical systolic ratio. I therefore cannot treat either that maximum or the absence of exceedances as a certified capacity statement.

The descriptor R sums the absolute symplectic areas of all two-dimensional faces and divides by the square root of four-dimensional volume. Taking absolute values separately prevents different faces from canceling, and also removes the need to choose a consistent orientation when evaluating the sum. The quotient is invariant under common rescaling. A face can have Euclidean area without contributing an equally large symplectic area; this is specifically a measurement involving the symplectic form.

Across the combined population, smaller R is strongly associated with larger stored targets when values are ranked. The much weaker Pearson correlation means a straight-line relationship would describe the observation poorly. Products also have a higher average target than generic polytopes, so the pooled association may partly reflect differences between the two sources. Nothing here yet establishes an association within each source or a general inequality relating R and true capacity.
