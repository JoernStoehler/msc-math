From a ridge-area association to a selection rule

Can a geometric measurement identify bodies with large systolic ratio before their capacities are computed? The retained sample suggests that the symplectic areas of two-dimensional faces are useful for this purpose. It comprises 4,096 generic four-dimensional polytopes with 5–12 facets and 10,240 Lagrangian polygon products with factor side counts 3≤k≤m≤63\leq k\leq m\leq6, equally allocated within each kind of group. Facet normals were sampled uniformly on the relevant spheres and support heights uniformly in [0.8,1.2)[0.8,1.2), accepting only bounded bodies with all proposed facets present. The observation therefore concerns these admission-conditioned distributions, rather than an unspecified population of convex bodies.

The target is the systolic ratio sys(K)=cEHZ(K)22vol4(K). \operatorname{sys}(K)=\frac{c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}(K)^2}{2\operatorname{vol}_4(K)}. For the empirical results below, syŝ\widehat{\operatorname{sys}} denotes the recorded numerical ratio. Later reevaluation supplies certified capacity intervals for 14,335 of the 14,336 original bodies, but does not certify their floating-point volumes or retrospectively turn the recorded ratios into exact values.

A two-dimensional face is called a ridge. Its symplectic area is the absolute value of the integral of ω0=dq1∧dp1+dq2∧dp2\omega_0=dq_1\wedge dp_1+dq_2\wedge dp_2 over that face. This can vanish even when the Euclidean area is positive. Define R(K)=∑F|∫Fω0|vol4(K). R(K)=\frac{\sum_F\left|\int_F\omega_0\right|} {\sqrt{\operatorname{vol}_4(K)}}. Taking the absolute value on each face prevents cancellation between faces; dividing by the square root of volume removes common scale. Both RR and the systolic ratio are also unchanged by translations and linear symplectic maps.

In the broad sample, smaller RR accompanies larger numerical systolic ratio. The pooled Spearman correlation is approximately −0.938-0.938, whereas the Pearson correlation is only −0.205-0.205: the striking relation concerns ordering, rather than a linear dependence of values. Inverse rank association also occurs within every fixed facet-count or factor-side-count group, so it does not arise solely from pooling different groups. Nevertheless, restricting the original sample to its lowest ten percent by RR within groups weakens the pooled correlation of within-group ranks from −0.944-0.944 to −0.222-0.222. The broad ordering cannot simply be assumed to persist among the most promising bodies.

For Lagrangian products the measurement has a concrete geometric meaning. Put a polygon PP in the qq-plane and QQ in the pp-plane, with cyclic edge vectors ai,bja_i,b_j and areas AP,AQA_P,A_Q. The factor faces have zero symplectic area, while the mixed rectangular face associated with two edges has area |ai⋅bj||a_i\cdot b_j|. Hence R(P×LQ)=∑i,j|ai⋅bj|APAQ. R(P\times_LQ)=\frac{\sum_{i,j}|a_i\cdot b_j|}{\sqrt{A_PA_Q}}. This turns a statistical descriptor into a quantity that can be followed exactly along a rotation family.

The edge formula gives R(Kθ)=16sin(π/5)cosdR(K_\theta)=16\sin(\pi/5)\cos d. Combining it with the proved capacity profile yields sys(Kθ)R(Kθ)2=16(3+5). \operatorname{sys}(K_\theta)R(K_\theta)^2=16(3+\sqrt5). Thus decreasing RR and increasing the systolic ratio are equivalent within this family. This is an exact explanation of one inverse relation, not a fitted trend. Its scope matters: for equilateral triangle products, both RR and the exact systolic ratio strictly decrease with the reduced rotation angle. Moreover, polygon products satisfy R≥8R\geq8, and the aligned cube attains this minimum with systolic ratio 1/21/2; the pentagon HKO body has larger RR and ratio greater than one. Specified triangle–hexagon and square–square paths likewise show numerical decreases in both quantities at every retained step. These comparisons rule out treating ridge minimization as a universal improvement direction, without erasing its value for population selection.

That weaker task can be tested prospectively. In a fresh pool of 100,000 products, selecting the ten lowest-RR bodies in each side-count group before capacity evaluation gave mean numerical ratio 0.6260.626, against 0.3160.316 for equally allocated, disjoint same-pool controls chosen without capacity values. The candidate count is not an evaluation count: the packet evaluated a 1,675-body union across its selection and control sets. A separate 100,000-product pool tested an additional descriptor, C=maxFAω(F)/∑FAω(F)C=\max_F A_\omega(F)/\sum_F A_\omega(F), the largest ridge’s share of total symplectic area. Within the lowest one percent by RR per group, the lower-CC half exceeded the upper half in mean ratio by 0.0340.034 overall, with positive differences in eight of ten groups. This compares two already selected halves, rather than isolating a causal effect of concentration.

Finally, the frozen two-stage rule was tested on a changed source: common support height one, separate factor area normalization, and joint eligibility restrictions. Each of the 4×64\times6 and 6×66\times6 groups supplied 3,200 eligible candidates, sixteen ridge-selected bodies and sixteen controls. Controls were chosen by fixed hash order outside both this selection and a concurrent covariance selection. The ridge-selected means were 0.6370.637 versus 0.3710.371 for 4×64\times6, and 0.7080.708 versus 0.5230.523 for 6×66\times6. These finite comparisons show that the rule retained a mean advantage on this particular changed source. Neither generated-selection packet nor the transfer panel found a numerical ratio above one. One transfer seed and two groups establish neither source-independent stability nor a probability of finding a counterexample. The geometric gain is therefore specific: ridge area encodes capacity variation exactly in one family and helps select higher-ratio populations in named experiments, while explicit families prevent promoting that success to a general optimization principle.
