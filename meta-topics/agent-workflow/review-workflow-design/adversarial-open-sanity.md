# Open sanity review — 18 September 2026

## Judgment

The current plan is a reasonable local investigation, but its framing risks turning a thesis-completion problem into a historical-review-recovery problem. The decisive missing comparison is whether **better scientific content and an explicit reader purpose make current target passages easier to write**, versus whether a stronger detector is needed to rescue the old passages. Both may be needed. The current experiments do not separate them.

I used full conversation context, the current-position packet, the recognition/repair/content reports, project-facts scope, current todos, the earlier process-audit reconciliation, and the retained direct DS feedback. This is adversarial interpretation, not new experimental evidence. No manuscript or active instructions were changed.

## 1. Writing and scientific explanation are coupled; the split can hide the best intervention

The repair report says neither DS variant provides the concrete conjecture-generating geometric interpretation. The content report simultaneously identifies a prepared exact product-ridge law that answers a question in the old thesis. The earlier independent process audit already highlighted precisely that cross-chapter connection as a source of better ideas. Jörn explicitly predicted that fixing content would make writing easier because the material would be less incomplete.

This is not evidence that every narrative defect is a mathematical gap. The original DS feedback describes a hoped-for discovery route, not a requirement to invent a successful final conjecture/proof. A well-written negative or incomplete scientific outcome remains in scope. But polishing an experimental ledger while preserving its explanatory deficiency is a poor test of the best authoring workflow available today.

**Decision change:** Include one contemporary DS authoring task supplied with the checked ridge-law interpretation, actual empirical limits, and intended scientific question. Compare it with a context-informed repair of the historical sample. Judge whether the reader can now understand what was sought, found, and learned. This directly tests whether the missing semantic content explains much of the apparent writing difficulty. Keep historical chronology truthful; a later analytic explanation must not become an invented historical discovery.

**Rival outcomes:** If both receive comparable human objections about ordering and sentence habits, content closure alone is insufficient and detector/workflow work stays central. If the scientifically enriched version is substantially easier to accept, prioritize content-aware authoring rather than increasingly sophisticated critique of obsolete material. A human preference without identifying what became understandable would be weaker evidence.

## 2. Recovering known objections is useful diagnosis, but not a prerequisite for acting on them

The setup deliberately withholds known human criteria to measure discovery. That makes sense for identifying a failure mode. Production, however, has those criteria and can use them. Failing to rediscover the HKO geometry-before-coordinates objection does not mean an autonomous run must fail to repair it. The supplied-feedback probe already provides narrow counterevidence.

Conversely, the current agents' positive endorsements show that an unguided final review cannot safely overrule the human judgments. The right operational split is known obligations versus unknown defects, not perfect detector versus no detector. For known defects, apply the available explanation and test the actual replacement. For unknown defects, develop a discovery process and investigate its residual errors.

**Decision change:** Do not make detector recovery of historical labels a gate on completing already-specified repairs. In the preparation phase, allow current on-task authoring probes to consume all legitimate feedback. Evaluate their new defects separately. This is compatible with, and more directly useful than, blind historical tests alone.

This objection would weaken if the proposed next experiment were already primarily a complete current-text authoring/acceptance cycle; current-position mentions such cycles, but its concrete next action still emphasizes historical-purpose review.

## 3. The sample portfolio can overinvest in easy-to-package issues

HKO was already a passing chapter with local objections. The two-page DS sample had comfortable sentences and failed structure. These are excellent diagnostic contrasts, but together they do not sample the principal remaining burden: rejected preliminaries, longer explanation dependencies, transitions among heterogeneous results, and final synthesis. A quick HKO pair is a sensible way to establish the new review desk's interaction. Repeatedly improving it would provide diminishing evidence about whole-thesis viability.

**Decision change:** After the desk's first exchange, select its next items by their contribution to whole-thesis uncertainty. At least one should come from a currently failing or substantially rewritten high-burden section, and one should expose a multi-paragraph dependency. Use actual latest workflow outputs. Do not require a statistically representative corpus before doing this; the task is choosing informative probes, not estimating a population error rate.

A low-burden current chapter reaching PASS without repeated root rescue would change my assessment more than another successful two-paragraph HKO repair. Failure on a fresh high-burden unit would identify where the universal design process has not yet transferred.

## 4. Relevant earlier reasoning was omitted from the fresh-agent packet

[The independent audit reconciliation](../process-audit/independent/reconciliation.md) already discusses reader-purpose review, diverse human coverage, cross-chapter synthesis, false completion from source integration, and avoiding benchmark-suite prerequisites. It also records successful computational recovery, warning against learning only the celebrated analytic-simplification lesson. The current packet does not point to it.

This does not invalidate new experiments: these prospective runs supply evidence the older audit lacked. It does mean apparently new recommendations can duplicate prior untested recommendations while giving the impression of progress. The useful question is now what happened when those recommendations were operationalized, and which were never tried.

**Decision change:** Add that reconciliation as an optional context pointer and distinguish previously proposed from experimentally supported interventions. Do not launch another broad retrospective. The user requested this adversarial review, but repeating the same historical diagnosis further would have little value.

## 5. Preparation needs an operational exit, not certainty that autonomy scales

Jörn's stated aim is to ensure autonomous work scales before starting the new eight-hour budget. No finite set of small probes can ensure whole-thesis PASS. The current position properly calls its readiness criterion underspecified. A prolonged sequence of cheap experiments can still be expensive in Jörn's attention and delay, and can optimize for isolated samples.

A useful readiness demonstration would be a bounded current-section completion rehearsal: recover evidence, decide its scientific account, write, review, repair, integrate, and obtain scoped human acceptance, while recording root intervention and time. This is not a new requirement to perform the full thesis twice. It tests whether the whole sequence actually runs and where the current coordination needs human judgment. It should follow the next cheap diagnostic, not become a parallel infrastructure project.

**Decision change:** Before expanding the benchmark portfolio, choose what observed result would trigger production, another specific intervention, or a pause. Actual acceptance of a substantial section and a plausible remaining-work estimate are stronger triggers than “more confidence in the detector.” Some uncertainty must remain; communicate it rather than replacing it with an arbitrary improvement threshold.

## What I would keep

The immutable artifacts, separation of human labels from AI interpretation, direct review desk, supplied-feedback repair probe, and bounded technical evidence checks are useful. Cheap distinct investigations can run in parallel. The original budget was cancelled, so there is no missed active deadline to litigate. Prepared mathematics should not be reopened merely to manufacture independent activity; equally, frozen prose should not constrain the new scientific account. The final scope remains all agreed research areas, with multiple results rather than one forced thesis-wide story.

## Highest-value next move

Let the desk complete its already-prepared HKO interaction. In parallel, prepare one DS unit from the strongest currently supported scientific account, with exact evidence and truthful limitations, using the existing human purpose feedback. Send that actual replacement through the desk and inspect the nature of remaining objections. Preserve a historical-context repair comparator if cheap. This would test an intervention that may reduce both writing difficulty and content incompleteness, rather than assuming these must be solved independently.
