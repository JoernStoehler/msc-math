\section{Numerics}\label{sec:numerics}

The computations in this thesis use different arithmetic according to the
claim they support.  Finite checks that enter a proof are performed with exact
rational or algebraic arithmetic.  In particular, the HKO certificate and the
rotated-pentagon certificate are verified in SageMath over ordered algebraic
fields; floating-point values may help to find candidate data, but the
verifiers reconstruct the proof-facing objects and decide the required
equalities, inequalities, and ranks exactly.  By contrast, high-throughput
search and exploration use binary64 arithmetic.  Selected numerical paths use
route-local true, false, or indeterminate labels for positivity and related
branch decisions; an indeterminate case may be rejected, propagated, or have
its KKT equations and positivity checked exactly where a local fallback is
implemented.  Such fallback proves neither that the KKT witness is the maximum
for that fixed word~\(\sigma\) nor that every relevant candidate was retained.
Conditional error estimates and small exact-versus-floating-point audits provide
engineering and regression evidence on selected cases, not coverage theorems.
Thus theorem-level finite claims in this thesis rest on the exact arguments
and SageMath certificates, not on floating-point acceptance.
