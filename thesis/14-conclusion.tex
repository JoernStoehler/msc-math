\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The counterexample of Haim--Kislev and Ostrover changes the question posed by
Viterbo's conjecture.  The universal inequality
\(\operatorname{sys}(K)\leq1\) is false, but the geometry behind its failure
is not thereby understood.  The motivating problem of this thesis was
therefore to determine whether the known pentagon product is easily improved,
to identify finite descriptions of the dynamics that make this question
tractable, and to test whether broader computational searches expose another
source of bodies above the former bound.  The answer obtained here is
deliberately partial: the HKO example is locally rigid in the ten-facet
stratum and optimal in the rotated-pentagon family, while the tested broader
searches produced no new counterexample.

The strongest conclusion is
Theorem~\ref{thm:hko-ten-facet-local-maximum}.  In the Hausdorff space of
polytopes with exactly ten facets, \(K_{\mathrm{HKO}}\) is locally maximal for
the systolic ratio.  After the neighborhood is made smaller, the nearby
equality set consists exactly of translations, positive scalings, and linear
symplectic images of \(K_{\mathrm{HKO}}\).  Thus the maximum is strict after
quotienting by transformations that leave the ratio unchanged.  The proof
passes to a labelled dual-vertex chart, removes the \(15\) symmetry
directions, and uses \(26\) smooth feasible upper functions.  Their exact
derivative rows span the \(25\)-dimensional transverse cotangent space and
have a strictly positive convex relation summing to zero.  Consequently at
least one upper function decreases to first order in every nonzero transverse
direction, which forces the true ratio to decrease locally.

The rotated-pentagon theorem supplies a different kind of rigidity.  For the
specific family
\[
  K_\theta=P_5\times_LR(\theta)P_5,
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:rotated-pentagon-product-formula} determines the complete
profile
\[
  \operatorname{sys}(K_\theta)
  =\frac{5+2\sqrt5}{10}\sec^2 d(\theta).
\]
The HKO relative position is therefore a global maximizer within this
one-parameter family, and the maximal value is
\((3+\sqrt5)/5\).  This conclusion comes from an exhaustive comparison of the
finite product-word family over an ordered number field, with continuity used
only to fill the finitely many exceptional parameters.  It is stronger than
the sampled curve that suggested the formula, but it is not a statement about
arbitrary polygon products or arbitrary deformations of the two factors.

Both the local theorem and the family profile depend on converting boundary
dynamics into finite data.  Haim--Kislev's quadratic-program formula
\cite{HK2017} provides the global capacity value through ordered facet words
and feasible weights.  The simple-minimizer argument explains why this finite
family contains a capacity minimizer without classifying every minimizing
generalized Reeb orbit.  For Lagrangian products of planar polygons, the
billiard reduction further yields an exhaustive two- or three-block family,
and bilinearity shows that some capacity maximizer uses at most three facets
from each factor.  These results make exact branch comparison possible while
preserving the difference between a feasible word, a stationary branch, and a
global capacity maximizer.

The flow-graph and first-order constructions answer complementary
computational questions.  Under the stated flow-graph regularity hypotheses,
affine passages through facet-pair sections and a rational fixed-point search
recover the capacity; outside that regular class, the algorithm is allowed to
reject rather than claim completeness.  In normalized facet coordinates, the
volume variation and nondegenerate fixed-word derivatives give practical
local models.  At the singular HKO data, feasible sections provide the weaker
upper functions actually needed for proof.  This separation is important:
binary64 exploration can discover words and directions, while the two
theorem-bearing conclusions use exact algebraic predicates and the
mathematical arguments that interpret them.

The empirical search gives a bounded answer to the broader discovery
question.  None of the \(14336\) rows in the retained random-polytope and
random-product table has recorded systolic ratio above \(1\).  Models built
from invariant descriptors explain substantial variation within that table,
but those rows already had their target values and hence do not validate a
candidate proposer.  In the separate pre-evaluation test, frozen scalar rules
screened \(100000\) generated random products; the selected and baseline union
contained \(1675\) evaluated rows, again with no recorded value above \(1\).
These observations are finite benchmarks for the specified samples,
generator, rules, and evaluator.  They neither show that random search cannot
succeed nor validate a new source of counterexamples.

Taken together, the strands narrow the search without resolving the global
extremal problem.  There is no improvement inside a sufficiently small
ten-facet neighborhood transverse to the natural symmetries, no improvement
within the full relative-rotation family of two regular pentagons, and no
row above \(1\) in the stated random and frozen-proposer experiments.  None
of these conclusions implies that HKO is locally maximal when facets may
appear or disappear, that it is globally maximal among four-dimensional
convex bodies, or that another structured family cannot exceed its value.

Three next questions follow directly from these boundaries.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Can the local theorem be extended across adjacent facet-count strata?
    A first step would be to analyze controlled facet splitting and
    facet-addition germs at \(K_{\mathrm{HKO}}\), where the present labelled
    ten-facet slice no longer supplies a complete chart.
  \item Can one construct a complete one-sided first-order theory at singular
    Haim--Kislev data?  Such a theory would need to organize appearing
    supports, singular optimizer sets, and competing branch germs rather than
    assuming a persistent nondegenerate catalogue.
  \item Which other polygon-product families admit exhaustive exact profiles,
    and do their minimizing-word transitions suggest deformations not sampled
    by the present random generators?  Extending the product enumeration from
    a numerical proposer to a symbolic family comparison offers a direct route
    to this question.
\end{enumerate}

The principal contribution is therefore not a replacement global conjecture.
It is a precise local theorem around the known counterexample, an exact profile
for the family that contains it, and finite computational tools whose
hypotheses and evidential roles remain explicit.  This identifies where the
HKO example is demonstrably rigid and, just as importantly, where the search
for larger systolic ratio must still remain open.
