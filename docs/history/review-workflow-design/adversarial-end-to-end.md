# Adversarial review: preparation must predict a finished thesis

2026-09-18. Independent read-only review of the proposed preparation, not an acceptance review of the thesis. No manuscript changes, experiments, evidence replays or external research were performed. This report applies the repository harness-engineering skill. Sources: [current position](current-position.md), [recognition results](recognition-results.md), [repair results](repair-results.md), [content audit](content-cruxes.md), [architecture menu](architectures.md), [decision map](decision-map.md), [experiment design](experiments.md), [project facts](../project-facts.md), and this worktree's actual [candidate entry point](../../thesis/candidate/main.tex).

## Assessment

The preparation has exposed a real failure of the existing review process and has appropriately resisted calling compliant edits a PASS. It has **not yet established a process likely to finish the accepted thesis autonomously within eight hours**. The proposed next investigations are reasonable diagnostics, but their success could leave the decisive uncertainty almost untouched: can the team independently choose, execute and integrate all consequential repairs, then stop at an acceptable PDF?

This is not a demand for a statistically validated universal detector. The best next evidence is a small, realistic rehearsal that can fail for the same reasons production would fail. An additional carefully controlled passage comparison is useful only if it selects the workflow for that rehearsal or resolves a concrete obstacle discovered there.

## 1. The readiness proxy does not yet test autonomous editorial ownership

**Evidence.** The repair authors received explicit human objections, including explanatory content. The review was also criterion-conditioned. There is no fresh human verdict on the resulting drafts in the supplied record. Recognition tested discovery but not repair; repair tested execution but not independent discovery or final acceptance. Root states these limitations correctly.

**Consequence.** Combining these two results does not fill the missing link. Future successful local cycles can still depend on root selecting the right material, supplying the right account of its purpose, resolving conflicts and recognizing when a proposed repair is sufficient. Those are production tasks, not free experimental infrastructure. A workflow might work with skilled editorial direction and fail when nobody supplies it during the autonomous run.

**Change.** Freeze a realistic production packet and let a fresh team own a complete chapter revision, including issue selection, source consultation, edits, verification and a stopping decision. Root may provide established scope and reader facts but must record any subsequent editorial rescue as an intervention. Obtain a fresh human verdict on the resulting rendered chapter, through the existing review desk. Do not ask only whether particular supplied objections disappeared. A global verdict plus the first consequential objection is more informative than a long forced checklist.

**What would change my mind.** An unrescued chapter that Jörn accepts, with preserved mathematical/empirical meaning and a measured usable completion time, would materially increase confidence. A passage rewrite accepted after another detailed human diagnosis would improve confidence in execution, not autonomous ownership. Failure after a frozen packet would locate the missing capability without making a universal incapability claim.

## 2. Whole-thesis integration is a different task, and it already has observable failures

**Evidence.** The content audit found an interrupted candidate that mixes frozen and replacement chapters, with existing replacement preliminaries/flow files not wired into the build. Pentagon companions, numerics claims, introduction/conclusion and reproduction promises require coordinated changes. The candidate entry point contains foundations, QP, flow, variation, HKO, DS, pentagon, visualization, numerics, availability, AI reflection, conclusion and computational appendices. HKO and a two-page DS sample cover only a fraction of that surface.

**Consequence.** More than one passing local text type is necessary evidence for transfer, but no numerical sample threshold turns it into whole-thesis readiness. Failures include omitted required material, correct facts under incompatible conventions, chapter openings assuming definitions that moved, accumulated reader fatigue, disproportionate emphasis and promises that lag behind revised results. The audited mixed-source build is direct evidence that integration deserves its own test, not a speculative extra concern.

**Change.** Make the rehearsal include at least one real dependency boundary: a revised result and its introduction/conclusion or numerics/reproduction companions. Produce one assembled PDF. Independently check the actual input manifest and follow a small set of important claims through statement, proof/evidence, numerical interpretation and conclusion. Preserve the multi-result scope: project fact 9 explicitly rejects forcing all results into one tight narrative. A DS discovery narrative must not silently become a template for the whole thesis.

The purpose of this boundary test is early falsification, not miniature certification. It complements a chapter acceptance test; neither alone covers the entire document. Before resuming, every required content area needs an explicit disposition: retain with supporting evidence, revise through a demonstrated route, or record a concrete unresolved decision. This can be a short inventory derived from existing scope, not a new elaborate planning system.

**What would change my mind.** A chapter-plus-companions rehearsal that survives both human reading and independent cross-source checks would support scaling much more than ten accepted excerpts. If most untouched chapters already have current human acceptance on exactly the assembled versions, the coverage burden is smaller; the present record does not establish that.

## 3. The eight-hour forecast needs a critical path, not cheap-agent extrapolation

**Evidence.** Recognition cost $2.50 including coordinator/workers during its 108-second accounting window, excluding preparation and writeup. This establishes that one diagnostic was cheap. The content audit's judgmental estimates total approximately **5.25–13.25 focused-agent hours**, including DS's 1–4 hours and excluding the optional general-product proposition. Writing-quality iteration is explicitly excluded. These are not measured runtimes, independent tasks or an additive wall-clock forecast.

**Consequence.** Parallelism may make technical work fit comfortably. It cannot establish that DS receipt/replay or fresh study → defensible scientific account → chapter revision → dependent introduction/conclusion updates → final review fits. A later scientific correction can invalidate earlier prose. The large dollar allowance does not buy away serial diagnosis, human feedback delay, source conflicts or final editorial decisions. Conversely, the evidence does not show that the dollar limit is remotely binding.

**Change.** Use the rehearsal to measure the entire elapsed interval, coordinator work, waiting, author/reviewer usage and repair rounds, including unsuccessful candidates. Then map its throughput onto the actual remaining units and dependency order. Name a reserved interval for assembled reading/build/references and repairs after that review; do not allocate all eight hours to initial drafting. Give a range and identify the serial assumption that would break it. No fine-grained rate model or precise success percentage is justified yet.

The four short checks already proposed in the content audit should run alongside workflow investigation when authorized. DS recovery is particularly urgent: it determines whether authors should explain retained evidence or wait for a new scientific study. Source-correspondence and QP checks similarly prevent elegant writing around unstable claims. They are not substitutes for writing research, but delaying them sacrifices useful concurrency and leaves the rehearsal's input contract uncertain.

**What would change my mind.** Timed recovery/replay with stable scientific claims and measured chapter integration would make eight-hour completion substantially more credible. Finding only evaluator infrastructure and no usable DS population would weaken that forecast until representative generation and analysis throughput is measured.

## 4. Preparation currently has no operational stopping decision

**Evidence.** The current position explicitly says readiness thresholds and extrapolation are underspecified. Older design files still discuss 60–90 minutes of screening inside an eight-hour session, while the current position says the production clock was cancelled and restarts later. Neither provides a current preparation stopping policy. The architecture menu is large enough to sustain indefinitely informative but nondecisive experiments.

**Consequence.** Moving research outside the production clock can make the eventual eight-hour claim misleading if preparation quietly performs most editorial work. More seriously, each failure can invite another detector variant while source integration and substantive authoring remain untested. A universal writing detector is neither necessary nor sufficient for one accepted thesis. A direct source-grounded authoring/reconstruction workflow could succeed even if its explicit critic misses historical labels.

**Change.** Predeclare one bounded decision round: (a) close the cheap technical uncertainties; (b) select at most two substantially different complete workflows, including a direct authoring/reconstruction route rather than only critique-first variants; (c) rehearse realistic output; (d) obtain the scoped acceptance judgment through the desk. Report preparation work separately from production work, including any thesis-ready material created.

After this round, choose among resuming with a concrete critical path, trying one specifically justified alternative whose rival outcomes change the decision, or telling Jörn that current evidence does not support the requested autonomous outcome. This is a decision checkpoint, not an automatic requirement to abandon after a fixed number of failures. Do not request an unlimited sequence of new prose judgments without showing how the next judgment changes the choice.

Final PDF acceptance by Jörn is inherently part of the task; it is not a hidden dependency. Continuous human diagnosis during production would be a different operating assumption and must not be smuggled in by extrapolating from interactive preparation. Existing project facts also leave some DS scope and final AI-reflection length unresolved. Surface those only where they materially change the proposed artifact, with the current evidence and a concrete recommendation; do not force unnecessary scope decisions before producing reviewable work.

## Most informative next action and rival observations

After the DS/source checks establish a usable packet, run a frozen-input complete chapter-plus-companions rehearsal through a meaningfully different representation or direct authoring route. Choose a unit with actual revision work and a real dependency boundary, not an already-passing HKO subsection alone. The desk should arrange the smallest review that can judge the resulting unit without concealing defects through selective excerpting.

| Observation | Interpretation | Decision consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Jörn accepts the complete revised unit; sources and companions agree; no editorial rescue; measured path fits remaining scope | Practical workflow capability, with limited transfer evidence | Resume if the remaining technical/coverage inventory also fits; retain a final integration reserve |
| Local prose is comfortable but the chapter still fails on purpose, scope or organization | Local detector/repair progress is not the dominant missing capability | Change chapter planning/authoring operation; do not buy more local reviewers |
| Acceptance requires new human explanations or repeated root rewrites | Interactive editorial assistance works; autonomous ownership remains unshown | Improve the missing preparation/context or explicitly revisit the autonomy expectation |
| Writing succeeds but claims, source state or assembly break | Technical/integration uncertainty was consequential | Resolve the concrete dependency before further detector work |
| No workflow improves the relevant acceptance outcome and no distinct rival predicts a different result | Further preparation currently lacks a decision-relevant bet | Present the negative result and concrete alternatives to Jörn rather than claim readiness |

The existing experiments are valuable precisely because they falsified easy reassurances. The next round should test whether the team can finish a coherent part of the actual thesis under the intended operating conditions, not merely explain why the previous reassuring reviews were wrong.
