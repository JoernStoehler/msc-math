\section{The Rotated-Pentagon Profile}
\label{sec:rotated-regular-polygons}

We now isolate one structured Lagrangian-product family for which the capacity
can be determined exactly.  Let \(P_5\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a centered regular
pentagon of circumradius \(1\), let \(R(\theta)\) denote counterclockwise planar
rotation, and set
\[
  K_\theta=P_5\times_L R(\theta)P_5
  \subset \mathbb R^2_q\oplus\mathbb R^2_p.
\]
Only the relative rotation of the factors matters.

This family contains the Haim--Kislev--Ostrover counterexample.  Their paper
uses the symplectic form \(\sum_i dp_i\wedge dq_i=-\omega_0\), and writes the
second pentagon as \(R(-\pi/2)P_5\).  The convention-changing map
\((q,p)\mapsto(q,-p)\) carries this to \(K_{\pi/2}\) in our convention.  The
simultaneous-reflection symmetry used below also identifies \(K_{\pi/2}\) with
\(K_{-\pi/2}\).  Thus the result below recovers their value
\((3+\sqrt5)/5>1\) \cite[Proposition~1.4]{HaimKislevOstrover2024}, and shows
that their relative angle maximizes the systolic ratio within the entire
one-parameter family.
