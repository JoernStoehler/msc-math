# Review Reader Understanding

Use this reference to test the effect of candidate prose on a thesis reader.
Use `review-thesis-work.md` for the broader review lenses.

Give a fresh reviewer the candidate in its real surrounding context and name
the intended reader. When unprimed interpretation is evidence, do not disclose
the intended diagnosis or coach the reviewer toward the planned explanation.

Ask what the reviewer understood, what questions arose, which dependencies
were assumed, where confidence changed, and what remained ambiguous or
unmotivated. Compare this with the intended reader state; do not merely collect
style preferences or ask whether the prose is clear.

Inspect material effects such as:

- a definition arriving before the reader sees its purpose;
- a proof whose technical steps are visible but whose mechanism is not;
- a qualification that hides the main result;
- an unanswered question that should be acknowledged;
- process or implementation language displacing mathematical explanation;
- surrounding chapters creating expectations the passage does not meet.

These are recurring examples, not a finding whitelist. Report any important
reader effect supported by a concrete location and explanation.

Review rendered pages when typography, equation breaks, theorem placement,
figures, or page competition could change comprehension. A source-only review
does not establish the effect of the final reading surface. Use
`review-figures.md` or `design-tables-listings-displays.md` when the design of
a material figure, table, listing, or other asset is itself part of the
finding.
