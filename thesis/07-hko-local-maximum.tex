\section[HKO Local Maximality]{HKO Local Maximality Among Ten-Facet Polytopes}
\label{sec:hko-local-maximum}

The HKO example is already known to violate Viterbo's predicted bound. The
local question is whether the same ten-facet model contains a nearby
improvement, or whether the HKO point is locally best once translations,
scalings, and linear symplectic changes of coordinates have been quotiented
out. This section proves the latter statement by reducing the geometric local
problem to a finite exact certificate.

Let \(\mathcal K\) be the space of convex bodies in \(\mathbb R^4\), equipped
with the Hausdorff metric, and let \(\mathcal P_{10}\subset\mathcal K\) be the
subspace of convex polytopes with exactly ten facets. Let
\(P_5\subset\mathbb R^2\) be the centered regular pentagon of circumradius
\(1\) with a vertex at
\((1,0)\), and let \(R(\theta)\) denote counterclockwise rotation. The HKO
paper uses the symplectic form \(\sum_i dp_i\wedge dq_i=-\omega_0\) and the
body \(P_5\times_L R(-\pi/2)P_5\).  Changing to our convention sends this
body to \(P_5\times_L R(\pi/2)P_5\).  Let \(D(x,y)=(x,-y)\).  Since
\(D(P_5)=P_5\) and \(D R(\theta)=R(-\theta)D\), the block map
\(D\oplus D\) is symplectic and identifies this body with the representative
\[
  K_{\mathrm{HKO}}=P_5\times_L R(-\pi/2)P_5.
\]
Thus \(K_{\mathrm{HKO}}\) is symplectically equivalent to the HKO
counterexample from \cite[Proposition~1.4]{HaimKislevOstrover2024} in the
convention of this thesis.
In particular, \(0\in\operatorname{int}(K_{\mathrm{HKO}})\). It lies in
\(\mathcal P_{10}\), and the computation in
\cite[Proof of Theorem~1.3]{HaimKislevOstrover2024} gives
\[
  \operatorname{sys}(K_{\mathrm{HKO}})
  =
  \frac{\sqrt{5}+3}{5}
  >1.
\]
The result of this section is local in the ten-facet space. Nearby equality
occurs exactly along the \(G_{\operatorname{sys}}\)-orbit from
Definition~\ref{def:preliminaries-systolic-ratio-symmetries}; all other small
perturbations decrease the systolic ratio.
The restriction to \(\mathcal P_{10}\) is part of the local model: the HKO body
itself has ten facets, and after polarity a Hausdorff-small ten-facet
perturbation is described by a labelled perturbation of its ten dual vertices.
This is the coordinate model in which the exact certificate is verified. Thus
the theorem answers the fixed-facet local-improvement question around the HKO
counterexample, while the broader question with changing facet count remains
outside the theorem.

\begin{theorem}[HKO is locally maximal in the ten-facet space]
\label{thm:hko-ten-facet-local-maximum}
There is a Hausdorff neighborhood \(\mathcal U\) of \(K_{\mathrm{HKO}}\) in
\(\mathcal P_{10}\) such that
\[
  \operatorname{sys}(K)\leq \operatorname{sys}(K_{\mathrm{HKO}})
  \qquad (K\in\mathcal U).
\]
Moreover, after shrinking \(\mathcal U\) if necessary, the equality set is
exactly the part of the \(G_{\operatorname{sys}}\)-orbit of
\(K_{\mathrm{HKO}}\) that remains in \(\mathcal U\):
\[
  \{K\in\mathcal U:
    \operatorname{sys}(K)=\operatorname{sys}(K_{\mathrm{HKO}})\}
  =
  \mathcal U\cap G_{\operatorname{sys}}\cdot K_{\mathrm{HKO}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

The proof route is as follows. First, a Hausdorff-local ten-facet neighborhood
is put into the fixed labelled dual-vertex chart used by the certificate. In
that labelled chart, the \(G_{\operatorname{sys}}\)-directions are split off by
a transverse slice. The
exact certificate then verifies data defining \(26\) smooth feasible sections
and their upper functions for \(\operatorname{sys}\), all touching the HKO value, whose
derivative rows span the quotient cotangent space and have a positive convex
relation summing to zero. The Sage verifier checks this finite exact predicate;
the surrounding hand argument turns it into strict decrease on the slice and
then into the Hausdorff-local theorem.

\begin{certificatecheck}
\textbf{Proof spine.}  At the HKO point, the three reductions are
\[
\begin{gathered}
 K\in\mathcal U
 \quad\longleftrightarrow\quad
 a\in\mathcal V\subset(\mathbb R^4)^{10},
 \\[1mm]
 K=K(a),\qquad
 a_{\mathrm{HKO}}\in\mathcal V,\qquad \dim\mathcal V=40,
 \\[2mm]
 T_{a_{\mathrm{HKO}}}(\mathbb R^4)^{10}
   =T_{\mathrm{orb}}^{\vphantom{*}}\oplus S,
 \qquad \dim T_{\mathrm{orb}}=15,
 \qquad \dim S=25,
 \\[2mm]
 \operatorname{sys}(a_{\mathrm{HKO}}+s)\leq
 U_\sigma(a_{\mathrm{HKO}}+s),
 \\[1mm]
 U_\sigma(a_{\mathrm{HKO}})=\operatorname{sys}(a_{\mathrm{HKO}})
 \quad(\sigma\in\Sigma,\ |\Sigma|=26),
 \\[1mm]
 s\in S,\qquad \lVert s\rVert\text{ sufficiently small}.
\end{gathered}
\]
Here \(\mathcal U\) is a sufficiently small neighborhood from the theorem and
\(\mathcal V\) is its labelled chart.  The first arrow uses polarity and the
unique local labelling of the ten dual vertices; its left side is the
unlabelled Hausdorff-local problem.
On the slice, the rows
\(r_\sigma=DU_\sigma(a_{\mathrm{HKO}})|_S\) span \(S^*\) and satisfy
\[
  \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\lambda_\sigma r_\sigma=0,
  \qquad \lambda_\sigma>0,
  \qquad \sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\lambda_\sigma=1.
\]
Thus every nonzero \(h\in S\) has \(r_\sigma(h)<0\) for at least one selected
upper function.  A uniform Taylor estimate gives strict decrease on the
slice.  The first line and the local symmetry slice then return to the
Hausdorff theorem.  The direct sum displayed here is tangent-space data; it is
not a global linear decomposition of the space of polytopes.
\end{certificatecheck}
