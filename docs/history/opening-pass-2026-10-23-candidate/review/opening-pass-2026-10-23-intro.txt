1     Introduction
Viterbo’s conjecture [Vit00] states that, among convex bodies of fixed volume, the ball has
the largest symplectic capacity. For the Ekeland–Hofer–Zehnder capacity in dimension four it
asserts that every convex body K ⊂ R4 satisfies

The quantity sys(K) is the systolic ratio. It is unchanged by translations, positive dilations and
linear symplectic maps, so it measures shape alone.
    The inequality matters beyond its own statement. The Gromov width satisfies it by
volume preservation, so a violation separates the EHZ capacity from the Gromov width and
refutes the conjecture that all normalized capacities agree on convex domains. For a centrally
symmetric convex body L and its polar L◦ , Artstein–Avidan, Karasev and Ostrover prove
cEHZ (L × L◦ ) = 4 [AKO14], so on these products Viterbo’s inequality is the symmetric Mahler
conjecture.

1.2   Relation to the literature
Abbondandolo, Edtmair and Kang prove that EHZ and cylindrical capacity coincide on convex
bodies in R4 [AEK24, Corollary 1], so every capacity above is a cylindrical capacity. Abbondan-
dolo and Benedetti prove that Zoll contact forms locally maximize the contact systolic ratio,
which includes the volume–capacity inequality near the ball [AB23]; Theorem 7.1 is a local
maximality result at a body with corners and ratio larger than the ball’s. Haim–Kislev studies
hyperplane cuts of KHKO [Hai25]; a small cut adds a facet, whereas our theorem fixes the
number of facets, so local maximality under a change of facet number remains open. Section 7.8
compares these results in detail, and Section 2.3 gives the background on capacities.

1.3   Outline
Sections 2–4 reduce the capacity of a polytope to a finite quadratic program. Section 5 gives the
flow-graph method and Section 6 the first variation. Section 7 proves the two local maximality
theorems; the flow-graph and data chapters are not needed for it. Section 8 reports the
empirical study, and Sections 9–12 treat the product families. Sections 13–16 cover visualization,
numerics, code and data, and the use of AI; Section 17 concludes. The appendices contain the
computations needed to check the results.


2     Preliminaries
A polytope has edges and vertices where the smooth definition of a characteristic no longer
applies. We first extend that definition to convex bodies with nonsmooth boundaries. We then
introduce a variational description of capacity in terms of closed curves that need not lie on the
boundary. This will allow us to modify curves when deriving the finite capacity formula.
    All convex bodies below are compact convex subsets of R4 with non-empty interior. Unless
stated otherwise, the origin lies in the interior.
