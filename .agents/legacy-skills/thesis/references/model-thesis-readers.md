# Model Thesis Readers

Use this reference when choosing explanation, assumed knowledge, emphasis,
notation, visual presentation, or review questions for thesis-facing work.

Model three overlapping reader roles:

- Kai is the primary advisor and a subject expert who can judge the symplectic
  mathematics and thesis-level contribution without needing textbook-level
  expansion of every standard fact.
- Elizabeth is the second advisor and a mathematically expert reader who must
  be able to follow the thesis's conventions, dependencies, and claims without
  project history.
- Future master's students are capable mathematical readers who may reuse the
  results but do not know the repository, experiments, agent history, or why
  the project made its local choices.

Treat these role descriptions as calibration, not complete personal models.
Use observed feedback when available and distinguish it from inference based
on academic role. Do not invent tastes or background knowledge for a named
reader.

Infer the local entering state from what the thesis has actually told the
reader before the passage. The effective reader may know definitions and
conventions introduced earlier, but never knows facts merely because they are
available elsewhere in the repository. Explain project-original arguments,
delicate convention translations, claim limitations, and proof/computation
boundaries at the depth needed to assess and reuse them.

Update the reader model when candidate prose or an unprimed review reveals a
different important question, false inference, or missing prerequisite.

Work in both directions. Given the entering reader state, ask what question
makes the next object or argument worth introducing. Given the intended
explanation, ask what new questions, expectations, or misunderstandings it
creates. Record only distinctions that affect writing; do not force every
paragraph to answer an explicit prewritten question.

A chapter may succeed by making an important limitation or open question
visible when the reader understands why it matters and why the thesis does not
settle it. If a cold reader asks a different important question or fails to ask
the intended one, update both the model and the prose rather than grading the
reader against the plan.
