## 1. Endpoint normalization and the preliminary small-cap bound

Use the moving-facet normalization from Theorem E. Its ten vertices span R^4 linearly, so bounded values of D on those vertices give a uniform bound on its norm. Oscillation one also gives a lower norm bound. Thus d=tD tends to zero precisely at the needed split scale, and a subsequence of normalized directions converges.

For a lower-endpoint sequence, the limiting direction has maximum zero and minimum minus one on F0. Its zero set E is a proper exposed face of F0, with dimension k=0,1,2. Vertices outside the labelled moving E(A) have D-values at most a fixed negative constant. If a convex combination has positive D-value, its coefficient mass on those outside vertices is O(mu), since every remaining vertex has value at most mu. This proves the O(mu) tube estimate even when the directional error is larger than mu. The facet integral is consequently O(mu^(4-k)), including sign-degenerate directions.

The one-slack chart in E5 is uniform in the moving old presentation: simplex vertices of the projected parallel section solve fixed nonsingular systems and move by O(y); their tangential Jacobians are 1+O(y). The cutting error is O(||d|| y), rather than an absolute tilt error. The removed thickness is therefore at most f_+/(1-C||d||), and the resulting relative cap bound is valid at arbitrarily small cap depth. Combining it with monotonicity and the accepted ten-facet deficit gives, on an improving sequence,

    Delta V/V_H = O(t mu^(4-k)),
    r = O(t mu^(4-k)).

For k<=1 the chosen flat-duplicate insertion weight alpha mu gives gain of order t mu^2. Choose alpha sufficiently small before passing to the tail of the sequence. The quadratic cost is at most C alpha^2(t+r)mu^2; the linear old-motion error and old-seed deficit are bounded using r=O(t mu^3). All volume and old-motion contributions are then o(t mu^2), while the insertion coefficient is strictly positive. The contradiction in Section 4 of ENDPOINTS follows.

## 2. Uniform two-slack chart when the edge coefficient shrinks

For k=2 the linear span of E has dimension three: E is an affine two-face contained in a0·x=1. Its annihilator is the line generated by aj-a0. The sign and oscillation give D*=ell(aj-a0), ell>0. Set s=ell t and e=b-[(1-s)a0(A)+s aj(A)]. Then

    ||e||/s -> 0,
    delta/s = mu/ell -> 0,
    r = O(delta^2/s),
    r/s = O((delta/s)^2) -> 0.

The error caused by replacing the base normals with their moving versions is O(tr), hence also absorbed in ||e||/s -> 0. These statements use no comparison between delta and ||e||.

There is a uniform projected-section chart for the two moving slacks. Here is the requisite justification beyond the phrase “the same parametrizations remain available.” The two normals remain independent. In their two-dimensional common tangent kernel, each vertex of the base polygon E(A) is determined by two additional independent active constraints; simplicity leaves all other constraints strictly inactive. Finite active-set enumeration and the fixed inactive gaps give one small bound on |y|, uniform in nearby A, in which precisely those polygon vertices persist. Each vertex is affine in y, smoothly in A. Map a fixed labelled triangulation to these moving vertices. For sufficiently small |y| this is a homeomorphism of the convex polygon onto the projected parallel section and has tangential Jacobian 1+O(|y|). Its displacement is O(|y|), with one common constant. This construction depends on A and y, not on s or e, so its radius does not deteriorate as s tends to zero.

The chart covers the entire removed cap. At vertices in F0 outside E, the b-support gap is at least cs because e=o(s). At vertices outside F0, the old fixed support gap persists and is larger than cs for small s. Thus all old vertices outside E(A) have gap at least cs below the support maximum. For a removed point, its total convex coefficient outside E(A) is at most delta/(cs). Both nonnegative slacks vanish on E(A) and are uniformly bounded on other vertices, proving |y|=O(delta/s). This tends to zero and places every removed point inside the uniform chart. The support maximum itself occurs on E(A) by the same gaps.

With T_A the orthogonal right inverse, x=Phi_A,y(z)-T_A y gives the exact identity

    b·x-1 = f(z)-(1-s)y0-s yj
             +e·[Phi_A,y(z)-z-T_A y].

The final term is bounded by C||e|| |y|. For 0<s<=1/2 and y>=0,

    |y| <= C[(1-s)y0+s yj]/s.

Consequently its relative size is O(||e||/s), regardless of delta/||e||. Removal requires f(z)>0 and lies in the quadrant triangle with slack cost at most f_+/(1-O(||e||/s)). The coarea factor is exactly 1/||a0(A) wedge aj(A)||. Tangential derivatives with respect to y do not add a determinant error: the decomposition into the normal right-inverse directions and the tangent kernel makes the full Jacobian block triangular. Integration gives E14 with relative error

    O(||e||/s + delta/s).

One may extend the containing triangle beyond the cap to upper-bound its area; the Jacobian bound is needed only on actual removed points. There is no absolute O(t^2) volume error.

## 3. Moving quadrature and E16–E17

## 4. Duplicate interchange and symmetry normalization

    t'_n/t_n = osc_F'_n(-D_n) -> 1,
    max_F'_n(-D_n) -> 0,
    mu'_n -> 0.

These are continuity statements at a limiting facet. They do not assert an error bound relative to 1-mu_n, which could be arbitrarily smaller than t_n or the old motion.

## 5. Other-face neighborhoods and the Hausdorff reduction

Multiplicative inclusions between cut bodies with the same extra row give the O(r) comparison uniformly: all bodies contain one common ball and are bounded, and scaling inward preserves the extra inequality. Also |delta-delta0|=O(r), so replacing squared depths costs O(r). Combining this bound with -kappa r+C delta^(4-k) by positive weights proves the full coupled neighborhood for k<=1 because 4-k>2. No raywise assertion is being promoted.

At a fixed open polar edge, its two positive coefficients are bounded below, so the two-slack argument above specializes to the fixed-edge estimate. Nonzero-pairing cancellation closes the regime r=O(delta^2), while monotonicity closes larger r. For zero pairings, keep the fixed-old depth in delta0^2/e. Since delta0=O(e), the insertion weight is uniformly small, the ratio-conversion error is smaller than its gain, and the cap volume is absorbed as e shrinks. Positive combination with -kappa r+C delta0^2+Cr^2 proves the full neighborhood for every r/e ratio. At e=0 the latter estimate alone applies. Thus the inherited open-face theorems include approaching cuts from adjacent faces and nonlinear paths.

Finally, Hausdorff convergence and a common interior ball give polar convergence. Each exposed vertex of the base polar forces a nearby vertex of the approximating polar: maximize a fixed strictly exposing linear functional and use its fixed gap outside a small neighborhood. Choose one in each of ten disjoint neighborhoods. At most one vertex remains. The selected ten rows form a simple old presentation converging to HKO. Otherwise its limit is a duplicate vertex. Genuine eleven-facet presentations require a sign-changing split; endpoint sequences are excluded by the result above. The remaining limiting direction has mu strictly between zero and one and can be handled by a single fixed finite strict certificate at that limit, if the new packet supplies it.
