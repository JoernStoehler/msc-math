# Revise Existing Prose

Use this reference before treating an existing draft as the object to improve.

Ignore sunk writing cost. Read the draft to extract mathematical content,
source clues, useful local explanations, and evidence about both successful
and failed structure. Active status, length, prior review, and writing cost are
evidence about what decisions may need recovering; they do not entitle the
headings, paragraph order, emphasis, or wording to preservation.

Compare at least the plausible routes:

- retain the conceptual spine and revise locally;
- compress or prune aggressively;
- reorganize while salvaging selected passages;
- draft from the reader purpose and primary sources, consulting the old text
  only afterward for omissions.

Judge routes by expected final quality, mathematical risk, and total review
cost. Sentence-level repair is often expensive when the underlying reader path
is wrong. A clean-sheet rewrite can still discard a subtle proof or source
decision that should have been extracted first; recover the decision without
thereby preserving its prose.

When clean comparison is useful, move the old text out of the immediate editing
surface in scratch or use the worktree and Git history as the preserved copy.
Do not create durable `.bak` files by default. Restore individual passages only
because they outperform the new alternative for a named reader purpose.

Review the resulting passage without privileging retained language. The right
question is whether it now serves the thesis, not how much of the previous
draft survived.
