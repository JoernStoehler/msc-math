# Qualifications specialist: development rationale

This draft targets one observed failure: a reviewer can recognize a statement as true and incorrectly infer that its defensive wording helps the reader. It is a candidate prompt, not an evaluated improvement. The development evidence contains only a few relevant labels from two selected passages; it cannot establish a general reader preference against caveats.

The reusable prompt contains no case identifiers or exact source examples. It asks reviewers to separate the positive fact an argument needs from a denial appended to it, then identify the actual local reading need. Its output budget is four concerns and three retained qualifications, with exact quotations and contextual conditions rather than unconditional phrase bans.

## Labeled concerns informing the design

- HKO: “not the reverse bound.” The historical comparison records a human objection to an alternative the reader has not entertained, and a prior specialist's contradictory endorsement of it as necessary. Retain the positive upper bound and the independent source of equality. The denial could become useful if an actual preceding argument or stated reader question had confused the bound's direction.
- DS: “This is an analytic example outside the finite random table, so its value above one does not conflict with that table's recorded maximum.” The human judged this aside out of place. The finite sample and analytic family have already been described separately. A section explicitly comparing the sample's coverage with a known example could motivate this distinction; mathematical correctness alone does not motivate it here.
- HKO: “without floating hints, branch search, or printing 1040 expanded field entries.” The historical record identifies implementation advertisement. Retain what the formulas specify. A computational-method comparison addressing numerical reliability or representation size could give the avoided work a purpose.

## Qualifications the design should preserve

These are development judgments except where human support is expressly stated; unmarked passages are not human-approved negatives.

- “Small ridge sum is therefore not a universal ascent objective.” The human calls this a nice result. The immediately preceding opposing paths supply its mathematical content; removing it because it is negative would erase a finding.
- “An invertible five-column minor makes this solution smooth, and strict positivity of the base weights makes positivity persist nearby.” These conditions explain why the feasible continuation exists. They are part of the construction, not anticipatory defense.
- “These are historical numerical evaluator observations: the original computation records do not establish that they satisfy the guarantees of the current certified capacity implementation.” This limits the evidential status of reported numbers. Its length, terminology, or placement can need revision while its distinction remains necessary; the human's placement objection does not authorize portraying the numbers as certified.

## Limits and next test

The main overcorrection risk is deleting scope conditions, negative results, computational attribution, or proof dependencies because they resemble defensive prose. The retained-qualification task checks the reviewer's discrimination without treating three endorsements as a required quota. Missing surrounding context must condition a concern rather than become a fabricated reader misconception.

Only the authorized development source, development labels, historical comparison, and topic-transfer extraction informed these examples. The governing harness-engineering-v2 skill was also read. No held-out source, classification output, or additional case data was consulted. No evaluation was run. A later frozen test must distinguish removal of distracting wording from loss of necessary mathematical information.
