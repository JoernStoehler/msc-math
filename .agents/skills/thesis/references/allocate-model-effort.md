# Allocate Model Effort

Use this reference to choose a model and reasoning effort for writing-related
subtasks. Treat all routing guidance as a prior to test, not a fixed model map.

Spend the strongest writing model on work where explanation quality and
mathematical organization are inseparable: choosing a proof spine, resolving
expository logic, writing a bounded publication-facing passage, or
restructuring prose around a better reader path. Do not spend that budget on
broad file discovery, mechanical extraction, build repair, or generic
summarization.

Use faster models or explorers for bounded source location, exact passage
identification, dependency mapping, contradiction searches, and initial cold
reader review. Their output should route the writer to sources rather than
replace those sources with an authoritative synthesis.

Current local evidence does not establish a fixed model or effort map for
thesis writing. Treat Jörn's observation that earlier agents often produced
poor thesis prose as a reason to pilot and review, not as evidence that a
particular replacement will work.

Before a large run, test the proposed writer on a representative bounded unit
whose quality exercises the real difficulty. Compare reusable artifacts and
review cost, not fluency, completion length, or the model's description of its
own process. Update this prior through `learn-from-writing-trials.md`.
