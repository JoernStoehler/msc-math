# Message review design notes

On 8 October 2026 Jörn proposed making the communication guidance actionable by requiring prepared, non-synchronous messages to identify a separate subagent that approved their exact text, with a prepared checklist/workflow for non-mechanical violations. He authorized making the guidance actionable; this package implements that local request without changing separately governed Codex configuration.

The immediate incident: the audit root reviewed an artifact's factual claims but delivered an activity-oriented chat answer, then offered corrections to an assessment Jörn had not accepted. The gate reviews the outgoing text in the actual request/steering context, not its report alone. The rejected audit remains evidence, not user acceptance. Its private explanatory claims should not be promoted into proven causal diagnoses.

Exact text includes the reviewer attribution. Unicode NFC, CRLF and whitespace variation are accepted; punctuation/word changes require renewed review. Context hashes and relevant-steering invalidation prevent reuse after the decision changes. Coordinator cross-check against native tool identity is required because a JSON receipt or Python script cannot authenticate its author.

Synchronous conversation is exempt to preserve useful direct interaction. It is defined by the interaction and deliverable, not by the channel or presence of a latest user question. Reports/progress messages remain covered. Misclassification that exempts reports is a known risk; so is unnecessary review latency for actual dialogue. Native private reviewer returns are exempt to avoid infinite recursion.

The initial implementation is an instruction gate and portable verifier. Pre-delivery runtime interception, reviewer quality, reliable selection/application and walltime benefit need separate evidence. Stop hooks after streaming cannot establish pre-delivery enforcement. No host/project config edit or global rollout is implied. Evaluate exact-text mechanics separately from whether reviewers catch relevance, attention, scope and continuation defects.

## Context-first reviewer prompt, 8 October 2026

Jörn objected that the initial relevance checklist assumed a request and unnecessarily defined an ordinary word. The root acknowledged this but delayed delivering a usable replacement; he then explicitly requested the prompt/template. The substantive workflow now starts from the actual conversation and examines the sender's interpretation, including unsettled goals and exploratory contributions. Review lenses do not prescribe a universal interaction model. Historical failure examples remain evidence, not definitions of relevance or mandatory contents of every message.

The receipt's existing keys are retained for mechanical compatibility and explicitly separated from situational review criteria. This avoids invalidating retained approvals while allowing reviews of exploratory messages and consequential concerns outside those fields. The sender must supply actual conversation rather than only its preferred summary. Independence and small application probes do not establish reliable future selection or review quality.

Jörn then asked whether review is one inference or requires inherited messages, logs or repo retrieval. The initial implementation did use reviewer read/write tool rounds; its final reviewer also reran already-passed tests. The revised default supplies prompt, candidate and pertinent verbatim turns inline to a fresh reviewer, asking for a single no-tool response when sufficient. Sender-computed hashes and verbatim storage of the reviewer-authored return remove compulsory filesystem tool rounds. Retrieval remains conditional on consequential missing context; no measured token/latency improvement or guaranteed native internal call count is claimed.

After reading AGENTS.md, Jörn challenged its growth against harness-engineering. The added 29-line review section duplicated the conditional workflow and enforcement discussion. It is replaced by a seven-line invariant/trigger, with exemption details retained in this skill. This preserves the selected gate and its uncertainty/unavailable-review boundaries without requiring all task consumers to load the procedure in root instructions. Other project-wide guidance is preserved; size alone is not treated as evidence of a skill violation or a mandate for a broad rewrite.

Jörn also challenged the schema/hashes as costly constraints that could degrade review. Their intended benefit was exact-text consistency; the implementation did not enforce delivery and had no measured net benefit. The active return now names the frozen candidate and gives approval/rejection with substantive findings. No fixed checklist fields, JSON, hashes or repeated candidate text are required. Native identity and exact version approval remain mandatory; previous verifier/source and receipts are retained as historical evidence. This reduces required output/format work but does not establish improved judgment or reliable adherence.

Jörn proposed that sender and reviewer read the same skill stating what the message must fulfill; the reviewer then receives the message/file, skill and read-only review assignment. The separate inline prompt template and repeated procedural descriptions are replaced by one shared standard with role-specific actions. The good context-first prompt's substantive requirements are preserved, while exact-text/version attribution, true exemptions, unavailable-review handling and non-enforcement remain explicit. Read-only is not advertised as sandbox confinement on a runtime where only the assignment restricts operations.
