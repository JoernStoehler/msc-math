\subsection{Numerical error bound validation}
\label{sec:q-error}

All experiments in this chapter depend on the numerical KKT solver
producing accurate capacity values.
Lemma~\ref{lem:q-error-bound} provides a proven error bound~$E$ on
the corrected capacity value~$\tilde{Q}$, computed from the KKT
residual.
We validate this bound with two complementary checks on the 7~known
polytopes with $F \le 10$.

\paragraph{Error bound sweep.}
For every combinatorial type~$(S,\sigma)$ across all 7~polytopes,
we solve the KKT system and verify that the error bound~$E$ and
the residual correction both remain below~$10^{-6}$.
Table~\ref{tab:q-error-sweep} reports the worst case per polytope.
The largest error bound observed is $E = 2.9 \times 10^{-11}$
(HK-O pentagon), well below the~$10^{-6}$ threshold.

\paragraph{Exact rational comparison.}
For the capacity-winning node of each polytope, we solve the KKT system
exactly over~$\mathbb{Q}$ via Gaussian elimination with arbitrary-precision
rationals and compare the exact capacity~$Q_{\mathrm{exact}}$ against the
f64 result~$\tilde{Q}$.
Table~\ref{tab:q-error-exact} shows that the actual numerical
errors are at machine epsilon (${\sim}10^{-16}$), confirming
that the solver introduces no algorithmic error beyond f64
rounding.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrllllc}
\toprule

\midrule

Hypercube            & 4 & 0.1250 & 0.1250 & $0$                & $1.2\times10^{-29}$ & OK \\
HK-O pentagon        & 7 & 0.1453 & 0.1453 & $8.3\times10^{-17}$ & $1.1\times10^{-29}$ & OK \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Exact rational comparison on capacity-winning nodes.
  $m$~is the orbit length.
  The symplectic $\triangle{\times}\triangle$ winning node has a
  singular KKT matrix and is omitted.
  The assertion $|\tilde{Q} - Q_{\mathrm{exact}}| \le \max(E,\, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}})$
  passed for all 6~solvable polytopes.}
\label{tab:q-error-exact}
\end{table}

The two checks are complementary: the sweep confirms that the
proven error bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:q-error-bound}) is never violated
across over 1.1~million nodes in practice, while the exact comparison
confirms that the f64 solver introduces no error beyond machine
precision at the capacity-winning nodes that determine the final result.
