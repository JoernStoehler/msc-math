# Thesis review package

**Reviewed document:** *Probing Viterbo’s Conjecture*, the supplied 86-page PDF.

Start with **`01_examiner_report.md`** for the assessment, grading dimensions, major structural problems and proposed reorganization. **`02_anchored_findings.md`** contains 35 detailed findings with locations, status, explanation and repairs, followed by three separately labelled verification/hardening questions. **`03_mathematical_audit.md`** records the independent checks and supplies two useful mathematical additions: an analytic proof of the rotated-pentagon profile and an exact ridge–capacity law across the sys=1 threshold. **`04_sources_and_scope.md`** identifies the primary sources and states the limits of the review.

**`annotated_thesis.pdf`** preserves the original pages and numbering. Its highlights and margin comments carry the same finding identifiers. PDF comment visibility varies by viewer; the Markdown register is the authoritative complete text of the findings.

The **`verification/`** directory contains reviewer-produced exact HKO verification, supporting diagnostics, input data, logs and environment versions. These checks corroborate the main certificate; they are not a claim that the entire thesis repository or all historical experiments were independently reproduced.

## Main assessment

The HKO theorem’s printed finite certificate passed independent exact reconstruction. The most important problems are the inaccurate/currently incomplete literature account, the missing connection between the ridge descriptor and the pentagon profile, avoidable proof machinery, the empirical chapter’s organization and evidential quality, the incomplete release, and the absence of attested final author review of the current candidate. The review does not equate these issues with a refutation of the main theorem.

Major, substantial and local priorities distinguish effort and impact. Factual errors, methodological limitations and editorial judgments are labelled separately. The three Q-items are not counted as demonstrated theorem failures. Related findings should not be treated as independent grading penalties.
