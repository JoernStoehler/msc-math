# Six-dimensional golden simplex: bounded primary-source check

Checked 28 October 2026, 23:22 UTC. This assesses the exact expression and strict local theorem, not whether a six-simplex beating the standard simplex was previously known.

**Haim-Kislev 2019, MSc thesis, Remark 1.3, pp. 4–5.** Already gives a numerical six-simplex beating the standard simplex, with explicit decimal vertex coordinates, capacity about 1.5143, volume about 1, and unnormalized ratio about 3.4725 (standard 10/3). Dividing this convention by 6 gives a ratio about 0.57875. It does not identify the golden expression `(945+75 sqrt(5))/1922` or prove strict local maximality of that example in the inspected passage. The remaining simplex sections (§3–4, pp. 10–34) concern the explicit closed characteristic formula and the four-dimensional bound; targeted full-text searches find no golden expression, hypergraph, or local-maximality assertion. Primary PDF: https://tau.alma.exlibrisgroup.com/view/delivery/972TAU_INST/9932955590304146 ; inspected local text `/tmp/msc-math/restart/frontier-sources/simplex-novelty/hk-msc-2019.txt`, particularly lines 206–245. The numerical phenomenon is unequivocally prior art.

**Leipold 2026, dissertation, §12.4–12.5, pp. 122–125, Table 1 and appendix p. 129.** Reports root-normalized six-dimensional value 0.8334409621 and capacity 0.1689718761, compared to Haim-Kislev's earlier 0.8333555549629893. Her reference [45] is the 2019 MSc thesis. The numerical output is described as approximately Clarke-stationary, with slight further improvements potentially possible. §12.5 discusses why branch Hessians do not explain the observed local maximality mechanism and proposes a second-order framework. The inspected text does not state the exact golden value 0.833442350445 or prove strict local maximality of the reported simplex. This provides strong directly relevant evidence that our exact face/Hessian/normal-cone mechanism goes beyond the stated numerical result; it does not certify priority. Source: https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/80566/1/Dissertation_karla_leipold_.pdf ; local extraction `leipold.txt`, lines 5093–5225 and appendix matrix near 5300.

**Haim-Kislev 2025, Dynamical extensions of Zoll to nonsmooth convex bodies, Example 1.10.** Attributes the four-dimensional simplex maximum to HK2019 and says standard-simplex maximality no longer holds in higher dimensions. It does not give an exact six-dimensional optimizer or a simplex strict-local-maximum theorem. Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16644v1#S1.SS3 , especially Example 1.10. This paper concerns generalized Zoll and cuts properties, not the specific affine simplex optimization proved here.

## Calibrated conclusion

The fact that a six-simplex beats the standard simplex is old, explicit numerical prior art. The particular neighborhood of our candidate is represented by Leipold's best numerical output. The exact golden expression is independently already the classical hypergraph F32 Lagrangian (Baber–Talbot); that constant alone is not new. In these primary sources, I found no exact realization of that constant as a six-simplex systolic ratio and no proof of strict local maximality against every affine simplex deformation. Those are therefore candidate contributions supported by the present bounded comparison, not a priority guarantee. A safe manuscript formulation is “an exact structural realization of the numerically observed optimizer, with a proof of strict local maximality.” No global bound or equality classification in dimension six has been proved here.
