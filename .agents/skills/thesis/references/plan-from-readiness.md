# Plan From Readiness

Use this reference to assess whether substantial prose is ready for its next
real consumer, or to plan backward from an expensive review or submission
gate.

Readiness is a contextual judgment, not a whitelist, proof of completion, or
low-effort property test. Begin with the actual reader, decision, and reading
surface. Use `review-thesis-work.md` as incomplete reminders, then
interpret them against the passage's mathematical burden, source status,
surrounding chapters, and known ways similar work has failed.

Identify the real costly gate, the decision it informs, and the failures it is
likely to expose. Remove cheaper prerequisite failures in dependency order:
source and correctness problems before prose polish, and local reader-path
problems before scarce advisor attention. Rehearse the gate as faithfully as
is economical by reviewing the candidate in context and, when material, in
the rendered PDF. Reserve the actual gate until the candidate is plausibly
ready for its decision.

In the rehearsal, ask whether important reader questions are answered to a
justified degree, including questions the thesis must acknowledge without
answering. Check whether explanatory placement and dependencies work across
chapter boundaries, not only whether the local passage reads smoothly.

Plan backward from the expected findings and repair cost. A plan that ends
with first-pass generation and assumes a style or reader review will pass has
not covered the writing risk. Pilot the hardest representative passage early,
subject it to the closest economical rehearsal of the intended gate, and
preserve enough budget and source context to rewrite it if the result is not a
useful new starting point.

Record chapter-specific readiness judgments, unresolved findings, and reopen
conditions in the relevant companion or review artifact. Preserve a past failure
durably only when it corrects a prior that future work is otherwise likely to
repeat; state what happened and the scope of the lesson rather than inventing
a universal prohibition.

A passing gate is evidence for the reading surface and failure modes it
actually exercised. It is necessary evidence when the task depends on it, not
a sufficient certificate that the chapter or thesis is done.
