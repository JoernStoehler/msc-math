\section[HKO Local Maximality]{Local maximality of the HKO pentagon product}
\label{sec:hko-local-maximum}
Let $P$ be the regular pentagon of circumradius one, centred at the origin
and with vertex $(1,0)$. In coordinates $(q_1,q_2,p_1,p_2)$ put
$K_0=P\times R(-\pi/2)P$, with symplectic form
$\omega_0=\sum_j dq_j\wedge dp_j$.
The Haim--Kislev--Ostrover capacity formula~\cite{HaimKislevOstrover2024} for this body gives
\[
 c_{\rm EHZ}(K_0)=2\cos(\pi/10)(1+\cos(\pi/5)),\qquad
 \operatorname{sys}(K_0)=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{5}>1.
\]
Here $\operatorname{sys}(K)=c_{\rm EHZ}(K)^2/(2\operatorname{vol}_4 K)$.
The counterexample itself is due to Haim--Kislev and Ostrover; the result
proved here concerns its local maximality when the number of facets stays ten.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hko-ten-facet-local-maximum}
There is a Hausdorff neighborhood $\mathcal U$ of $K_0$ among convex
four-dimensional polytopes with exactly ten facets such that
$\operatorname{sys}(K)\leq\operatorname{sys}(K_0)$ for $K\in\mathcal U$.
Equality holds precisely for those bodies in $\mathcal U$ obtained from
$K_0$ by translation, positive dilation and a linear symplectic map.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A second ten-facet local maximum}
\label{subsec:non-hko-local-maximum}

The HKO body is not the only local maximum in the ten-facet space. Consider
the row-normal presentation
\[
A_* =
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2&0&0\\
12/7&4/7&0&0\\
12/7&-4/7&0&0\\
0&-2&0&0\\
-12/7&-4/7&0&0\\
-12/7&4/7&0&0\\
0&0&4/3&2\\
0&0&-4&2\\
0&0&-4&-2\\
0&0&4/3&-2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P_*:=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:A_*x\leq\mathbf1\}.
\]
The first six rows define a hexagon and the last four a quadrilateral in
complementary Lagrangian planes. The exact certificate proves the following.

\begin{theorem}[A non-HKO ten-facet local maximum]
\label{thm:non-hko-ten-facet-local-maximum}
The polytope $P_*$ is simple, has ten genuine facets and 24 vertices, and
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_4(P_*)=\frac12,
 \qquad c_{\rm EHZ}(P_*)=1,
 \qquad \operatorname{sys}(P_*)=1.
\]
There is a Hausdorff neighborhood $\mathcal V$ of $P_*$ among convex
four-dimensional polytopes with exactly ten genuine facets such that
\[
 \operatorname{sys}(P)\leq1\qquad(P\in\mathcal V).
\]
The maximum is non-strict. Locally, after quotienting by translations,
positive dilations and linear symplectic maps, equality is the saturation of
the two-parameter product family described below.
\end{theorem}

For completeness, write
\[
 u=1-v+v^2,\quad a=\frac12-s,\quad b=\frac12+s,\quad t=v-\frac12,
\]
and let $E(v,s)=H(v,s)\times T(v)$, where
\[
\begin{split}
H(v,s)&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-a,\tfrac12),(a,\tfrac12),(b,t),
 (a,-\tfrac12),(-a,-\tfrac12),(-b,-t)\},\\
T(v)&=\operatorname{conv}\{(u,0),(0,v),(u-1,0),(0,v-1)\}.
\end{split}
\]
At $(v,s)=(1/2,1/12)$ this is $P_*$. The areas are identically $1$ and
$1/2$, and exact continuation of all product closure cases gives
\(c_{\rm EHZ}(E(v,s))=1\) and $\operatorname{sys}(E(v,s))=1$ for parameters
sufficiently close to the base point.

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch and trust boundary]
For a word with positive feasible HK weights and objective $Q>0$, the
Haim--Kislev formula gives the upper bound
\[
 \operatorname{sys}(P(A))\leq
 U(A):=\frac{1}{8\,\operatorname{vol}_4(P(A))\,Q(A)^2}.
\]
Thus the selected feasible sections are upper bounds for the actual ratio;
they are not lower bounds and need not remain optimizing away from the base.
On the product family, the Lagrangian splitting reduces the complete capacity
calculation to the vertices of two planar closure polytopes. Enumerating all
624 cyclic orders gives $Q_{\max}=1/2$, 12 tight cases, and a strict gap
of $1/14$ for every other case. Exact rational-function continuation of the
closure supports and the tight cases proves the capacity and equality claims
on $E(v,s)$.

For arbitrary ten-facet perturbations, 24 positive feasible sections are
continued through the full normal chart. Their gradient bank has rank 22 and
has a positive normalized relation whose smallest coefficient is
$3/36617$. The symmetry tangent space has rank 15; the two parameters of
$E(v,s)$ add two directions, and one remaining transverse direction $W$
is checked by an exact rational-function identity. Along it,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_4(P(A_*+zW))=\frac12+\frac{13}{1728}z^2,
 \qquad Q_j(z)=\frac12,
 \qquad U_j''(0)=-\frac{13}{432}
\]
for every selected section. The last displayed number is the curvature of
the feasible upper sections, not an asserted second derivative of the actual
capacity.

The positive relation forces a uniform linear decrease in the 22 sharp
coordinates. The negative quadratic term controls $W$, and a uniform Taylor
estimate absorbs the mixed terms. The inverse function theorem, using the
full rank-40 symmetry-and-slice chart, then gives a genuine neighborhood in
the Hausdorff space of ten-facet polytopes. This proves the theorem. The exact
verifier and an independent direct differentiation check are retained at
\path{experiments/hko-local-maximum/non-hko/}; exact arithmetic checks the
finite predicates, while the displayed analytic argument supplies their
geometric implication.
\end{proof}

This result is deliberately narrower than a global theorem. It does not
prove local maximality when facets are added, uniqueness among all ten-facet
polytopes, or any new violation of Viterbo's inequality. Since HKO has 25
vertices and ratio $(3+\sqrt5)/5$, while $P_*$ has 24 vertices and ratio one,
the two bodies are not related by any invertible affine map, in particular not
by a symplectic or anti-symplectic symmetry.
