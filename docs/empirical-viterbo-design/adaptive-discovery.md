# Adaptive empirical discovery: geometry, descriptors, questions and data evolve together

Prepared 2026-09-18. This is a scientific design proposal, not an executed experiment or a literature survey. No new numerical claim or expensive computation is made here. The production budget remains paused.

## The useful object is a discovery process, not a fixed prediction table

A broad heterogeneous table is a starting collection of examples. It lets us notice unexpected distinctions, generate candidate explanations, and identify regions where existing descriptions fail. It need not remain the sampling distribution, feature space, or learning problem. The next useful object can be a deliberately designed pair of polytopes, a controlled path, an exact calculation, a new inexpensive descriptor, or a theorem question. Predicting sys more accurately is one intermediate objective, not the definition of scientific progress.

A suitable loop is:

1. Choose a scientific question and inspect relevant existing examples, including equality/positive controls and counterexamples to proposed explanations.
2. Record several live explanations and predictions that differ between them.
3. Identify the least expensive additional information that separates those predictions. This may be a new feature on old rows rather than a new body, or mathematics rather than computation.
4. Acquire it, retaining generation and selection provenance, unavailable computations, evaluator status, and costs.
5. Revise the representation or question. If a regularity survives, seek a conjecture with a precise domain and attack it with deliberately adverse examples.
6. For a claimed empirical generalization, freeze the rule and acquire a fresh relevant test. For a theorem, supply a proof; repeated survival does not become proof.

A campaign may succeed by finding that an appealing explanation is wrong, identifying a geometric obstruction, discovering a formula on a natural family, or exposing a parameterization blind spot. A new maximum is not required.

## Campaign A: find the geometry hidden by an insufficient descriptor

**Question.** Which geometric differences explain why low ridge magnitude is useful in a population but can fail along controlled paths?

**Starting material.** The regular-pentagon family with its exact identity, the two retained reversal paths, and pairs of retained product bodies in the same polygon-count bucket with similar ridge sums but substantially different sys. Historical sys is sufficient for exploratory selection; consequential pairs require current evaluation if available geometry and cost permit.

**Cheap pilot.** First re-read the reversal-path sources and identify the actual changing geometric quantities. Then select a handful of feature-collision pairs rather than thousands of fresh random examples. Initially inspect ridge-area distributions and existing volume-free concentration summaries. Plot known mathematical paths alongside those pairs, not a pooled regression alone.

**Possible next representations.** Retain which two-face areas meet along incidences, rather than aggregating all areas into moments; study concentration by incident facet; compare shape/difference-body information in product factors; separate branch-action class information from target-free descriptors. Each is a hypothesis, not a guaranteed new invariant. Check whether existing packets already implement it.

**Branching outcomes.**

- One inexpensive descriptor separates the collisions and makes the opposing paths intelligible: freeze a relation and test deliberately chosen additional paths. It may become a useful conjecture or search filter.
- The descriptor distinguishes populations but not within-family collisions: report the mixture limitation and ask whether a family-specific statement is mathematically natural.
- Only post-capacity orbit information explains the difference: valuable explanatory science remains possible, but do not advertise an inexpensive candidate proposer.
- No stable pattern: establish a concrete insufficiency result for the proposed summaries, with representative counterexamples; stop adding cosmetic models to that representation.

**Cost/dependencies.** Pair selection from existing columns is small; geometry access and new face features may dominate. Expensive capacity calls should be reserved for informative pairs. The exact pentagon identity itself needs no new capacity run.

## Campaign B: interventions that discriminate mathematical mechanisms

**Question.** What operation changes capacity, volume, and the competing orbit branches in the way an empirical association suggests?

A controlled geometric operation offers more than adding controls to a regression. Examples worth screening against existing packets are changing selected supports with normals fixed, perturbing factor shapes while preserving prescribed constraints, and relative orientation in product constructions. Their relevance depends on the mathematical question; not every parameter variation is an intervention on a single feature.

**Cheap pilot.** Take one body with an interpretable winning orbit and one nearby competitor, then a short one-dimensional support path. Track volume, candidate actions, winner changes, ridge statistics, feasibility/combinatorial changes, and evaluator availability. Use a path with known exact structure as a positive control where possible. Do not silently identify a locally retained orbit with the true minimizer along the whole path.

**Branching outcomes.**

- Improvement occurs while the same certified minimizing branch persists: derive its local formula and ask for a geometric explanation or inequality.
- A tempting branch loses to an overlooked competitor: the competition is the result; invent a descriptor for the obstruction rather than optimizing the wrong branch harder.
- Capacity stays fixed while volume changes: this motivates a precise constrained deformation question, not a causal interpretation of the old regression.
- The effect depends on combinatorial transitions: study the transition itself rather than average over it.

**Cost/dependencies.** This is cheap only when evaluators expose the needed branch witnesses. A pilot should first test observability and sample runtime. Effective constraint rank/free deformation directions may require a small mathematical derivation. The old equality of active-facet counts means counting alone is not enough.

## Campaign C: map which discoveries depend on how examples are generated

**Question.** Which observed regularities reflect geometry, which reflect the sampling construction, and where does the current representation fail to cover interesting bodies?

The existing heterogeneous set is not a uniform sample of 'all polytopes.' Varying a generator is a scientific experiment, not merely more rows. Candidate contrasts include support-height variability, fixed-normal versus varying-normal generation, products versus nonproducts at comparable complexity, and controlled perturbations of special bodies versus independently generated bodies. Existing alternative-source experiments must be read before selecting a new contrast.

**Cheap pilot.** Freeze one scientifically interpretable finding, then choose two generator changes that should discriminate its rival explanations. Generate a small balanced batch with independent seeds and a few interpretable controls. Benchmark one representative geometry from each anticipated cost class first; use staged acquisition if capacities are expensive.

**Branching outcomes.**

- Association survives but quantitative calibration shifts: formulate a robust ordering claim, not an unchanged regression law.
- It disappears when source labels or count buckets cease to distinguish targets: narrow the discovery to a population-composition fact.
- A new source makes the descriptor useless but yields a new clean relation: evolve the question and feature set rather than call this total failure.
- Failures/timeouts concentrate in a geometric region: coverage is unresolved there; report it rather than omit those bodies and generalize from survivors.

**Cost/dependencies.** New source code can be more expensive than additional sampling. Prefer existing generators when they produce the contrast of interest; do not privilege them if they cannot test it. No universal sample size is justified before seeing computational cost and effect resolution.

## Campaign D: let feature invention respond to explanatory failures

**Question.** What information is absent from the current summaries, and can a small mathematical feature recover it?

A practical feature search is not 'calculate every candidate on every row.' Maintain a registry of definitions, intended symmetries, cost, needed geometry, numerical failure states, and whether the quantity needs a capacity evaluation. Start with a few diagnostic examples; reject features that are undefined, representation-dependent in an unintended way, redundant with known controls, or unable to distinguish those examples. Only then extend computation to a larger relevant panel.

Potential families include weighted face-incidence information, factor duality/difference-body relations, and orbit-obstruction quantities. These are directions for mathematical formulation, not claims that their obvious formulas are already valid or cheap. Covariance of coordinate features can also be useful experimentally if its representation dependence is explicit; the active invariant-only table would require a separately documented exploratory surface rather than silently accepting such columns.

**Cheap pilot.** Choose one concrete collision from campaign A and formulate two mathematically distinct descriptors. Prove/check the claimed affine-symplectic/scaling/translation behavior, test numerical implementations on equivalent representatives, then compute only on the selected pairs and known paths. Repeat on a new pair before expanding.

**Branching outcomes.** A representation artifact directs attention to the measurement contract; a true but redundant descriptor avoids wasted compute; a genuinely discriminating descriptor warrants a new conjecture and prospective test. All three are informative.

**Terminology.** Face-lattice summaries and face-based symplectic area summaries are invariants of the relevant affine-symplectic action on polytopes, with the stated scale normalization. Do not imply they are invariants under arbitrary nonlinear symplectomorphisms, which need not preserve the polytope face structure. An algorithm's orbit labels are yet another kind of object. Preserve these distinctions in the dataset and prose.

## Campaign E: discover small families where a theorem could emerge

**Question.** Can numerical regularity expose a low-dimensional family with a tractable capacity formula or inequality?

Search for more than large sys: piecewise elementary behavior, stable minimizing words, branch crossings with a simple symmetry, a constant product of geometric quantities, or sharpness at recognizable shapes. The pentagon formula is evidence that this type of result can be valuable, not evidence that another formula is easy to obtain.

**Cheap pilot.** Before acquiring data, inspect existing path and orbit records for such structure. Pick one mathematically natural parameter family, compute a coarse grid with witness information, and try to derive the observed expression from the finite formulation. Use additional points to falsify candidate formulas; do not use symbolic regression fit as a proof. If a cheap symbolic derivation determines the whole family, stop sampling and prove it.

**Branching outcomes.** A stable candidate word provides an upper bound but not automatically equality; the remaining scientific task is excluding competitors. Frequent unexplained switches suggest a different family or a theorem about branch competition. A formula that only holds on a special locus may still be an interesting result if that restriction has geometric meaning.

## Acquisition and validation protocol shared by all campaigns

Keep three layers separate:

- **Discovery archive:** every available example and adaptive exploratory query, with its history. It is allowed to evolve freely.
- **Prospective batches:** frozen predictions, generator and evaluation plan, untouched until tested. After use, they may join discovery, but a revised prediction needs another fresh test if an independent confirmation claim is made.
- **Exact examples/proofs:** known geometric controls and mathematical arguments, with proof status independent of empirical counts.

Split related objects by base body or construction lineage. Randomly distributing rotations, perturbations, or optimizer iterates of one body over train/test sets can reward recognition of that body rather than transfer. Keep general symplectic images as equivariance/implementation checks, not independent scientific observations of a new systolic value.

Record why each geometry and feature was acquired. Targeted sampling can estimate performance under that specified selection protocol; it does not by itself estimate rarity in the original population. Missing expensive features are selection-dependent unless sampled otherwise. Model disagreement can prioritize questions, but several models using the same information can agree for the same wrong reason; reserve a small independent geometric exploration stream if pursuing an adaptive program.

Cache immutable geometry and measurements keyed by geometry, evaluator/version/configuration, feature definition, and actual numerical status. Do not mix old target values and new certified routes under a single unlabeled target. On-demand computation saves cost only if failures, retries, and feature/evaluator changes remain visible.

The cheapest checks should be used as filters only with a stated role. If a feature is itself almost as expensive as capacity, its value may be explanation rather than screening. Budget generator time, feature time, capacity time, failed evaluations, analysis, proof follow-up and writing separately.

## First action recommendation

Run a bounded **collision-and-path explanation pilot** before building general adaptive-search infrastructure. It uses the broad dataset, existing reversal paths and new mathematical interpretation together; it can demonstrate whether representation changes and targeted acquisition yield useful knowledge. In parallel, inventory prospective source-transfer results and candidate-action observability to prepare campaigns C and B. Do not launch a full new table or model tournament merely to make this process look systematic.

This is a choice for information value and existing dependencies, not a claim that the thesis must revolve around ridge area. If a broader scan reveals an equally tractable, more consequential question, the campaign should change. The parent portfolio should retain independent lines for other empirical approaches so this pilot does not become a monopoly on DS work.
