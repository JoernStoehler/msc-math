## Concrete reader and inference obstacles

1. **The reported transfer benefit cannot be attributed to a specified decision rule.** The decisive span is “a frozen ridge selector increased mean target relative to disjoint controls.” Earlier, the first screening experiment only says “applied geometric selection rules”; the refinement experiment does specify its rule. I cannot tell whether the transfer selector uses only low total ridge area, the concentration refinement, or a fitted multifeature model. This matters to the concluding inference, “In sampled product populations it can help allocate capacity evaluations”: the reader cannot identify which usable rule received this comparative support. The controls are also not described as randomly chosen or otherwise matched, so the practical comparison represented by their mean is unclear.

2. **The changed proposal source is described too indirectly to interpret the transfer.** The spans are “using a separately area-normalized construction” and “This tests transfer to one named alternative source.” No source is actually named here, and the original product generation is described only through “seed 42 and support heights in [0.8,1.2]” plus construction acceptance. In the displayed product formula, independent positive rescaling of the factors leaves the descriptor unchanged. Consequently, area normalization alone does not explain to this reader what geometrically different population was tested. I understand the restriction to one alternative population but cannot identify the change that makes this a transfer test.

3. **The status of later numerical targets is not explicit.** The initial caveat says “The targets in this table are historical numerical estimates” and “Their original computation records do not establish the guarantees of the current capacity implementation.” Later experiments return to “largest stored target,” “mean target,” and “91 distinct targets,” without specifying whether these share that caveat or use a different evaluation procedure. The qualitative experimental story is intelligible, but I cannot determine whether subsequent selection benefits are verified capacity comparisons or benefits against the same historically uncertain numerical labels. The distinction is material because the section expressly introduces it at the outset.

4. **The refinement criterion’s provenance is asserted without an identifiable source.** “Before target evaluation, the criterion was fixed” gives the result a prospective interpretation. The actual criterion and observed outcome are clear, including the lack of a significance test. But the section gives no experiment name, record, or reference by which a reader could locate that prior criterion or the bucket outcomes. For a thesis argument relying on the difference between retrospective pattern finding and prospective selection, this leaves an important evidential step unavailable to the reader of this section.
