\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Why the competitor list is exhaustive.}
It remains to prove that no branch has smaller action.  By
Theorem~\ref{thm:lagrangian-product-finite-enumeration}, for every \(\theta\)
there is an active word realizing the maximum in the capacity formula, with
two or three blocks of each type, alternating between \(q\) and \(p\).  Each
block contains either one facet or an ordered pair of adjacent facets, and
distinct blocks of one type use disjoint facets.  This is an existence
statement for the capacity value; it does not claim that all minimizing orbits
have two or three bounces.

The certificate generates a raw superset of this family.  For \(k=2,3\), it
chooses \(k\) disjoint \(q\)-blocks and \(k\) disjoint \(p\)-blocks, fixes one
selected \(q\)-block as the cyclic starting block, and enumerates all relative
orders of the others.  Fixing the start loses nothing: cyclic shift preserves
the closure constraints and \(Q_\sigma\).  The generator does not quotient by
word reversal or by pentagon dihedral symmetry, so those duplicates remain as
separate checks.  It produces \(7200\) two-bounce and \(43200\) three-bounce
cyclic representatives before pruning.

Consecutive same-factor facets of an active word must intersect.  A mixed
transition must also have the positive directed symplectic sign.  On
\(0<\theta<\pi/10\), the certificate proves exactly that every such mixed sign
is either strictly positive throughout or strictly negative throughout.
Removing the negative transitions therefore removes only impossible active
words.  Exactly \(3340\) distinct raw words survive.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Exact classification of a fixed word.}
The computation uses
\[
  t=\tan(\theta/2),\qquad 0<t<\tan(\pi/20).
\]
All pentagon constants lie in the maximal real subfield \(K\) of
\(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{20})\), and
\[
  \cos\theta=\frac{1-t^2}{1+t^2},\qquad
  \sin\theta=\frac{2t}{1+t^2}.
\]
Thus every KKT matrix entry, multiplier, quadratic value, and action gap is a
rational function in \(K(t)\).

For each surviving word \(\sigma\), SageMath solves the equality-constrained
KKT system over \(K(t)\).  An active word realizing that maximum has positive
weights, and hence must occur among these stationary branches.  For a returned
branch the proof predicates are
\[
  \beta_i>0,\qquad Q_\sigma>0,\qquad
  \frac{1}{2Q_\sigma}-c_{\mathrm{act}}(\theta)\ge0.
\]
The roots and poles of the finitely many rational functions divide the open
half-domain into cells.  SageMath isolates these algebraic numbers exactly in
its real algebraic field and checks one exact sample in each cell, where every
sign is constant.  No floating-point sign decision enters the certificate.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{One branch by hand.}
To make the repeated calculation concrete, consider the word with three
blocks per factor
\[
  \tau=(0,5,3,8,1,7).
\]
Write \(s=\sqrt5\).  Exact row reduction of the KKT system gives, in the order
of the facets in \(\tau\),
\[
  \beta_\tau=
  \left(
    \frac{5-s}{20},\frac{s}{10},\frac{s}{10},
    \frac{5-s}{20},\frac{5-s}{20},\frac{5-s}{20}
  \right).
\]
Thus all six weights are positive, independently of \(\theta\).  Substitution
in the quadratic form gives
\[
  Q_\tau(\theta)
  =\frac{3(s-2)}5\cos\theta
   +\frac{(5s-11)\cos(\pi/10)}5\sin\theta.
\]
This is positive on the entire half-domain.  The active word above has
\(Q_{\mathrm{act}}=(3-s)\cos\theta/5\), and direct simplification yields the
particularly transparent comparison
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rotated-pentagon-worked-q-gap}
  Q_{\mathrm{act}}(\theta)-Q_\tau(\theta)
  =\frac{5s-11}{5}\sin\!\left(\frac{\pi}{10}-\theta\right).
\end{equation}
Since \(5s-11>0\), the competitor has smaller \(Q\), and hence larger action,
for \(0\le\theta<\pi/10\); it ties at the right endpoint.  In action form the
same branch is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rotated-pentagon-worked-action}
  A_\tau(\theta)
  =\frac{5(2+\sqrt5)}
  {2\bigl(\tan(\pi/5)\sin\theta+3\cos\theta\bigr)},
\end{equation}
and the exact action gap is
\[
  A_\tau(\theta)-c_{\mathrm{act}}(\theta)
  =\frac{10\sin(\pi/10-\theta)}
  {\bigl(4\tan(\pi/5)\sin\theta+12\cos\theta\bigr)\cos\theta}.
\]
This example also illustrates why it is useful to retain whole branch
formulas: \(A_\tau\) has its own strict minimum at
\[
  \tan\theta=\frac13\tan\frac\pi5,
  \qquad \theta\approx13.61^\circ,
\]
although it never determines the capacity on the open half-domain.  This is the
regular positive-\(Q\) path through the executable's classifier: solve the KKT
system, test positivity, and determine the sign of the action gap.  Many words
instead reach the inconsistent, zero-\(Q\), forced-zero, or no-feasible-cell
outcomes; the remaining feasible words either tie identically or have strictly
positive gap, as Table~\ref{tab:rotated-pentagon-certificate-outcomes} records.

\par\medskip\noindent\textbf{Exceptional parameters and endpoints.}
The rational-function computation proves the comparison away from a finite
set: besides the roots and poles used in the sign-cell decomposition, the
coefficient and augmented KKT matrices can drop below their generic ranks only
at zeros of finitely many nonzero minors.  Taking the union of these
exceptional sets over the finite word list leaves a dense open subset of
\(0<\theta<\pi/10\).  On that subset no feasible branch lies below
\(c_{\mathrm{act}}(\theta)\), while the active word attains this value.  Hence
\eqref{eq:rotated-pentagon-half-profile} holds there.

The path \(\theta\mapsto K_\theta\) is Hausdorff-continuous and its volume is
constant.  Proposition~\ref{prop:preliminaries-sys-hausdorff-continuity}
therefore makes \(\operatorname{sys}(K_\theta)\) continuous.  Equality extends
from the dense open subset to every exceptional interior parameter and to the
endpoints \(0\) and \(\pi/10\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:rotated-pentagon-half-profile}; the initial symmetries prove the
theorem for all real \(\theta\).
\end{proof}
