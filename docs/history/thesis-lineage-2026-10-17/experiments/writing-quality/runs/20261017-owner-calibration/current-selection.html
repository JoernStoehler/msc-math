Selecting products with small RR raises the mean numerical systolic ratio, but further restriction within that selection need not raise it again. In a fresh pool of 100,000 Lagrangian products, selecting the ten lowest-RR bodies per side-count group gave mean ratio 0.6260.626, compared with 0.3160.316 for the same number of comparison bodies. Selection used only geometry, before capacity was evaluated. Each factor-side-count group contributed equally. The comparison bodies came from the same pool, excluded the selected bodies, and were chosen without evaluating their capacities.

The distribution of area among the ridges supplies a second comparison. When the total symplectic ridge area is positive, define C(K)=maxFAω(F)∑FAω(F). C(K)=\frac{\max_F A_\omega(F)}{\sum_F A_\omega(F)}. This is the share of the total carried by the largest ridge; like RR, it is invariant under translation, common dilation and linear symplectic maps. Among products in the lowest one percent by RR in a separate fresh sample, the half with lower CC had a mean advantage of 0.0340.034 over the other half, positive in eight of ten groups. Thus largest-ridge dominance distinguishes mean ratios within this selected pool, even when the total ridge area is small.

The strong ordering in the broad samples is not retained within the low-RR panels themselves. Across 1,000 evaluated products in the lowest one percent by RR, correlations within the ten side-count groups range from −0.315-0.315 to +0.301+0.301; pooling ranks computed within each group gives +0.032+0.032. The most extreme tenth has a larger mean ratio than the rest in only two groups. Small RR distinguishes this population from the comparison bodies, but does not reliably order the ratios within it.
