# Relative-radius inequality: bounded source check

Prepared 2026-10-29 for the rank-four zonoids line. Scope: targeted primary-source check of whether the proposed max-radius inequality is already a known Bonnesen/projection-body theorem. This is not a broad priority search.

## Proposed form

For a zonotope generated by v_i in a symplectic four-space, let

    a(u) = Σ_i |ω(v_i,u)|,
    b(u) = Σ_{i<j<k} |det(v_i,v_j,v_k,u)|,
    ρ = max_u b(u)/a(u).

The requested inequality is Ψ ≤ ρ(Φ−ρ). The coordinator reports an algebraic equivalence between this and the universal rank-four target after adjoining a segment and averaging. An all-direction strengthening has already been falsified at C+JC; this source note does not revisit that calculation.

## Closest primary results located

1. Fradelizi–Madiman–Meyer–Zvavitch, [*On the volume of the Minkowski sum of zonoids*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02123), §1, equations (5)–(7), Theorems 3.6/3.9 and 5.1. The paper discusses a ratio of volume to a fixed hyperplane-projection volume. It proves the monotonicity statement

       |A+B| / |P_{u⊥}(A+B)| ≥ |A| / |P_{u⊥}A|

   for zonoids A,B in R³ (its equation (7), via Theorem 5.1 and the equivalence theorem); it also states the stronger Bonnesen estimate for arbitrary convex bodies in equation (6). In its zonotope proof, the critical reduction is explicitly the planar Bonnesen ratio inequality (the proof of Theorem 5.1, around equations (38)–(39)); the source then uses that result to prove the 3D zonoid ratio monotonicity.

   This is a close structural precedent: both involve volume relative to projection data, zonoids, and a Bonnesen inequality after a segment/projection reduction. It is not the same statement. The source compares Minkowski addition to one fixed hyperplane projection in dimension three; it does not use a symplectic form, the ratio b(u)/a(u), a maximum over directions, or the 4D inequality Ψ ≤ ρ(Φ−ρ).

2. The classical relative Bonnesen inequality quoted as equation (6) in that paper is

       |A+B| ≥ (|P_{u⊥}A|^(1/(n−1)) + |P_{u⊥}B|^(1/(n−1)))^(n−1)
                 · (|A|/|P_{u⊥}A| + |B|/|P_{u⊥}B|).

   This explains the 2D ratio inequality used in their proof. I found no transformation of this bound into the proposed four-dimensional max-support-radius inequality; the dimension, variables and quantifiers differ.

## Assessment and boundary

The named inequality is not an immediate restatement of the primary results above. The FMMZ theorem offers a plausible proof template—translate the ratio into a segment-addition statement, reduce to a lower-dimensional Bonnesen inequality—but its proved case is 3D and its ratio is different. Since the coordinator's segment-extension argument already shows the exact proposed max-radius bound is equivalent to the open rank-four target, the bounded search found no route by which the known 3D theorem would settle it. I found no primary source stating the rho inequality itself.
