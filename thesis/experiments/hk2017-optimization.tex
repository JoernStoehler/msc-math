\subsection{Implementation Optimization of the HK2017 Algorithm}
\label{sec:hk2017-optimization}

We profiled and optimized the implementation of \texttt{ehz\_capacity\_pruned}
without changing the underlying algorithm. The goal: reduce wall-clock time
to enable larger-scale experiments at higher facet counts.

\subsubsection*{Profiling}

Instruction-level profiling (Valgrind callgrind) on random $F = 8$ polytopes
revealed that singular value decomposition (SVD) consumed over~75\% of all
instructions. Every call to the KKT solver used SVD unconditionally, despite
rank deficiency being rare in practice.

\subsubsection*{Optimizations}

Two changes were applied:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{LU fast path.} Try full-pivoting LU decomposition first;
    fall back to SVD only when LU reports singularity. LU is much cheaper
    than SVD for small dense matrices ($7 \times 7$ to $15 \times 15$),
    and rank deficiency occurs mainly in highly symmetric configurations
    (e.g., the hypercube). This alone gives a 3--5$\times$ speedup.

  \item \textbf{Lazy permutation generation.} Replace eager collection of
    all $(m{-}1)!$ cyclic permutations with an in-place callback using
    Heap's algorithm. For $m = 10$ this eliminates 362{,}880 vector
    allocations. Combined with eliminating an intermediate matrix copy,
    this adds a further 1.2--2.2$\times$ speedup.
\end{enumerate}

Two further candidates were tried and discarded: pre-allocated matrix buffers
(no measurable benefit for small matrices) and skipping the residual check
for LU solutions (broke correctness on rank-deficient systems).

\subsubsection*{Results}

Table~\ref{tab:hk2017-opt-timing} shows wall-clock times before and after
optimization on representative polytopes.

Peak heap memory dropped from 573\,KB to 17\,KB (34$\times$ reduction),
primarily from eliminating eager permutation collection.

The speedup scales with facet count: random polytopes at $F = 10$ see
6$\times$ improvement, while symmetric polytopes with many rank-deficient
KKT systems (which still require SVD) benefit less.
After optimization, instruction-level profiling shows LU at 31\%,
SVD fallback at 29\%, and our algorithm code at only 3.5\%---further
implementation-level gains would require changes inside the linear algebra
library.
