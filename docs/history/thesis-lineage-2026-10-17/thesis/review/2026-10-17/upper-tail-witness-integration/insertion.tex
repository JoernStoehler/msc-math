\subsection{From a geometric score to a ratio bound}
\label{subsec:ds-geometric-witness}

A geometric rule need not predict capacity accurately to bound the systolic
ratio. It is enough to select one sufficiently strong feasible candidate in
the Haim--Kislev formula. For a polytope $K$ with
$0\in\operatorname{int}(K)$, let $V=\operatorname{vol}_4(K)$ and let
$q=Q_\sigma(\beta)>0$ for weights satisfying the normalization and closure
constraints of Section~\ref{sec:quadratic-program-algorithm-hk2019}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-program-algorithm-hk2019-hk-formula} gives
\[
 q\leq Q_{\max},\qquad
 c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}(K)\leq\frac1{2q},\qquad
 \operatorname{sys}(K)\leq\frac1{8Vq^2}.
\]
Thus a target ceiling $S>0$ follows whenever the selected candidate satisfies
$q\geq(8SV)^{-1/2}$. To establish HKO maximality in a specified class,
one would need such a choice for every body in that class with
$S=(3+\sqrt5)/5$. This is a sufficient criterion, not an established
bound for arbitrary bodies.

For polygon products, Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-program-product-six-facet}
reduces the search to at most three facets from each factor. A geometric selector
must still choose facets, closure weights and an order giving a strong
enough candidate. The ridge sum aggregates absolute pairings of all primal
edges; the capacity formula instead uses signed pairings of dual facet
vectors, with weights constrained by closure. This identifies structure
that a sharper explanation could use, without explaining the observed
statistical association by itself.

A proposed rule should therefore be assessed against its intended conclusion.
For prediction, ordering and errors within the high-ratio population matter.
For a ratio bound, the candidate must meet the displayed threshold throughout
the stated class. A candidate below that threshold leaves the bound undecided
for that body; it does not disprove it. Recovery of HKO or success on finitely
many sampled bodies cannot establish the required uniform inequality.
