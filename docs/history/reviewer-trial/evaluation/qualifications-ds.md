## Concerns, ranked by reader cost

1. **Likely: an unexplained statistical denial interrupts an otherwise concrete criterion.**

   Smallest relevant span: “and was not a significance test.”

   The preceding sentences raise the question of how the experiment decided whether its prediction succeeded. They answer it in ordinary terms: a positive overall difference and positive differences in at least seven pairs, specified before evaluating capacities. The preceding qualification, “It required no minimum effect size,” further explains what passing that criterion means. The appended denial introduces an undefined technical alternative. For this reader, it demands learning what a significance test is without explaining any additional inference they can draw from these results.

   Minimal change: delete the quoted clause, leaving “It required no minimum effect size.” Preserve the later explicit limitation to the observed finite pools. If the intended audience is likely to interpret the sign criterion as controlling the probability of a chance finding, a short positive statement of what the criterion establishes would be useful here. A statistical methods discussion explicitly considering significance testing would also justify retaining the distinction there.

2. **Likely, lower cost: the empirical-scope paragraph closes by repeating limitations it has already stated.**

   Smallest relevant span: “They do not provide certified bounds or a statistical law for arbitrary convex bodies.”

   The paragraph has already restricted the applicable population, identified the missing execution guarantees, and concluded that the plots and correlations concern recorded numerical systolic ratios. Those statements answer the locally raised questions about what was sampled and what the computed values establish. The appended sentence restates both limitations, with “a statistical law for arbitrary convex bodies” introducing a much broader alternative than the section has claimed. Removing it leaves the numerical status and population restriction explicit.

   Minimal change: delete the sentence while retaining the preceding provenance account and its positive conclusion. It would become useful if earlier surrounding material claimed certified capacity bounds from these historical runs, or explicitly proposed a statistical law across arbitrary convex bodies. In that setting, identify and correct the specific claim rather than appending a broad denial.

## Qualifications worth retaining

- “Moreover, the retained capacity evaluations predate the current certified implementation: their original execution records do not establish its guarantees.” This is the source of an evidential limitation, not a generic disclaimer. It explains why the numerical results cannot inherit the current implementation's guarantees and supports the distinction between numerical and exact claims.

- “Decreasing $R$ does not, however, provide a generally reliable way to increase the systolic ratio.” The immediately following designed paths supply counterexamples to the natural extrapolation from the pentagon identity. This negative statement introduces mathematical evidence that changes what the association can be used for; it also motivates the switch from deformation to selection.

- “They do not establish how often enrichment would recur under other generators.” The two finite-pool comparisons have just been presented as evidence for successful selection under a changed generator. This sentence distinguishes observed enrichment from a claim about its recurrence. Unlike the earlier broad denial of a law for arbitrary convex bodies, it addresses the precise generalization the experiment raises.
