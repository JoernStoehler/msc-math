\section{Generalized Reeb Orbits On Polytopes}\label{sec:generalized-reeb-orbits-polytopes}

The boundary of a polytope is not smooth, so the smooth Reeb vector field is
replaced by a differential inclusion. This section records only the part of the
nonsmooth theory needed to use the Haim--Kislev finite quadratic program.

\subsection{Definition}\label{subsec:generalized-reeb-orbits-polytopes-definition}

Let
\[
  K=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^4:\langle a_i,x\rangle\leq 1,\; i=1,\ldots,F\}
\]
be a full-dimensional polytope with \(0\in\operatorname{int}(K)\). We assume
that this \(H\)-representation is irredundant, so the facet corresponding to
\(a_i\) is
\[
  F_i=\{x\in K:\langle a_i,x\rangle=1\}.
\]
In this normalization the contact-normalized Reeb direction on \(F_i\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalized-reeb-facet-vector}
  R_i=2J_0a_i.
\end{equation}
Indeed \(R_i\) is tangent to \(F_i\), because
\(\langle a_i,J_0a_i\rangle=0\), and for \(x\in F_i\),
\[
  \lambda_0|_x(R_i)
  =\frac{1}{2}\langle -J_0R_i,x\rangle
  =\langle a_i,x\rangle
  =1.
\]

A \emph{generalized Reeb orbit} on \(K\) is a curve
\(\gamma\in W^{1,2}([0,T],\mathbb{R}^4)\) with \(T>0\),
\(\gamma(0)=\gamma(T)\), image contained in \(\partial K\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalized-reeb-inclusion}
  \dot{\gamma}(t)
  \in \operatorname{conv}\{R_i:\gamma(t)\in F_i\}
  \quad\text{for a.e. }t\in[0,T].
\end{equation}
This is the polytope form of the Hamiltonian inclusion
\(-J_0\dot{\gamma}\in\partial g_K^2(\gamma)\), translated into the
contact-normalized Reeb directions. Since every \(R_i\) in the active convex
hull satisfies \(\lambda_0(R_i)=1\) at \(\gamma(t)\), a generalized Reeb orbit
has
\[
  A(\gamma)=T.
\]

A generalized Reeb orbit is \emph{simple} if \(\dot{\gamma}\) is piecewise
constant, each constant value is one of the pure facet directions \(R_i\), and
for each \(i\) the set
\[
  \{t\in[0,T]:\dot{\gamma}(t)=R_i\}
\]
is a single interval, possibly empty. Thus a simple orbit never uses a
nontrivial convex combination of active facet directions, and it does not
return to the same facet direction in separated time intervals.

\subsection{Words, dwell times, and closure}
\label{subsec:generalized-reeb-orbits-polytopes-words}

The finite computations later use the special case in which the velocity is
piecewise constant and equal to a pure facet direction. A cyclic word
\[
  \sigma=(\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_m)
\]
together with dwell times \(\tau_k>0\) and a base point \(b=\gamma(0)\)
describes the velocity data
\[
  \dot{\gamma}(t)=R_{\sigma_k}
  \quad\text{for a time interval of length }\tau_k.
\]
Such data can represent a closed orbit only if the closing equation holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:generalized-reeb-closure}
  \sum_{k=1}^m \tau_k R_{\sigma_k}=0.
\end{equation}
The closing equation is necessary but not sufficient by itself; one must also
recover a base point so that the resulting broken line lies on the prescribed
facets in the prescribed order. Thus facet words are candidates for orbits, not
orbits automatically.

The period and action of a closed pure-word orbit are
\[
  T=\sum_{k=1}^m \tau_k,
  \qquad
  A(\gamma)=T.
\]
This convention is the free-period convention used in
Theorem~\ref{thm:preliminaries-clarke-dual-action}.

Different triples may describe the same geometric orbit. Removing zero-duration
entries, if any are allowed, and cyclically shifting the remaining
\((\text{facet},\text{duration})\)-pairs does not change the orbit, provided
the base point is shifted to the corresponding point on the curve. Also,
breakpoints of \(\dot{\gamma}\) are velocity changes, not necessarily changes
in the minimal face containing \(\gamma(t)\): an orbit may move inside a
nonsmooth face while switching from one active facet direction to another.

\subsection{Existence of simple minimizers}
\label{subsec:generalized-reeb-orbits-polytopes-existence-of-simple-minimizers}

Haim--Kislev prove that for every convex polytope there is a
minimum-action generalized Reeb orbit with a simple finite description
\cite[Theorem~1.5]{HK2017}. Their statement uses a fixed interval and
velocities that are positive multiples of \(Jn_i\). Translating to the
contact-normalized, free-period convention of this thesis, one may choose a
minimizer whose velocity is piecewise constant, whose pieces are positive-time
pure facet directions \(R_i\), and in which each facet direction occurs in one
connected time interval, possibly not at all.

This is an existence theorem for at least one capacity minimizer. It is not a
classification of all minimum-action generalized Reeb orbits. In particular,
other minimizers may pass through nonsmooth faces and may use convex
combinations of active facet directions. The finite Haim--Kislev quadratic
program searches a family guaranteed to contain a capacity-realizing simple
word; infeasible words or duplicate descriptions can still occur.
