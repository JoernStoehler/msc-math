# Scope checks for integration

These checks concern this research proposal's mathematical content. They do not impose a new workflow or request a full audit of every historical result.

## 1. Preserve the specification

The organizing unit is a scientific question with a domain and success criterion. Keep a global class inequality separate from a local perturbation theorem; a scalar certificate separate from a trajectory classification; and a representation theorem separate from prediction or candidate selection. The seven tracks are a recommended map, not a requirement to maintain seven active projects.

## 2. Keep the two certificate directions visible

Under the supplied convention, a feasible word bounds capacity from above. A globally bounded action maximum gives capacity from below. A complete normalized translation cover also gives capacity from below, by a different theorem. A smaller cover can refute fixed-factor optimality; proving that every cover has large area is what establishes it. Section 7.1 supplies all constants and directions.

## 3. Check the exact local quantifiers

The range \(12\le m_*\le17\) is conditional on the supplied eleven-genuine-facet neighborhood theorem and seven-cut construction. The derivative threshold for a selected family does not prove the unrestricted lower endpoint. Neither a nonpositive first variation nor a finite list of birth types is a Hausdorff neighborhood theorem. The general eleven-facet statement for \(P^*\) remains open on the supplied evidence.

## 4. Separate three kinds of finite descriptions

A finite formula for the scalar, an optimizer using a bounded number of facets, and a finite classification of physical trajectories are different statements. Verify the map between normal-cone trajectories and optimizing words before transporting dynamical conclusions. The flow-graph theorem's generic assumptions and Chaidez–Hutchings's assumptions must be stated separately.

## 5. Preserve the recent theorem boundaries

The planar covering source includes arbitrary quadrilateral and affinely regular hexagon factors. Chaidez–Tanny proves the inequality for its specified right quaternionic action; weaker symmetry and low-regularity equality have different statuses. The spectral-norm-diameter implication is precisely equality with EHZ implies cut additivity. A strict cut defect rules out equality, but does not by itself quantify the gap.

## 6. Review the elementary deductions at their stated strength

Complete labeled pairings determine a spanning normalized facet configuration up to a linear symplectic map. This does not apply to a histogram or eigenvalue list. A symmetry envelope yields a coarse upper bound; its volume excess generally prevents it from excluding all ratios above one. The orientation sphere preserves Euclidean shape, not arbitrary orientation-sensitive descriptors. Semialgebraicity gives structure, not tractability. The smoothing estimate gives scalar control, not orbit control.

## 7. Keep status and resource claims literal

The twenty-facet value is an attributed certificate result; the later continuation remains unreviewed. Old unchecked volumes remain unchecked. Growing-dimensional hardness is not fixed-four-dimensional hardness. No practical runtime, novelty, success probability, or optimal allocation was measured. Allocation should change on the explicit evidence in §6 rather than on a larger unverified record alone.

## Suggested first integration decision

Select a small combination of the four leading discriminators: unrestricted cover lower bound, complete perturbation model, one degenerate minimizing family, and one controlled geometric-information test. Then identify the computational guarantee needed by that selection. The packet intentionally leaves different mathematically worthwhile choices available.
