# Pfaffian-polynomial route: source boundary and coefficient check

Prepared 2026-10-28/29 for the rank-four zonoid line. Scope: a focused check of the principal-Pfaffian / pure-spinor / sector-stability route, not a general literature-priority search or proof audit.

## Target polynomial

For a real skew-symmetric m × m matrix W of rank at most four, put

\[
P_W(t)=1+\left(\sum_{i<j}|W_{ij}|\right)t+
\left(\sum_{i<j<k<\ell}|\operatorname{Pf}(W_{ijkl})|\right)t^2.
\]

The proposed inequality is exactly real-rootedness of this positive-coefficient quadratic: its discriminant is nonnegative precisely when
\[
4\sum_{i<j<k<\ell}|\operatorname{Pf}(W_{ijkl})|
\leq\left(\sum_{i<j}|W_{ij}|\right)^2.
\]

## What the primary sector-stability source proves

Alimohammadi–Anari–Shiragur–Vuong, [*Fractionally Log-Concave and Sector-Stable Polynomials: Counting Planar Matchings and More*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02708), is the primary source checked here. Its Corollary 63 says that if a real matrix A has A + Aᵀ ⪰ 0, then each fixed-cardinality principal-minor polynomial
\[
f_k(z)=\sum_{|S|=k}\det(A_{S,S})\,z^{[n]\setminus S}
\]
and its dual are either zero or \(\Gamma_{1/2}\)-sector-stable, with nonnegative coefficients. For skew A = W, A + Aᵀ = 0, odd principal minors vanish, and every even principal minor is det W[S] = Pf(W[S])². Thus this gives stability statements for **fixed-size sums of squared principal Pfaffians**. Corollary 62 is the one-cardinality extraction step; it does not assert that a sum of several cardinality slices remains sector-stable.

That distinction is decisive here. The target uses absolute values, not squares, and its univariate polynomial adds the size-2 and size-4 absolute-Pfaffian levels. The cited theorem neither supplies that coefficient polynomial nor states its real-rootedness. A diagonal specialization of a sector-stable multivariate polynomial gives zero avoidance in the corresponding sector, which is weaker than real-rootedness. In particular 1 + t + t² is Γ₁/₂-sector-stable (its roots have arguments ±2π/3) but has negative discriminant. So sector stability alone cannot yield the target discriminant inequality.

There is a separate easy rank-four **squared-coefficient** inequality. Orthogonal normal form gives nonzero singular blocks \(\lambda_1J,\lambda_2J\), and Cauchy–Binet gives
\[
\sum_{i<j} W_{ij}^2=\lambda_1^2+\lambda_2^2,\qquad
\sum_{i<j<k<\ell}\operatorname{Pf}(W_{ijkl})^2=\lambda_1^2\lambda_2^2.
\]
Therefore \((\sum W_{ij}^2)^2\geq4\sum\operatorname{Pf}(W_{ijkl})^2\). This is the coefficient relation for the spectral/squared version; it does not imply the requested \(\ell^1\)-coefficient relation (the directions of Cauchy–Schwarz do not provide that implication).

The primary AASV paper is also narrower than the seminar-summary shorthand “linear delta-matroids are sector-stable” might suggest. The checked theorem is about the displayed principal-minor polynomials (and a separate graph matching polynomial, Corollary 64). It does not identify the **uniform support polynomial** \(\sum_{S:\operatorname{Pf}(W[S])\ne0}z^S\), nor the **absolute principal-Pfaffian polynomial**, with those fixed-size determinant polynomials. A seminar abstract is a useful locator, not a substitute for the missing precise primary statement. I found no result in this source that covers P_W.

## Pure-spinor / Wick relation boundary

For a skew matrix, its signed principal Pfaffians are Wick coordinates of a pure spinor; they obey quadratic Wick relations. A primary exposition is Rincón, [*Isotropical Linear Spaces and Valuated Delta-Matroids*](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/2954/pdf), which states that the spinor variety is parametrized by signed principal Pfaffians and that its ideal is generated by Wick relations. The four-index relation is
\[
w_{1234}w_\varnothing-w_{12}w_{34}+w_{13}w_{24}-w_{14}w_{23}=0.
\]

Taking coordinatewise absolute values does not preserve this relation. Exact example: set
\[
W_{12}=W_{34}=W_{14}=W_{23}=W_{24}=1,\quad W_{13}=3.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{Pf}(W_{1234})=1-3+1=-1\), and the signed Wick relation is \(-1-1+3-1=0\). Replacing each Wick coordinate by its absolute value gives \(1-1+3-1=2\ne0\). Hence the target coefficients are not, in general, coordinates of the same pure spinor; direct application of spinor-variety stability or Wick identities requires a new argument handling sign loss.

## EFLS 2024 boundary

Eur–Fink–Larson–Spink, [*Signed Permutohedra, Delta-Matroids, and Beyond*](https://mattlarson2399.github.io/Papers/EFLS2024.pdf), proves a Lorentzian/log-concavity result for transformed U-polynomials of delta-matroids with an enveloping matroid (Theorem B), including realizable delta-matroids. It does not study principal-Pfaffian absolute values. Its delta-matroid coefficients are those of a Tutte-like deletion/contraction invariant, not the target’s ℓ₁ sums over pair and four-set Pfaffians. This is mathematically adjacent background, not a derivation of the target coefficient inequality.

## Assessment

The sector-stability route identifies a useful neighboring object and an exact barrier: principal-minor stability sees \(\operatorname{Pf}^2\); pure-spinor geometry sees signed Pfaffians; the target needs sums of absolute Pfaffians across two cardinality levels. Neither source bridges either gap. The known sector result therefore yields no coefficient-4 ℓ₁ bound. In its skew rank-four specialization, the elementary spectral calculation yields the squared/ℓ₂ coefficient relation above. Whether the absolute-value polynomial P_W is always real-rooted remains exactly the open target; no theorem located in this bounded pass settles it.
