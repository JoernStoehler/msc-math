# Independent mathematical review

## Verdict and theorem boundary

**Accepted.** The argument in `MATHEMATICAL-HANDOFF.md` proves, for the
packet-normalized regular triangle `T` and rotated regular hexagon `Q_delta`,

```text
0 <= delta <= pi/6
    implies
c_EHZ(T x_L Q_delta) <= 3 sqrt(3) sec(delta)
and
sys(T x_L Q_delta) <= (3/4) sec(delta)^2 <= 1.
```

At `delta=pi/6`, the separate non-translatable-curve argument proves the exact
values

```text
c_EHZ(T x_L Q_{pi/6}) = 6,
sys(T x_L Q_{pi/6}) = 1.
```

The interior equality

```text
c_EHZ(T x_L Q_delta) = 3 sqrt(3) sec(delta)
```

is **not** proved. The explicit billiard is a capacity competitor, so its
action gives an upper bound on capacity. Proving that it is minimizing at every
interior angle would require an all-branch lower bound. That stronger result is
unnecessary for Viterbo's inequality on this path: any shorter competing branch
only lowers `c_EHZ` and `sys`.

This is an independent agent review of the promoted argument. It is not a
formalization, thesis-facing exposition, or Jörn review or approval.

## Normalization and source interface

Write `e(theta)=(cos(theta),sin(theta))`. The packet producer
`experiments/sys-datascience/methods/ridge-symmetry-completion/design_candidates.py`
constructs

```text
T = conv{2 e(2k pi/3) : k=0,1,2},
Q_delta = conv{(2/sqrt(3)) e(delta+k pi/3) : k=0,...,5}.
```

Thus both factors have inradius one. Their areas are

```text
area(T)=3 sqrt(3),
area(Q_delta)=2 sqrt(3),
vol_4(T x_L Q_delta)=18.
```

The product-volume identity and relative-rotation fundamental interval are
recorded in `formal/lagrangian-product-rotation-symmetry.tex`, especially
Lemma `lem:rotation-fundamental-domain`. For the `3x6` pair that lemma gives
`0<=delta<=pi/6`.

The source convention being used is the one in
`papers/hko2024/counterexample.tex`, Definition `def-T-billiard-in-K`: a closed
polygon with table points `x_j` and momentum points `p_j` is a generalized
`Q_delta`-billiard in `T` when

```text
x_{j+1}-x_j in N_{Q_delta}(p_j),
p_{j+1}-p_j in -N_T(x_{j+1}).
```

The same paper, Theorem `thm-capacity-lagrangian-product`, identifies
`c_EHZ(T x_L Q_delta)` with the minimum `Q_delta^circ`-length of such a
billiard and with the minimum length among non-translatable polygons having at
most three vertices. The length of an increment `v` is `h_{Q_delta}(v)`. This
also agrees with the action normalization explained after Theorem
`thm:quadratic-program-algorithm-hk2019-hk-formula` in
`thesis/04-haim-kislev-quadratic-program.tex`.

## Generalized-billiard sign and index check

Let

```text
a0=e(pi/3),  a1=e(pi),  a2=e(5pi/3),
t=tan(delta),  lambda=sqrt(3) sec(delta).
```

The triangle is given by `a_i dot x <= 1`. Use the table points from the
handoff in the cyclic order

```text
x0=((1-sqrt(3)t)/2, (sqrt(3)+t)/2),
x1=((1+sqrt(3)t)/2, (t-sqrt(3))/2),
x2=(-1,-t).
```

They lie respectively on the facets with normals `a0,a2,a1`. Put

```text
u0=e(3pi/2+delta),
u1=e(5pi/6+delta),
u2=e(pi/6+delta),
```

which are outward facet normals of `Q_delta`, and

```text
p0=sec(delta)e(3pi/2),
p1=sec(delta)e(5pi/6),
p2=sec(delta)e(pi/6).
```

Direct subtraction gives

```text
x1-x0=lambda u0,
x2-x1=lambda u1,
x0-x2=lambda u2,
```

and

```text
p1-p0=-lambda a2,
p2-p1=-lambda a1,
p0-p2=-lambda a0.
```

The second list uses the facet normal at the **arrival** point: `x1` is on the
`a2` facet, `x2` on the `a1` facet, and `x0` on the `a0` facet. It therefore
checks the minus sign and the `j+1` index in the HKO definition, rather than a
shifted or sign-reversed convention.

Exact facet membership also holds over the closed interval. For each `x_j`,
the active triangle pairing is one and every inactive pairing is at most

```text
(sqrt(3)tan(delta)-1)/2 <= 0 < 1.
```

Hence all three `x_j` remain in the relative interiors of their indicated
triangle edges. For each `p_j`, the active `Q_delta` pairing is one and all
other facet pairings are drawn from

```text
-1,  1/2 +/- (sqrt(3)/2)tan(delta),
     -1/2 +/- (sqrt(3)/2)tan(delta).
```

Their maximum is `(1+sqrt(3)tan(delta))/2<=1`. It is strictly below one away
from the selected facet when `delta<pi/6`. Consequently

```text
lambda u_j in N_{Q_delta}(p_j),
-lambda a_arrival in -N_T(x_{j+1}),
```

and the displayed points satisfy the generalized-billiard conditions with the
source's signs and indexing.

## Endpoint nonsmoothness

At `delta=pi/6`, each `p_j` acquires one additional active hexagon facet and
becomes a vertex of `Q_{pi/6}`. This does not invalidate the construction.
The normal cone is then generated by the two adjacent outward facet normals;
the already used ray `R_+ u_j` remains inside that enlarged cone. The `x_j`
remain in triangle-edge interiors, so their normal cones are still exactly the
rays used in the momentum-jump equations. Thus the endpoint orbit is a valid
generalized billiard even though the momentum boundary is nonsmooth there.

## Action and capacity upper-bound direction

Because the `u_j` facet of `Q_delta` has support height one,

```text
h_{Q_delta}(x_{j+1}-x_j)
  = h_{Q_delta}(lambda u_j)
  = lambda.
```

The three-bounce length, hence the lifted generalized-characteristic action,
is therefore

```text
A_H(delta)=3 lambda=3 sqrt(3) sec(delta).
```

Since capacity is the **minimum** billiard length, the logical direction is

```text
c_EHZ <= A_H,
```

not the reverse. With volume `18` and the packet normalization
`sys=c_EHZ^2/(2 vol_4)`, this gives

```text
sys <= A_H^2/36 = (3/4)sec(delta)^2 <= 1.
```

No capacity continuity, numerical branch identification, or global
minimality assumption enters this bound.

## Independent endpoint lower-bound check

At `delta=pi/6`, let `f_i(x)=a_i dot x`. Comparing the six vertices gives

```text
h_{Q_{pi/6}}(v)
  = (2/3)(max_i f_i(v)-min_i f_i(v)).
```

For a closed polygonal curve `gamma`, set

```text
M_i=max_{x in gamma} f_i(x),
S=M_0+M_1+M_2.
```

Because `a0+a1+a2=0` and these normals span the plane, translation by `z`
changes the three support maxima by numbers `f_i(z)` whose sum is zero. The
strict inequalities needed to put every vertex in `int(T)` are feasible
exactly when

```text
sum_i (1-M_i)>0.
```

Therefore

```text
gamma is non-translatable into int(T) iff S>=3.
```

Choose occurrences `z_i` on the cyclic polygon where `f_i(z_i)=M_i`. Their
order around the curve is one of the two cyclic orientations. Let

```text
rho(v)=max_i f_i(v)-min_i f_i(v).
```

The range function is a norm on the plane identified with the zero-sum
three-coordinate subspace, so the `rho`-length of each arc is at least the
`rho`-distance between its endpoints. If an arc runs from `z_i` to `z_j`, then

```text
rho(z_j-z_i)
 >= (a_j-a_i) dot (z_j-z_i)
  = M_j-f_j(z_i)+M_i-f_i(z_j).
```

Apply this to the three arcs in their actual cyclic order. In either
orientation, summing and using
`sum_l f_l(z_i)=0` gives

```text
length_rho(gamma)>=3S.
```

This remains valid if maximizing occurrences coincide: zero-length arcs are
allowed and the same weak inequalities apply. Hence every non-translatable
curve has

```text
length_{Q_{pi/6}}(gamma)
  = (2/3)length_rho(gamma)
  >= 2S
  >= 6.
```

The non-translatable-polygon characterization in
`papers/hko2024/counterexample.tex` therefore yields `c_EHZ>=6`. The explicit
endpoint billiard has action six and yields `c_EHZ<=6`, proving equality and
`sys=6^2/(2*18)=1`.
