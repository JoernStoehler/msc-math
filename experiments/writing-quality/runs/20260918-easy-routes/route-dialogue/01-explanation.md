# Informal explanation for a mathematically trained agent

The thing I want a reader to understand is why a cheap-looking geometric number can help a capacity search without becoming a replacement for capacity. We have a big random table, and one of the strongest signals says: bodies with smaller total symplectic area of their two-faces tend to have larger recorded systolic ratio. That sentence needs unpacking, but the distinction at its heart is straightforward. A signal for deciding where to look need not tell you which way to move a particular body.

Take each polygonal two-face F of a four-dimensional polytope, integrate the symplectic form over it, take the absolute value, then add over faces. Divide by the square root of four-volume. Call this R. Taking absolute values before adding matters: orientations cannot make different faces cancel. It is a scale-free measure of how much symplectic area the collection of faces carries, not their Euclidean surface area.

For a Lagrangian product P in the q-plane times Q in the p-plane, this becomes especially concrete. Write a_i and b_j for the edge vectors of the two polygons. Factor faces lie in Lagrangian planes and contribute zero. The other faces are rectangles, with one edge from each polygon, and their contribution is |a_i dot b_j|. Thus R is the sum of those absolute dot products divided by sqrt(area(P)area(Q)). Relative orientation changes these pairings even if the two polygon shapes and the volume do not change.

The statistical signal came from 14,336 retained rows: 4,096 generic polytopes with 5–12 facets and 10,240 polygon products in the ten buckets 3<=k<=m<=6. Heights were drawn in [0.8,1.2], seed 42, and the samples were conditioned on acceptance. R had Spearman correlation -0.9384 with the stored target, whereas Pearson correlation was only -0.2050. So this is strongly about ordering, not a straight-line fit. There are two large qualifications: the mixture composition matters, and the recorded target is a historical numerical estimate whose original evaluator receipts are incomplete. I would call that target hat(sys), reserving sys for the mathematical capacity ratio. The maximum recorded value was 0.862586, not a counterexample and not a bound on anything outside that table.

A later exact example helps make the correlation geometrically intelligible. Let K_theta be the product of a unit regular pentagon and its planar rotation through theta. Reduce theta to its distance d from the nearest multiple of pi/5, so 0<=d<=pi/10. The edge-pair sum gives R=16 sin(pi/5) cos d. A separate proved capacity profile gives sys=(5+2sqrt(5))/10 sec^2 d. Multiplying yields sys R^2=16(3+sqrt(5)). Along this family, reducing R really is the same as increasing sys. This is not a history in which the formula predicted the screen: it is an analytic illustration that became available later. At d=pi/10, sys is about 1.047214, the known HKO position; the random table did not sample that exact structured family successfully just because it sampled many products.

But the family restriction is essential. Existing designed 3x6 and 4x4 paths have R and numerical sys decreasing together at every retained step, ending at mathematical ratios 3/4 and 1/2. So a universal instruction to minimize R is wrong. A selector can still work: generate many shapes cheaply, rank by descriptors without evaluating their targets, then spend capacity evaluations on the selected subset and controls. In one 100,000-candidate experiment only 1,675 distinct selected/control bodies were evaluated. Its maximum was 0.867546. Separate fresh concentration validation met its predeclared enrichment criterion, and frozen selectors enriched mean targets in both 4x6 and 6x6 buckets of another named source, although that transfer experiment evaluated only 91 distinct bodies. These are positive results about concentrating search effort below the threshold, not discoveries of a counterexample.

The neighboring optimizer chapter has another strategy: exploit local branch structure of the capacity objective. Its 448-run comparison belongs there. Here I want the reader to leave with the ridge descriptor's precise meaning, one genuine exact explanation, the reason it cannot be a universal objective, and a concrete role for it in candidate search.
