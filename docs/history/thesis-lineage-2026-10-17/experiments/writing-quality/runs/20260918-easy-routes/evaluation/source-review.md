# Scientific and comprehension audit

Audited A–D independently against `inputs/*.md`, `inputs/historical-baseline.txt`, and the supplied concentration README/verdict in `evaluation/supplemental-source/`. Line references below refer to the frozen candidate files. I did not inspect route directories or the candidate key. This is a source-grounded audit, not recertification of the historical evaluations or a prediction of human acceptance.

## Candidate A

The central descriptor definition (lines 7–14), dot-product formula (18–21), angle reduction and inverse-square identity (24–47) agree with the packet. Absolute values are correctly taken face by face. The pentagon restriction, opposite numerical paths, and distinction between selecting and deforming bodies are explicit. The practical purpose is clear from line 3 through the conclusion.

- **Missing explanation for an asserted selection success:** line 53 says a fresh experiment “met its predeclared criterion for incremental enrichment from ridge concentration.” Neither concentration nor the criterion is explained. The unresolved reader question is: *What additional rule was tested beyond low R, against which comparator, and what counted as improvement?* This matters because the clause supplies positive search evidence, whereas the preceding 100,000-pool paragraph reports only a maximum and no values above one. The supplement supplies a concrete answer: split the lowest 1% by R within each bucket into halves by maximum symplectic face-area share, and compare the means overall and in at least seven buckets. The candidate's statement is supported, but its explanatory dependency is absent.
- **Standalone normalization dependency:** lines 40–47 introduce the mathematical sys profile, boxed constant, and value above one without defining `sys = c_EHZ²/(2 vol_4)`. Line 5 defines only the historical hat notation. The reader cannot recover the convention responsible for the factor two or threshold one from this passage alone. This is conditional on chapter context: an already established convention would resolve it; it is not a false formula.
- **Undefined auxiliary selector:** line 53 names “rho selectors” without defining rho. This obstructs understanding what that particular transfer arm did, although the ridge-selector result independently carries the paragraph's main conclusion. The supplied search account also does not define rho, so this is inherited source incompleteness, not invented mathematics.
- **Optimizer comparison boundary:** line 55 appropriately labels the 0.984783 result a historical target and denies a certified optimizer ranking. “The initial random-table maximum is not a practical ceiling” is defensible at the recorded-search level and is supported by the packet. It should not be read as a controlled comparison of the two methods or evaluators; the passage does not explicitly make that stronger claim. The named counts and median match the source.

No definite unsupported central mathematical assertion or invented discovery chronology found. The introductory “exact explanation of its behavior” is broad in isolation, but the explicit one-family limitation at line 47 resolves it.

## Candidate B

The descriptor and normalization of R (9–22) are unambiguous; the explicit symplectic form makes the product formula independently readable. The trigonometric calculation, capacity profile and independent-factor-scaling claim (24–54) match the packet. The chronology and opposite-path limitations at line 56 correctly restrict the interpretation. The opening and closing explain the scientific purpose.

- **Undefined criterion:** line 60 says the concentration experiment “met its predeclared incremental-enrichment criterion” without saying what was selected, what comparison was made, or what success meant. This is the same consequential missing dependency as in A. A reader cannot distinguish a tiny positive descriptive contrast from a significance threshold or meaningful effect-size threshold using the candidate alone. The source establishes the former descriptive criterion, with no minimum effect size. The candidate does not falsely claim significance, but leaves the meaning of its positive evidence inaccessible.
- **Standalone sys normalization:** lines 44–54 display both capacity and sys but never define their relationship. The numerical convention is therefore not explicit, despite the supplied packet defining it. A chapter-level prior definition would resolve this. This is a context dependency rather than an algebra error.
- **Transfer-rule specificity:** line 60 reports “Frozen selectors” and their finite mean enrichment without identifying the rules. This is sufficient for the modest existence claim but does not let a reader reconstruct why that experiment supports this particular descriptor. The preceding paragraph identifies scalar selectors only generically. The source supports the claim; the cost is a less informative connection from R to the actual transfer intervention.

The early phrase “the direction of the observed association has an exact explanation” (24) could initially suggest a population mechanism. Lines 54 and 56 explicitly limit it to an example and say it “does not explain the whole sampled population.” In full context this is not an overgeneralization. Omitting the optimizer comparison is consistent with the declared boundary at line 3, not evidence of a defective ridge subsection.

## Candidate C

The mathematical ratio is explicitly normalized (17–22), and line 24 carefully distinguishes reproducing an association from certifying target accuracy or capacity ordering. The product formula, angle sum, capacity profile, and restricted identity are source-consistent. Lines 55–61 correctly identify capacity as requiring a separate argument: the proof outline is compressed, but the passage attributes the result to the pentagon proof rather than pretending that the edge-area calculation determines capacity. The scientific aim is unusually explicit at line 3: selection and individual improvement require different evidence.

- **Rendering obstructs mathematical reading in several places:** for example, line 15 contains literal `(3\leq k\leq m\leq6)`, line 42 `(s=\sin(\pi/5))`, and line 55 `(A\cos\theta+B\sin\theta)`. These are ordinary parentheses rather than math delimiters. In a Markdown renderer the commands remain raw text. This is not wrong mathematics, but it specifically impedes reading the sampling ranges and proof outline; the valid display equations do not repair every occurrence.
- **Selection evidence is less explicit, not false:** line 73's original-pool experiment reports counts and maximum but no selector/control contrast. Line 75 supplies the positive contrast in the transfer study and thus does support a bounded selection claim. It would be incorrect to count the omission of the independent concentration experiment as a missing logical prerequisite: the transfer result already supplies finite enrichment evidence. The cost is that this version gives no concrete incremental selection criterion or effect size.
- **Undefined rho:** line 75 names “rho and ridge selectors.” As in A, rho is not defined in the supplied search account either. The reader cannot identify that arm's geometric operation; the ridge arm still supports the central result.

The wording “mathematical ratios” for the two endpoints (71) is supported by the packet's stated exact endpoint values; the candidate separately and correctly calls the retained path observations numerical. The packet does not reproduce the endpoint proofs, so this audit cannot independently certify that mathematical endpoint assertion. That is a source limitation, not evidence of a candidate error. Likewise, “data and their association analysis are reproducible” (24) is supported by the recovered hash/replay account, but is not a promise that this subsection alone provides retrieval commands.

This is appropriate for a subsection using that result. A final manuscript needs a resolvable reference to that analysis; no candidate contains an actual numbered cross-reference. It is not necessary to repeat the capacity proof here.

No consequential source contradiction, false central formula, universal-law claim, or invented chronology found. The long line-42 paragraph contains three different experiments, so a reader must keep the original selected/control union, the concentration complement comparison, and the transfer controls distinct. The distinctions are present; this is density, not an absent dependency.

## Comparisons and shared evidence limits

All four preserve the main scientific separation: a pooled historical rank association, an exact restricted geometric identity, numerical counterexamples to a general deformation heuristic, and finite target-free candidate selection. None reports 100,000 capacity evaluations, turns Pearson/Spearman into a linear law, or asserts that the pentagon calculation prospectively predicted the historical screen. None requires the tangentialization/orientation panels to make its scoped argument valid.

A and B include more study labels than explanations of what constitutes their positive selection result. D spends substantial space on one complete comparator/criterion/outcome chain; that concretely addresses the missing question in A and B, at the cost of a dense final evidence paragraph. C provides the strongest explicit historical-target/order caveat and a helpful capacity-proof bridge, while carrying less concrete incremental-selection evidence and several malformed inline mathematical expressions. A carries a bounded optimizer result; B–D mostly refer that complementary scientific question elsewhere. These are useful scope differences, not a basis for forcing an overall ranking.

The packet itself remains the evidentiary limit. Historical execution provenance is incomplete; replaying features cannot establish certified targets. The search account explicitly inherits many packet-reported results without rerunning producers. The finite path and transfer findings do not establish population laws. The capacity identity is supported by the accepted analytic proof described in the packet, not by the 1001-angle floating-point check. All four candidates substantially respect these boundaries.
