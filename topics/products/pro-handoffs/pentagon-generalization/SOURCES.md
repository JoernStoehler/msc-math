# Primary literature pointers

These links were checked on 18 September 2026. External articles are not
bundled. This is a focused starting set, not an exhaustive novelty search.

1. **Pazit Haim–Kislev, “On the symplectic size of convex polytopes.”**
   Geometric and Functional Analysis 29 (2019), 440–463.
   [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-019-00486-4),
   [arXiv:1712.03494](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.03494).
   Theorem 1.1 is the finite capacity formula used here. Our normalized
   covectors and earlier-entry-first word convention are explained in
   CONTEXT.md; the bibliography's old key HK2017 refers to the preprint date,
   not a different mathematical source.

2. **Pazit Haim–Kislev and Yaron Ostrover, “A counterexample to Viterbo's
   conjecture.”** Annals of Mathematics 203 (2026), 603–622.
   [Final article](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2026/203-2/p05),
   [arXiv:2405.16513v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16513v3).
   Proposition 1.4 supplies the regular-pentagon endpoint capacity used in
   the proof. The full angular profile in the supplied note uses that known
   value; it does not prove the imported endpoint independently. Check the
   paper's hypotheses and symplectic convention when extending its use.

3. **Alexey Balitskiy, Ivan Mitrofanov and Alexander Polyanskii, “Triangle
   covering problems and the Viterbo inequality in the plane.”**
   [arXiv:2603.12495v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12495v1), 12 March 2026.
   A particularly relevant neighboring approach via triangle covers and
   billiards. Theorem 1.2 proves the Viterbo inequality for a product with
   arbitrary planar convex first factor and second factor an arbitrary
   quadrilateral or affinely regular hexagon; §4 treats triangles. Section
   6.1 discusses the regular pentagon and HKO counterexample; §6.2 discusses
   squares truncated at one corner. These provide alternative geometric
   perspectives and constraints on proposed extensions. The quadrilateral
   theorem is not an exact angular-capacity formula; the hexagon theorem
   does not cover every nonregular hexagon. No claim is made here that this
   paper settles the prospective research question you will choose.
