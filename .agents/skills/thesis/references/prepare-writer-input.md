# Prepare Writer Input

Use this reference to reduce avoidable discovery work for a later writer
without replacing source truth by a lossy summary.

Begin with a named passage, reader need, claim, or proof burden. Gather only
material that can change what the writer says or how the reader reaches the
intended understanding. Prefer exact source paths, theorem or artifact
identifiers, short descriptions of relevance, and explicit uncertainty over a
second narrative of the mathematics.

Separate where it matters:

- accepted facts and stakeholder decisions;
- primary mathematical or empirical sources;
- agent interpretation and proposed explanation;
- old prose worth consulting;
- unresolved contradictions or missing support.

Do not make one file self-contained at the cost of copying every source. A
writer packet may be one companion or a small routed set of files. Its value is
that the writer knows what to inspect, why it matters, and what cannot safely be
claimed—not that the writer can avoid primary sources.

Do not ask a gathering agent to flatten an open-ended repository area into a
generic chapter summary. Give it a bounded question, source surface, and
downstream writer decision. If gathering reveals that the reader purpose or
claim is unsettled, stop preparing prose inputs and return that decision to the
main agent.

Writer preparation is successful only if it reduces expensive rediscovery or
prevents a likely error. Length, apparent completeness, and the number of
sources collected are not useful success measures by themselves.
