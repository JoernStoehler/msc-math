# Draft Thesis Prose

Use this reference for a substantial new publication-facing passage after its
reader purpose and mathematical or empirical source route are sufficiently
clear.

Choose the smallest unit that can express and test a coherent explanatory
move. A unit may be a subsection, a proof introduction plus one argument, or a
short sequence of definitions and motivation. Do not generate a whole chapter
merely because its headings and sources exist.

Make the unit honest about its boundary. Define the local notation and state
the ambient hypotheses needed for its claims, or identify the stable preceding
text that already does so. Check that the draft actually performs the
explanatory or computational work promised by its purpose; a polished
regularity statement, example, or transition is not a substitute for a
missing formula, proof step, or scope condition.

Write for the reader's mathematical understanding. Introduce an object when
the reader can see the question it answers or the later work it enables. Make
logical dependence and emphasis visible in the order of sentences and
paragraphs rather than by narrating the writing process.

Keep source status, caveats, and limitations precise, but do not repeat the same
qualification until it obscures the result. Put execution state, repository
ownership, agent review history, and API mechanics outside publication prose
unless they are themselves part of the thesis claim.

After drafting, read the passage as a continuous argument and in its rendered
context. A locally polished paragraph can still fail because it arrives too
early, answers no live question, duplicates a later explanation, or hides the
main claim behind preliminaries. Review it against its source and intended
chapter role before treating a fluent first pass as merge-ready prose.
