\subsection{Definitions behind the local contact prediction}
\label{app:ds-local-contact-definitions}

The score uses the labelled dual-normal presentation
\[
 P(A)=\{x\in\mathbb R^2:a_i\cdot x\leq1\},\qquad
 P(B)=\{y\in\mathbb R^2:b_i\cdot y\leq1\},\quad 0\leq i\leq4,
\]
with $z=(A,B)$ and $V(z)=\operatorname{area}(P(A))
\operatorname{area}(P(B))$. All nearby factors must have five genuine
facets and contain zero in their interiors. The algebraic HKO reference is
$z_H=(A^*,B^*)$, where $s=\sqrt5$, $J(x,y)=(-y,x)$,
\begin{align*}
 a_0^*&=(1,0),&a_1^*&=((s-1)/4,1),&a_2^*&=(-(s+1)/4,(s-1)/2),\\
 a_3^*&=(-(s+1)/4,(1-s)/2),&a_4^*&=((s-1)/4,-1),&b_i^*&=Ja_i^*.
\end{align*}
For each three-index support $E=(i,j,k)$ whose closure weights are positive,
put
\[
 (\alpha_i,\alpha_j,\alpha_k)
 =\frac{(\det(a_j,a_k),\det(a_k,a_i),\det(a_i,a_j))}
 {\det(a_j,a_k)+\det(a_k,a_i)+\det(a_i,a_j)},
\]
with all other weights zero. Define $\gamma$ similarly for $B$.
These are the vertices of the factor closure polytopes of
Section~\ref{subsec:quadratic-program-product-six-facet}. Each factor
has precisely five such supports near $z_H$.

For each closure pair and each cyclic order $w$ of its six supported
facets, fix one starting facet, let $\epsilon_{ij}(w)$ be $+1$ when
$A_i$ precedes $B_j$ and $-1$ otherwise, and set
\[
 Q_w(z)=\frac14\sum_{i,j}\epsilon_{ij}(w)\alpha_i\gamma_j
                           (a_i\cdot b_j).
\]
Closure makes this expression independent of the cyclic starting point.
The six-facet reduction gives
\[
 Q_{\max}(z)=\max_w Q_w(z),\qquad
 \operatorname{sys}(z)=\frac1{8V(z)Q_{\max}(z)^2}.
\]
At $z_H$, the maximum is $Q_0=1/10$. Of the $25\cdot120=3,000$
cyclic words, fifty attain $Q_0$; call this set $\mathcal A_H$.
The finite score is defined without a choice of basis by
\[
 g_w=\left.\left(2\frac{\mathrm dQ_w}{Q_0}
                      +\frac{\mathrm dV}{V(z_H)}\right)\right|_{z_H},
 \qquad D(\delta)=\max_{w\in\mathcal A_H}g_w[\delta].
\]
There are twenty distinct forms $g_w$. Evaluating them at a new geometry
requires no new capacity optimization. Their algebraic coefficients are
fixed by these formulas and the reference, rather than fitted to ratios.

\paragraph{Why this is a local law.}
Nonzero determinants and strict signs keep the same closure supports in a
neighborhood of $z_H$. The largest inactive objective at the reference is
$(-5+3\sqrt5)/20<Q_0$, leaving a strict gap
$(7-3\sqrt5)/20$. Continuity excludes all inactive branches nearby.
Every active $Q_w$ is positive and analytic there, as is $V$. Taylor
expansion of $\log(8VQ_w^2)$ has a uniform quadratic remainder because
there are finitely many branches. Taking their maximum gives
\[
 \left|\log\frac{\operatorname{sys}(z_H+\delta)}{\rho_H}
                      +D(\delta)\right|\leq C\|\delta\|^2
 \quad(\|\delta\|<r)
\]
for some $C,r>0$. No numerical values of $r,C$ are supplied.
The twenty forms sum to zero, have rank eleven and annihilate the nine
independent infinitesimal symmetries (four translations, four product
linear symplectic parameters and common dilation). Consequently
$D\geq0$ and vanishes exactly on this symmetry tangent. It is a generally
asymmetric polyhedral gauge on the quotient, not a Euclidean distance.
The stated radial direction gives $D(t\delta)=2\sqrt5|t|/5$.
The finite census, rank and directional claims require the accompanying
exact computational witnesses; the analytic argument alone does not
establish those identities.

\paragraph{A finite normalization defines $I$.}
For any convex pentagon product, choose interior origins in both factors,
then cyclically order the dual rows.
Enumerate both factor roles, both common cyclic orientations and the five
starting indices independently in each factor (at most 100 candidates).
For each candidate, let $p_i$ be the intersection of rows $a_i,a_{i+1}$,
with indices modulo five, and let $p_i^*$ denote the reference vertices.
Use the unique affine map $x\mapsto Lx+t$ sending
$(p_0,p_2,p_4)$ to $(p_0^*,p_2^*,p_4^*)$; transform the second factor
by $L^{-T}$. Center the latter at its area centroid and scale it
positively until its first dual normal has $y$-coordinate one.
Retain only candidates for which that pre-scaling coordinate is positive.
The reference triangle strictly contains zero, and a bounded pentagon
has a normal with positive $y$-projection, so candidates exist and the
normalized dual rows are defined. Write $C_h(z)$ for each normalized
twenty-coordinate tuple and set
\[
 I(P(A),P(B))=\min_{h\text{ valid}}D(C_h(z)-z_H).
\]

For fixed corresponding labels, transform the factors by
$P'=\lambda GP+u$, $Q'=\lambda G^{-T}Q+v$,
where $\lambda>0$ and $G$ is invertible.
The new triangle map has $L'=\lambda^{-1}LG^{-1}$.
It gives the same normalized first factor. The transformed second factor
is a translate of $\lambda^2L^{-T}Q$, so centering and the final scale
remove the difference. The valid candidate sets correspond, also when
$\det G<0$ reverses both orientations. Thus $I$ is unchanged by these
transformations, facet relabelling and factor interchange. In particular,
translation invariance shows independence of the chosen interior origins. Its domain
retains the product splitting; invariance under general four-dimensional
symplectic maps destroying that splitting is not asserted.

At exact HKO, fifty candidates are valid: ten normalize exactly to $z_H$,
while the others have $D\geq2\sqrt5-4>0$. All final projection signs are
strict. Each zero candidate is an analytic, ratio-preserving map fixing
$z_H$; its normalized displacement is $O(\|z-z_H\|)$.
The positive gap excludes other candidates locally, so the raw expansion
applied to the finite zero-candidate family proves
\[
 \log\frac{\operatorname{sys}(z)}{\rho_H}
             =-I(z)+O(\|z-z_H\|^2).
\]
The candidate count, strict projection signs and exact positive gap are
additional witness obligations for this canonical statement.

\paragraph{Evidence limits.}
The fresh canonical test comprises five directions, three mechanisms and
three scales, giving forty-five distinct untransformed products. Nine
transformed copies are coordinate controls and are not extra prediction
tests. All forty-five are retained, including twenty-two ratios at most
one. The reported above-one errors concern the other twenty-three;
subsets were selected only for evaluation after all predictions.
Their capacities, areas and ratios use exact arithmetic conditional on
the imported capacity formula and complete product reduction.
The separate 1,024-body transfer test uses historical numerical target
scalars; its rank correlation is not a certified-capacity theorem.

For the separately certified rational product labelled
\texttt{random\_5x5\_s0\_92}, exact ordered-field checks give
\[
 I>\frac45,\qquad \operatorname{sys}>\frac45,\qquad
 \frac{\rho_H}{1+I}<\frac35.
\]
Since $e^I\geq1+I$, both proposed global upper bounds are false.
This does not refute $\operatorname{sys}\leq\rho_H$ and does not extend
or invalidate the local Taylor expansion.
