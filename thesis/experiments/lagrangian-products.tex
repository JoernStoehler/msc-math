\subsection{Lagrangian Products of Rotated Polygons}
\label{sec:lagrangian-products}

The only known 4D counterexample to Viterbo's conjecture is a Lagrangian
product of two pentagons (Haim-Kislev \& Ostrover 2024). Random 4-polytopes
(Section~\ref{sec:systolic-ratios}) never exceeded~$\mathrm{sys} = 0.68$, far
below the Viterbo threshold. We probe the conjecture by searching
systematically in the space of Lagrangian products, where counterexamples are
known to exist.

\subsubsection*{Mathematical setup}

Recall (Definition~\ref{def:lagrangian-product}) that the Lagrangian product
of convex polygons~$P \subset \mathbb{R}^2_q$ and~$Q \subset \mathbb{R}^2_p$
is $P \times_L Q = \{(q,p) : q \in P,\, p \in Q\} \subset \mathbb{R}^4$.

The 4-volume satisfies~$\operatorname{vol}_4(P \times_L Q)
= \operatorname{area}(P) \cdot \operatorname{area}(Q)$ (Fubini's theorem on
complementary subspaces).

\paragraph{Rotation parameter.}
Fix a polygon~$P$ in $q$-space. For an angle~$\theta$, let~$R(\theta)$ denote
the standard rotation in~$\mathbb{R}^2$ and
define~$Q_\theta = R(\theta)\,Q$. We study the one-parameter family
\[
  K_\theta \;=\; P \times_L Q_\theta.
\]
Since rotating both factors by the same angle~$\varphi$ is a symplectic
transformation~$\operatorname{diag}(R(\varphi), R(\varphi)) \in \mathrm{Sp}(4)$,
only the \emph{relative} rotation between the factors affects~$\mathrm{sys}$.

\begin{lemma}[Fundamental domain for rotation sweeps]
\label{lem:rotation-fundamental-domain}
Let~$P$ be a regular $n$-gon and~$Q$ a regular $m$-gon, both centered at the
origin. Then~$\mathrm{sys}(P \times_L Q_\theta)$ depends on~$\theta$ only
through its equivalence class in~$[0, \pi/\operatorname{lcm}(n,m)]$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Periodicity.}
The block-diagonal matrix~$M_\varphi = \operatorname{diag}(R(\varphi),
R(\varphi))$ is symplectic (direct verification:~$M_\varphi^T J_0 M_\varphi
= J_0$). Applying~$M_{2\pi/n}$ to~$P \times_L Q_\theta$ maps~$P$ to itself
(by $n$-fold rotational symmetry) and replaces~$Q_\theta$
with~$Q_{\theta + 2\pi/n}$. Similarly, the $m$-fold symmetry of~$Q$ gives
$Q_\theta = Q_{\theta + 2\pi/m}$. The combined periodicity
is~$2\pi/\operatorname{lcm}(n,m)$.

\emph{Mirror symmetry.}
The matrix~$M = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, -1, 1)$ is symplectic
(verification:~$M^T J_0 M = J_0$). It acts on~$\mathbb{R}^2_q$ as reflection
across the~$q_2$-axis and on~$\mathbb{R}^2_p$ as reflection across
the~$p_2$-axis. For our angle convention (normals starting at~$\pi/2$), both
regular polygons are invariant under these reflections, and~$M$
maps~$Q_\theta$ to~$Q_{-\theta}$.

Together: $\mathrm{sys}(\theta)$ has period~$2\pi/\operatorname{lcm}(n,m)$ and
satisfies~$\mathrm{sys}(\theta) = \mathrm{sys}(-\theta)$, so the fundamental
domain is~$[0, \pi/\operatorname{lcm}(n,m)]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For pentagon~$\times$~pentagon:
$\operatorname{lcm}(5,5)=5$, fundamental
domain~$[0^\circ, 36^\circ]$. The HK-O counterexample at~$90^\circ$
maps to~$90 \bmod 72 = 18^\circ$ in this domain.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{Family 1: Pentagon rotation curve}

We sweep~$\theta \in [0^\circ, 90^\circ]$ at~$0.25^\circ$ resolution (361
points) for the family~$\mathrm{Pentagon} \times_L R(\theta)\,\mathrm{Pentagon}$,
where each pentagon is regular with circumradius~1 (since~$\mathrm{sys}$ is
scale-invariant---$c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}$ is 2-homogeneous and volume is
4-homogeneous---the choice of circumradius does not affect~$\mathrm{sys}$).
We compute~$c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}$ using both the HK2017 algorithm and the billiard
algorithm (Section~\ref{sec:dataset}); the two algorithms agree on all
Lagrangian products to relative error~$< 10^{-8}$
(Section~\ref{sec:correctness}).
Each HK2017 capacity computation takes approximately~270\,ms after
optimization (10~facets); the billiard algorithm computes each in under~1\,ms.

Figure~\ref{fig:pentagon-sweep} shows the resulting curve. The systolic ratio
oscillates between~$0.9472$ and~$1.0472$ with period~$72^\circ$, confirming the
fundamental domain prediction of Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation-fundamental-domain}
($\pi/\operatorname{lcm}(5,5) = 36^\circ$, period~$72^\circ$). The mirror
symmetry~$\mathrm{sys}(\theta) = \mathrm{sys}(-\theta)$ holds to machine
precision ($< 10^{-14}$), with the curve symmetric around~$\theta = 18^\circ$
within each period.

The maximum~$\mathrm{sys} \approx 1.0472$ occurs at~$\theta \equiv 18^\circ
\pmod{72^\circ}$ (equivalently~$\theta = 90^\circ$, the HK-O
angle). The minimum~$\mathrm{sys} \approx 0.9472$ occurs
at~$\theta \equiv 0^\circ \pmod{72^\circ}$ (aligned pentagons). The violation
region~$\mathrm{sys} > 1$ spans approximately~$\theta \in (13.5^\circ,
22.5^\circ)$ within each fundamental domain---about~$25\%$ of the period.

Since rotation preserves area,
$\operatorname{area}(Q_\theta) = \operatorname{area}(Q)$ for
all~$\theta$, and Fubini gives
$\operatorname{vol}(K_\theta) = \operatorname{area}(P) \cdot
\operatorname{area}(Q)$, all variation in~$\mathrm{sys}$ comes from the
EHZ capacity.

\subsubsection*{Family 2: Regular polygon pairs}

We sweep the rotation angle for all pairs~$(n, m)$ with~$3 \le n \le m$
and~$n + m \le 12$, sweeping~$\theta$ over the fundamental domain
$[0, \pi/\operatorname{lcm}(n,m)]$ at adaptive resolution (360 points for
$F \le 8$, down to~30 for $F = 12$).

\paragraph{Algorithm reliability.}
Two pairs---$(4,4)$ and~$(4,6)$---exhibit capacity \emph{discontinuities}:
the HK2017 capacity jumps by a factor of~${\sim}2$ between adjacent
angles (e.g., from~$c = 2.0$ at~$\theta = 0^\circ$ to~$c = 3.99$
at~$\theta = 0.125^\circ$ for~$(4,4)$). Since EHZ capacity is continuous
in the Hausdorff metric, these jumps indicate a bug---likely in the
adjacency pruning or skeleton computation for near-degenerate
configurations involving squares. Results for these two pairs are excluded
from the analysis below. The remaining 8~pairs have 100\%
cross-validation and smooth capacity curves.

\paragraph{Findings from reliable pairs.}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$(3,6)$ at $\theta = 0^\circ$}: Triangle $\times_L$ hexagon
  (aligned) saturates the Viterbo bound at~$\mathrm{sys} = 1$ exactly.
  Lagrangian products with~$\mathrm{sys} = 1$ have been characterized by
  Balitskiy~(2020); Chaidez--Hutchings~(2021, \S1) cite the
  triangle~$\times$~square as a previously known example.

\item \textbf{$(5,5)$ at $\theta = 18^\circ$}: This reproduces the HK-O
  counterexample ($\theta = 18^\circ \equiv 90^\circ \pmod{72^\circ}$)
  with~$\mathrm{sys} \approx 1.047$, confirming the known Viterbo violation.

\item \textbf{$(4,5)$: square $\times_L$ pentagon.} Both algorithms agree
  (to relative error~$< 10^{-12}$) on all 201~sampled angles, with a
  smooth capacity curve. The systolic ratio exceeds~$1$ at \emph{every}
  angle:~$\mathrm{sys}$ ranges from~$1.324$
  (at~$\theta = 9^\circ$) to~$1.376$
  (at~$\theta = 0^\circ$). If correct, this is a substantially larger
  Viterbo violation than the HK-O pentagon counterexample---a~$37.6\%$
  excess over the threshold. This finding is computationally robust
  (cross-validated, smooth, consistent) but has not been independently
  verified; it should be treated as a conjecture pending analytic
  confirmation.
\end{itemize}

The remaining five reliable pairs~$(3,3)$, $(3,4)$, $(3,5)$, $(5,6)$,
$(6,6)$ all satisfy~$\mathrm{sys} < 1$ at every tested angle, with the
highest non-exceeding value being~$\mathrm{sys}((3,4)) \approx 0.928$.

\subsubsection*{Family 3: Random Lagrangian products}

We generate 234 random Lagrangian products by sampling random convex
polygons (3--6 sides each, heights in~$[0.3, 2.0]$) and forming their
Lagrangian product. The rotation between factors is implicit in the random
orientations.

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{../experiments/figures/random_products.png}
\caption{Left: distribution of systolic ratios for 234 random Lagrangian
  products. Right: sys vs.\ facet count. The dashed line marks
  $\mathrm{sys} = 1$.}
\label{fig:random-products}

None of the 234 random Lagrangian products exceeds~$\mathrm{sys} = 0.9$.
The maximum observed value is~$\mathrm{sys} \approx 0.772$,
with mean~$0.296$ and median~$0.280$. The distribution is
broadly spread over~$[0, 0.77]$ with no concentration near the Viterbo
threshold.

\subsubsection*{Findings}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two pairs exceed the Viterbo bound.}
  Among all regular $(n,m)$-gon Lagrangian products with~$n + m \le 12$
  and reliable cross-validation, two pairs achieve~$\mathrm{sys} > 1$.
  The $(5,5)$ pair (pentagon~$\times$~pentagon) reproduces the known HK-O
  counterexample with~$\mathrm{sys}_{\max} \approx 1.047$. The~$(4,5)$ pair
  (square~$\times$~pentagon) shows a larger violation
  with~$\mathrm{sys} \ge 1.32$ at all tested angles, pending independent
  verification. One pair---$(3,6)$---saturates the bound
  at~$\mathrm{sys} = 1$ exactly, while the remaining five reliable pairs
  satisfy~$\mathrm{sys} < 1$ at all angles.
  We restricted to~$n + m \le 12$ due to the exponential cost of the
  HK2017 algorithm; whether other polygon pairs violate the bound
  remains open.

\item \textbf{The $(5,5)$ violation region is narrow.}
  For pentagon~$\times$~pentagon, the Viterbo-violating angles occupy only
  about~$25\%$ of each fundamental domain ($9^\circ$ out of~$36^\circ$).
  The curve has small amplitude ($\approx 0.10$) relative to its mean
  ($\approx 0.997$), suggesting that the EHZ capacity varies only mildly
  with rotation. In contrast, the~$(4,5)$ pair exceeds~$\mathrm{sys} = 1$
  at \emph{every} tested angle.

\item \textbf{All predicted symmetries hold.}
  The~$72^\circ$ periodicity and mirror symmetry predicted by
  Lemma~\ref{lem:rotation-fundamental-domain} are confirmed to machine
  precision ($< 10^{-14}$) in the pentagon sweep, validating both the
  symplecticity of~$\operatorname{diag}(R(\varphi), R(\varphi))$ and
  of~$M = \operatorname{diag}(-1, 1, -1, 1)$.

\item \textbf{Random Lagrangian products stay far below the threshold.}
  Among 234 random Lagrangian products (3--6~sided factors, heights
  in~$[0.3, 2.0]$), the maximum sys is~$0.772$. None reaches~$0.9$.
  This is higher than the~$0.68$ ceiling observed for fully random
  (non-product) polytopes in Section~\ref{sec:systolic-ratios},
  suggesting that the Lagrangian product structure does push sys upward,
  but not enough to approach the Viterbo bound without specific
  regular polygon geometry.

\item \textbf{Algorithm reliability depends on polytope geometry.}
  The~$(4,4)$ and~$(4,6)$ pairs show capacity discontinuities where
  both HK2017 and billiard return values inconsistent with the known
  continuity of EHZ capacity. These failures affect polytopes involving
  squares, possibly due to the axis-aligned normal structure creating
  near-degenerate configurations in the skeleton computation. The
  remaining 8~pairs show perfect cross-validation, so the issue is
  geometry-specific rather than systematic.
\end{enumerate}
