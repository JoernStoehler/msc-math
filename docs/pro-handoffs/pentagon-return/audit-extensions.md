## Exhaustive closure supports

For odd regular normals, a positive three-point closure occurs exactly when all three cyclic gaps are less than a half-circle. The normals lie on a circle, so three distinct normals are never collinear. There are no antipodal pairs. Accordingly there are no degenerate closure vertices or two-point closures to add.

For five normals the positive integer gaps sum to five and are at most two: only `(1,2,2)` is possible, with five supports. For seven they sum to seven and are at most three: only `(1,3,3)` and `(2,2,3)` are possible, with seven supports each. Direct enumeration of the subsets independently returned exactly these counts. Rotation covers both orientations because the gap multisets have repeated entries.

For the heptagon, the representatives `{0,3,4}` and `{0,2,5}` have axial closure steps of length

- `s1 = 1/(1+h)`;
- `s3 = k/[h(1+k)]`,

where `h=cos(pi/7)` and `k=cos(3pi/7)`. The two side steps have real part `-s/2` and imaginary parts `±s*t/2`, with `t=tan(pi/7)` or `tan(3pi/7)` respectively. These formulas follow directly from the equal side weights and total factor mass one; they use support-normalized covectors, not unit normals.

## Stability, including support changes

The assumptions deliberately rule out the events at which a closure support can enter or leave the positive feasible list: a singular three-column affine system, a zero barycentric coordinate, or an antipodal two-point closure. With finitely many triples and pairs, all these exclusions persist on a common open neighborhood. Positive weights are continuous there. Thus the six-block coefficient list has fixed indexing and continuous coordinates.

The four-block hull does not require a fixed exposed face at each angle. Its finite generating points can be represented using signed facet covectors divided by widths. Widths are positive and continuous as polygons vary locally with the same labelled facets. The convex hull and support function therefore vary continuously, uniformly on the circle, even when the maximizing point or exposed edge changes. Strict initial containment gives a positive minimum of `h4(theta)-max_z Re(z*exp(-i theta))` on that compact circle. It persists after a sufficiently small perturbation.

The odd regular pentagons and heptagons satisfy all nondegeneracy assumptions: a line meets their circumcircle in at most two vertices, and a chord through the center would require antipodal vertices. The claimed open neighborhoods are consequently justified. No explicit perturbation radius, persistence of regular secant profiles, or stability of equality at pentagon switches follows, and none is claimed.

## Interpolation and triangle obstruction

At an interior angle of an interval shorter than pi, both sine-interpolation weights are strictly positive. Equality with the endpoint upper bounds therefore forces both endpoint deficits of an attaining candidate to vanish. Conversely, a common maximizing candidate supplies equality throughout. The finite coefficient hull is a polytope; intersecting exposed faces contain a shared vertex, which is an actual generating candidate. The stated criterion and its endpoint caveat are sound.

For the triangle, direct exact symbolic enumeration gives the six nonzero coefficients

`±1/3 ± i*sqrt(3)/9` (four independent sign choices), and `±2i*sqrt(3)/9`,

plus zero. Their hull has circumradius `2/(3sqrt(3))`, directions `pi/6+k*pi/3`, and inradius `1/3`. It strictly contains the four-block hexagon of circumradius `2/9`, so its support function alone determines capacity. The resulting capacity is `3sqrt(3)/4` times the stated secant; at angle zero it is `3/2`, whereas the four-block value is `9/4`.
