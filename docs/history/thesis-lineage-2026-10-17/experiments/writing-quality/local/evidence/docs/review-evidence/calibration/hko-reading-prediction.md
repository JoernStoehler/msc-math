## Prediction

The opening upper-bound mechanism should be followable: it states the geometric
question, explains why one decreasing upper bound suffices, and supplies the
sign and compactness steps before using the symmetry slice. I do not predict
a material objection to that logical mechanism. This remains a prediction,
not a passing-quality judgment on behalf of the reader.

My strongest predicted reading obstacle is the transition to **The concrete
base point and its volume**. It introduces `t,s,alpha,b,d` and the quartic
before explaining why that algebraic parametrization helps. A reader can
calculate with the constants but may reasonably ask why they must first
decode this representation of a simple regular pentagon. The first likely
repair is one sentence giving its purpose: explicit coordinates in a common
number field permit all later derivative and rank checks in exact arithmetic.

A second possible obstacle is the start of **One actual seven-facet feasible
section**: certificate entry and zero-based witness language precede the
geometric meaning of the selected word. Those identifiers serve lookup,
not understanding. They could move to the table or its caption.

A third, weaker risk is the symmetry step: the inverse function theorem is
summarized before the explicit action on normals appears at the end. The
dimension/complement argument is stated, so this may be an acceptable forward
reference for the intended reader rather than a real objection.

objection. Compare the actual passage and mechanism, and leave unmentioned
