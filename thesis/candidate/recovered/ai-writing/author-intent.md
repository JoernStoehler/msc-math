# Reflection chapter: author intent and sources

Rewritten on 18 September 2026 from Jörn's explanation relayed by the root
agent. Version 3 restores the first version and applies his ten annotations
retained in
`docs/ai-reflection-review/hypothesis-annotations-v1.json`. The central
distinction is understanding a user's goal versus selecting and pursuing a
goal. His practical requirement is sufficient overlap to produce a useful
end result, not identical goals or merely accurate inference of intent.

The unannotated opening and other prose are preserved from version 1 except
where the inference-versus-pursuit correction requires consistency. Version 2
was a broad rewrite; Jörn objected that it worsened previously unflagged text.
The annotation-to-edit guide records version 3 against version 1, not version 2.

The autonomous HKO account, agents recognizing goals and criticisms without
acting accordingly, corrective effort, cross-page reading difficulties, and
the greater difficulty of exposition than this project's proof work are
first-person observations. They are not controlled comparisons or general
capability theorems. The Grader/RLVR interpretation, prompting distinction,
and preference-training explanation of inflated prose are hypotheses or
working interpretations. The unsupported long-thread causal account and
isolated thread-length number from the first draft were removed. The
disclosure remains separate and unchanged.

Public sources support only the training descriptions: Guo et al.,
*DeepSeek-R1 incentivizes reasoning in LLMs through reinforcement learning*,
Nature 645 (2025), “DeepSeek-R1-Zero” and “Reward design”; and Ouyang et al.,
*Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback*,
arXiv:2203.02155 (2022), abstract. Both were read on 18 September 2026.
Neither establishes the chapter's proposed causal explanations. No private
frontier-model claim or public adoption timeline is asserted.
