\subsection{Finite-budget local optimization at ten facets}
\label{subsec:black-box-datascience-finite-budget-optimization}

The random-table scan does not ask how far a local method can improve one
polytope.  We therefore compared seven iterative optimizers on \(64\)
previously unused random ten-facet starting points.  The starts were disjoint
from the development and tuning populations.  Every run received the same
\(1000\)\,ms measured serial-compute budget, including full evaluations of the
historical evaluator and the optimizer's own work, together with a cap of
\(128\) full evaluator calls.  The manifest fixed all methods and
hyperparameters before the held-out outcomes were computed.

The methods are standard gradient, derivative-free, and population ideas
\cite{NocedalWright2006}, adapted to the lower-envelope structure
\[
  \operatorname{sys}(a)=\min_\sigma s_\sigma(a).
\]
Here \(s_\sigma\) is a smooth KKT branch only on its branch domain, and the
minimizing word and admissible branch set can change with \(a\).  Thus the
gradient of one minimizing branch need not ascend the lower envelope.  The
model \(f(x,y)=y-|x|\) already shows the issue: at its ridge, either selected
branch gradient points away from the ridge, while balancing both branches
gives the ascending direction along it.

The retained historical runs use a binary64 evaluator that reconstructs the
polytope and executes its configured legacy orbit search.  Write
\(\widehat{\operatorname{sys}}(a)\) for the JSON field called \texttt{sys} in
those traces.  Its action windows and admissibility decisions do not certify
that the returned orbit family realizes the complete mathematical capacity
envelope, so the empirical comparisons below concern
\(\widehat{\operatorname{sys}}\), not a verified value of
\(\operatorname{sys}\).  Completeness of the historical evaluator's candidate
family remains untested.

All local methods propose in the \(25\)-dimensional Euclidean slice transverse
to translations, common scaling, and the linear symplectic action.  Every
proposed outer step is then checked by a fresh geometry reconstruction and
the evaluator's full configured orbit-word search for
\(\widehat{\operatorname{sys}}\).  Table
\ref{tab:black-box-datascience-f10-optimizer-steps} states the actual frozen
steps; it does not claim that the chosen parameters are optimal for their
algorithm families.

The branch-history method has the largest held-out median.  On matched starts,
its median advantage over transition prediction is \(0.00333\), with bootstrap
95\% interval \(0.000073\)--\(0.00824\).  The more elaborate transition rule had
improved the development population, but did not generalize as an overall
replacement.  The finite-gap model remains a strong independent baseline.
Both single-branch gradients, CMA-ES, and coordinate search are substantially
weaker at this budget.  No held-out run reached
\(\widehat{\operatorname{sys}}\geq1\).  That negative result is not
interpretable on its
own: it could reflect the landscape, insufficient compute, or a defective
local model.  The following controls separate failures that the retained
evidence can distinguish.

\paragraph{Controlled ability to recover ascent.}
The held-out ranking alone does not show that the branch model recognizes
useful directions.  We therefore calibrated the same finite-step machinery on
HKO, whose local-maximality theorem supplies a known target.  The development
panel used four predeclared directions---two sentinels and two random
directions---at distances \(10^{-4},10^{-3},10^{-2},10^{-1}\), giving \(16\)
perturbations.  HKO itself was the false-positive control.  It accepted no
move, whereas every perturbation admitted a fully re-evaluated improving
branch-model move.  One step recovered \(93.4\%\)--\(100\%\) of the known
evaluator gap to HKO and removed \(77.9\%\)--\(100\%\) of the distance to HKO.
The gradient of one current minimizing branch recovered only
\(-22.1\%\)--\(41.4\%\) of the gap.  This supports the multi-branch direction
on these four development directions, but does not estimate its miss
probability on arbitrary endpoints.  In particular, it rules out a gross
failure to recognize known ascent on this panel, not failure on other local
geometries.

The objective curves alone do not establish convergence.  CMA-ES, literal
gradient, and coordinate search are still visibly improving at the budget
boundary, while a branch-aware method can stop because its local model returns
no proposal or its internal distance becomes too small.  We therefore tested
\(16\) population-stratified branch-history endpoints in both signs of all
\(25\) transverse basis axes, at relative radii \(10^{-3}\), \(10^{-4}\), and
\(10^{-5}\).  Seven one-second endpoints had an explicit fully recomputed
improving direction.  Repeating the same starts with a five-second ceiling
changed seven endpoint values and reduced the count to two, so five failures
were ordinary insufficient-depth failures.

Passing this restricted finite basis poll does not establish stationarity:
ascent may lie between the basis axes.  Indeed, a separate diagnostic followed a
branch-informed improving path from each of three deliberately selected
endpoints for ten validated steps, stopping only at the imposed step cap.  One
of these endpoints had no improving basis-axis probe, yet gained \(0.00223\)
over normalized path length \(0.01\) and still had positive tested slope
\(0.0620\).  These paths are lower bounds on remaining improvement, not
estimates of an eventual maximum.

\paragraph{Why some branch-model proposals fail.}
We also applied one continuation step to the eight highest four-anchor
endpoints from the \(128\)-start tuning population.  Five improved, by
\(1.57\cdot10^{-6}\) to \(4.61\cdot10^{-5}\), and none crossed
\(\widehat{\operatorname{sys}}=1\).  The
highest endpoint, at \(0.999962478\), had no improvement among the two
multi-branch models, five current-branch gradient moves, or \(50\) signed basis
probes.  More importantly, \(52/80\) multi-branch proposals decreased the
recomputed \(\widehat{\operatorname{sys}}\) even though the evaluator's target
winner was represented and its affine prediction was positive; \(40\) such
losses retained determinate, unchanged geometry.  All \(40\) current-branch
gradient proposals decreased.  This is outcome-selected diagnostic evidence,
not a prevalence estimate.

A three-proposal audit then compared two failures with one positive control.
At normalized radius \(10^{-5}\), the failures perturbed their named KKT
matrices by \(218\) and \(67\) times the smallest base eigenvalue magnitude
and crossed a zero eigenvalue; the control had ratio \(1.53\), did not cross
zero, and kept the correct sign.  At the diagnostic radius \(10^{-8}\), finite
differences converged to the implemented analytic derivative.  Binary64 and
exact-arithmetic geometry reconstruction agreed at all \(39\) audited points,
and relative volume discrepancies were below \(10^{-15}\).  For these cases,
the error is therefore finite-distance nonlinearity of an ill-conditioned KKT
branch, not omission of the evaluator's target-winning word, a geometry
predicate, the volume derivative, or a sign error in the infinitesimal
formula.  Euclidean distance alone is not an adequate trust scale; the KKT
spectral gap supplies a mechanistic warning, although this three-case audit
does not calibrate a universal cutoff.

We therefore select four-anchor branch history only as the strongest tested
finite-budget method for this random \(F=10\) population and one-second
budget.  The experiment neither proves convergence to a local maximum nor
transfers the ranking to another facet count, starting distribution, or
budget.  The completed diagnostics support a more specific engineering
conclusion: multi-branch models can identify useful oblique ascent, but their
finite-distance predictions need candidate refresh, branch re-solving, a
conditioning-aware trust check, and full validation.  They also leave open
whether any other fixed-\(F\) local maximum has systolic ratio larger than
\(1\).  Thus the missing local-maximum classification cannot be assigned
solely to the geometry of the mathematical \(\operatorname{sys}\): ordinary
insufficient depth and identified model failures both occur in the retained
runs.
