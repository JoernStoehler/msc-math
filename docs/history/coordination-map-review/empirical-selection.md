## Candidate assessment

### 1. Ridge circulation: directly improves an existing passage

**What it adds.** The DS chapter already defines C(K)=max ridge area / total ridge area. Stokes plus cycle decomposition proves C<=1/3 for every convex four-polytope and C<=1/4 for Lagrangian polygon products or centrally symmetric four-polytopes. This supplies a mathematical restriction on a measured feature and demonstrates the proposed data-to-mathematics approach without suggesting a capacity relation.

**Minimal presentation.** State the general and product max-share bounds, explain the facet adjacency circulation, and give the short cycle argument. Central symmetry can be an optional sentence/proof addition. Approximately half to one-and-a-half typeset pages is a planning estimate, not a drafted result. Put it after the empirical question motivates studying ridge geometry rather than expanding the initial list of descriptors.

**Avoid expansion by default.** N2=(sum a)^2/sum a² is an inverse-Simpson count, while the historical feature N1 is Shannon entropy exponentiated. Introducing both solely to display every proved consequence costs definitions and risks confusing measured and derived quantities. Preserve strict N2>3 / >4, sharp degenerations, matching inequality and N1 corollaries in the source packet or an appendix only if their use justifies it. The max-share bounds are non-strict; do not copy the effective-count strictness onto them.

**Limit.** No established capacity/systolic implication; the stronger product statement requires Lagrangian position. Do not transfer it to arbitrary orthogonal rotations. Historical replay needs complete ridge lists, not thresholded or failed face orderings.

### 2. Fixed P5 and arbitrary centrally symmetric partner: strongest optional short theorem

**What it adds.** For fixed regular pentagon Q and every centrally symmetric planar convex K, c(K×_L Q)=W(K), where W is a difference-body containment scale. The sharp systolic maximum is (10+6 sqrt(5))/25<1, attained precisely by translates/dilates of a specified polar decagon. Thus central symmetry of the partner rules out a counterexample in this whole class, and the maximizing partner is described geometrically. Unlike the affine-pentagon theorem, this varies the partner over arbitrary symmetric bodies, including smooth ones. Neither theorem subsumes the other: a nonsingular affine pentagon is not centrally symmetric.

**Exposition cost.** Must introduce the ten normal-triangle covering criterion (BMP Theorem 3.3), its scale convention, and the fixed orientation of the decagon. The proof itself is economical: fit all templates into B, get c<=W from segments, then use K-K=2K and monotonicity for the reverse inequality; containment gives the sharp area bound. A drawing of the decagon with the longest-edge template is especially useful. Rough planning cost: 2–3 pages including the imported criterion and figure, less if that criterion is already explained elsewhere.

**Selection suggestion.** If included, use a distinct short subsection on symmetric partners, not a claimed corollary of affine pentagons. Do not append the full harmonic-fiber campaign just because it shares the evaluator. The asymmetry necessary condition is an optional one-line consequence, not a reason to add another descriptor suite.

**Limits.** No optimality among arbitrary asymmetric partners; no proof that HKO is a global maximum in that class. Equality orientation matters. No novelty claim follows from the targeted source search.

### 3. Arbitrary-factor product-position initial regime: valuable but different research question

**What it adds.** For the specified J_theta path from symplectically orthogonal factor planes to Lagrangian factor planes, areas a<=b and terminal capacity ell imply c_theta=a/cos(theta) whenever tan(theta)<=ell/(2a). HK mass splitting proves this for polygons; continuity extends it to arbitrary planar convex factors. It can imply interior capacities larger than both endpoints. This relates geometry and the HK optimization with a short substantive argument.

**Why not just attach it to rotated pentagons?** Existing rotation changes one pentagon within a fixed Lagrangian splitting. Here the factor planes change symplectic type. It needs its own J_theta definition, orientation conventions, endpoint capacities and geometric illustration. Treating both as the same rotation question would actively confuse the thesis.

**Minimal coherent package if selected.** Present the initial-regime bound/proof and one explanatory counterexample distinguishing an existing lifted orbit from a globally minimizing orbit. The latter has strict containment and a feasible mixed HK competitor with smaller action. Its certificate is only an upper bound on capacity, which suffices; no full enumeration is needed. Estimated cost 3–5 pages for a readable combined account. The endpoint-collision family is an alternative explanatory example, not automatically another required subsection.

**Defer by default.** This opens enough new notation and motivation to require a separate scientific scope decision. It is not needed to repair any current selected theorem. The explicit profile family, local insertion/circumradius derivation and proof-transfer discussion can remain in the packet unless the thesis deliberately adopts the product-position question.

**Limits.** General guaranteed threshold is sufficient, not always the exact first transition. Endpoint-collision family separates later profiles but NOT first transition angles. Strict lift containment does not imply capacity minimality, even locally stable polygon/smooth perturbations; do not propose proving that shortcut later.

Keep out of the selected thesis account unless explicitly presented as a motivated unresolved question. A new open conjecture is not necessary to complete the thesis.
