\subsection{Flow-Graph Proof-Risk Ledger}
\label{sec:flow-graph-proof-risk}

This ledger records the route by which the flow-graph proof obligations were
isolated and the remaining implementation risks are tested.  The idealized
mathematical claims FG-PR-1 through FG-PR-4, together with direct exhaustive
search in FG-PR-5, are superseded by the active real-algorithm proof. Their old
status prose below is retained only to identify code-correspondence and falsifier
obligations; it is not the current mathematical status of the active thesis
theorem. Cutoff optimization, singular runtime branches, and arbitrary-input
validation remain separate. The retired binary64 prototype did not have a
sound true/false/indeterminate predicate contract and supplies no current FG
accepted-output evidence. The exact verification suite is implementation
evidence for its named boundaries, not a proof of the idealized theorem.

\begin{remark}[Milestone success criterion]\label{rem:flow-graph-truth-surface-success}
For the next FG milestone, every correctness claim needed by the thesis chapter
should have one of the following states:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item proved in a checked formal/thesis source;
  \item stated as a clear unverified claim with known hypotheses and a planned
    proof route;
  \item treated as an explicit supported-scope assumption or rejection case;
  \item omitted from theorem-strength prose; or
  \item attached to a targeted falsifier suite whose failure would change the
    claim or its hypotheses.
\end{enumerate}
The intended reader for this milestone is Jörn and capable current GPT-5.6
agents, not the final thesis reader.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Current FG theorem object]\label{rem:fg-current-theorem-object}
The current exact theorem target is the closed-word realization of
Definition~\ref{def:flow-graph-closed-word-realization}.  This choice is not a
claim that every generalized Reeb orbit naturally comes with a unique word.  It
is a proof target chosen because it matches the implemented tube construction:
finite words, two-face entry and exit charts, affine Reeb segments on facets,
positive segment times, and exact action values.

\textbf{Included object.}  A represented trajectory is a closed-word
realization: finitely many transition points, positive dwell times, one facet
velocity per segment, consecutive segments meeting on the declared two-face,
and action equal to the sum of dwell times.

\textbf{Accepted theorem-strength output.}  The trajectory object and the
accepted resolver output are different layers.  A positive-time realization is
the semantic trajectory object.  Accepted capacity output additionally requires
sound resolver semantics on the chosen search domain and the boundary in
Definition~\ref{def:flow-graph-exact-accepted-output-contract}.  Zero-time
tube records, words outside the tube-supported convention of
Definition~\ref{def:flow-graph-supported-closed-word}, positive-action
singular fixed-set outcomes, unsupported zero-$\omega_0$ transitions, and
f64-only predicate decisions are not theorem-strength accepted output by
default.  They must be compressed into positive-time realizations, rejected, or
accompanied by an exact certification rule.

\textbf{Open boundary.}  The realization definition records declared entry and
exit two-faces.  It does not impose a first-hit or no-intermediate-contact
condition inside each facet segment.  A later proof may keep this convention or
refine the word when intermediate lower-dimensional contacts matter, but it
must state that choice explicitly before making a theorem-strength
completeness claim.

\textbf{Corresponding implementation/evidence.}  The exact tube resolver,
closed-word reconstruction tests, and verification rows are evidence about
this object only insofar as they reconstruct finite orbit data with
nonnegative segment times or compare exact resolver outputs under the
flow-graph convention.  These checks do not by themselves certify that every
segment time is positive.  Retained-word rows with zero-time tube records are
implementation evidence, not direct members of the theorem object.

\textbf{Thesis claim supported.}  This object supports a conditional exact
flow-graph theorem: if the resolver is sound and complete for the chosen
finite search domain and that domain contains a realization of a
capacity-minimizing orbit, then the minimum returned positive action is
$c_{\mathrm{EHZ}}(K)$.  It does not by itself prove that the current
no-repeated-facet enumeration domain contains such a realization.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Historical FG restart order]\label{rem:fg-chapter-restart-order}
The ordered tasks below predate that proof. The first three are closed for the
idealized exact algorithm but remain useful questions when auditing the Rust
wrapper. The binary64 boundary was not completed; the prototype was retired
when project time ended.
The historical restart order was not a prose-polishing task list. Its ordered
blocking tasks were:
\begin{description}
  \item[First: supported input contract.]
    Use Definition~\ref{def:flow-graph-exact-accepted-output-contract} as the
    current boundary, and either prove it for the chosen exact route or convert
    its clauses into explicit thesis assumptions/rejections.  This decides what
    the abstract theorem is allowed to claim and what must remain an experiment
    diagnostic.
  \item[Second: resolver semantics.]
    Prove or explicitly assume the semantic resolver contract in
    Definition~\ref{def:flow-graph-closed-word-resolver}.  This includes
    primitive tube soundness (FG-PR-1), tube concatenation (FG-PR-2), and
    fixed-point-to-orbit soundness (FG-PR-3), including the rejection boundary
    for positive-action singular fixed sets.
  \item[Third: completeness bridge.]
    Split FG-PR-4 into its subclaims: HK2017 simple minimizer, existence of a
    word~$\sigma_*\in D_K$ with a closed-word realization whose associated
    curve is that minimizer, resolver return of that realization's action, and
    supported genericity or rejection assumptions.
  \item[Fourth: concrete search contracts.]
    Decide which search procedure is thesis-facing.  Direct exact enumeration
    is the simplest proof target once resolver semantics are explicit.  Cached
    and cutoff search need checkable equivalence or lower-bound certificates.
  \item[Fifth: numerical boundary (historical).]
    Any numerical frontend would need a sound predicate contract or exact
    resolution of every output-relevant predicate before its accepted output
    could enter the exact theorem route. The retired binary64 prototype did not
    meet that condition and is not a current route.
\end{description}
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Proof-risk status]\label{def:flow-graph-proof-risk-status}
The status tags used below have the following meaning.
\begin{description}
  \item[trusted-scaffold.]
    The statement is believed at high confidence and mainly needs formal
    cleanup or citation alignment.
  \item[high-belief-unproved.]
    The statement is believed to be true under the displayed hypotheses, but no
    checked proof is present.
  \item[risky-unproved.]
    The statement may be true, but its hypotheses or edge cases are not yet
    precise enough for theorem-strength reliance.
  \item[implementation-contract.]
    The statement is about which outputs the current code accepts, rejects, or
    resolves exactly; it needs tests and API documentation more than pure
    mathematics.
  \item[not-load-bearing.]
    The statement may explain motivation or design, but the capacity theorem
    must not depend on it.
\end{description}
\end{definition}
\end{unverified}

\begin{unverified}
\begin{remark}[Claim FG-PR-2: tube intersection is concatenation]%
\label{rem:fg-pr-tube-intersection-concatenation}
\textbf{Status:} proved for the idealized algorithm in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fg-tube-gluing}; Rust correspondence remains tested rather than
formally verified.

\textbf{Claim.}  For two exact nonempty tubes produced from the same flat
polytope input, if the first tube's end two-face chart equals the second tube's
start two-face chart and neither tube has been silently accepted after an
unsupported transition, then their intersection/composition represents exactly
the concatenated trajectory family that satisfies both tube constraints.  Empty
intersections represent no such concatenated family.  The composed affine map
is the composition of the affine maps.  The composed action function is the sum
of the first action function and the second action function pulled back along
the first map.  Any action cutoff used during composition must be recorded as
an additional restriction, not as a proof that discarded families are impossible
below the final minimum.

\textbf{Why it matters.}  This turns primitive tube soundness into soundness for
all resolved closed words.

\textbf{Targeted falsifiers.}  Crate-local tests compare or should compare direct
construction of longer words with manual primitive composition, including empty
and nonempty outcomes and action values.  The current exact private test for
this claim checks the nonempty exact F6 capacity word case in
\texttt{exact\_tube.rs}.

\begin{remark}[Claim FG-PR-3: closed fixed points are closed Reeb orbits]%
\label{rem:fg-pr-closed-fixed-point-soundness}
\textbf{Status:} proved for the idealized algorithm in
Lemma~\ref{lem:fg-closed-tube-fixed-points}; the broader Rust singular-outcome
boundary remains an implementation contract.

\textbf{Claim.}  For a closed raw word
$(s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_{m-1},s_0,s_1)$, every accepted positive-action fixed point
of the composed tube map gives a generalized closed Reeb orbit on~$\partial K$
with the returned action, after the zero-time boundary has been handled by
compression, rejection, or a separate exact certificate.  Empty tubes,
zero-action no-orbits, zero-time records outside the accepted boundary, and
positive-action singular fixed sets are not silently converted into capacity
values.  The current exact search boundary accepts the structural
length-three zero-time case from Lemma~\ref{lem:fg-length-three-zero-time} as a
no-orbit outcome.  It also returns an exact empty-tube outcome when a singular
fixed equation is inconsistent or its fixed set misses the searched tube.  An
intersecting singular fixed set outside the structural length-three case
remains unsupported unless a separate lemma covers it.

\textbf{Why it matters.}  This is the most direct route to minimum-action
soundness in the abstract axioms.

\textbf{Targeted falsifiers.}  Crate-local tests reconstruct the
piecewise-affine breakpoints for known positive exact closed words and check
closure, on-facet conditions, inside-$K$ inequalities, segment times, and
action.  The selected deterministic capacity-word tests require strict
positive segment times, while more general tube-semantic checks use the
implementation's nonnegative tube convention.  Lemma
\ref{lem:fg-length-three-zero-time} explains the structural zero-action
length-three singular fixed lines seen in generated cases.  A crate-local
classifier test checks that a singular fixed set with positive action is typed
as unsupported rather than accepted.  The verification packet
\texttt{experiments/verification/flow-graph-proof-risk} records public-output
rows for positive exact action agreement, zero-action no-orbits, and
unsupported zero-$\omega_0$ rejection.  It also replays the hypercube word
\texttt{[0,4,1,5]}, whose exact outcome is a positive-action singular fixed
set typed as unsupported.  These rows are not independent geometric
reconstructions.
\end{remark}
\end{unverified}

\begin{unverified}
\begin{lemma}[Length-three closed words have only zero-time fixed points]%
\label{lem:fg-length-three-zero-time}
\textbf{Status:} proved here for the displayed length-three case and subsumed
by the short-word exclusion used in the idealized theorem.

Let \(K\subset \mathbb{R}^4\) be described by facet covectors
\(a_r\) with facets \(H_r=\{x\mid \langle a_r,x\rangle=1\}\).
Consider a primitive closed flow-graph word
\[
  (i,j,k,i,j)
\]
whose three primitive transitions are defined by the exact tube
construction, and assume that \(a_i,a_j,a_k\) are linearly independent.
Then every fixed point of the composed closed tube has zero time on all
three segments.  The fixed set, if nonempty, is the part of
\[
  H_i\cap H_j\cap H_k\cap K
\]
inside the start face \(H_i\cap H_j\).  In particular its action is
identically zero, so this length-three word contributes no positive-action
closed Reeb orbit for the displayed strict word.

\begin{proof}
Let \(x\in H_i\cap H_j\) be a fixed point of the closed tube.  Write the
three segment times for the flows along facets \(j,k,i\) as
\(\tau_j,\tau_k,\tau_i\geq 0\).  With the flow-graph convention used by the
exact implementation, the Reeb direction on facet \(r\) is \(2Ja_r\).  The
fixed-point equation is therefore
\[
  x+2\tau_jJa_j+2\tau_kJa_k+2\tau_iJa_i=x .
\]
Since \(J\) is invertible, this is equivalent to
\[
  \tau_j a_j+\tau_k a_k+\tau_i a_i=0 .
\]
The linear independence of \(a_i,a_j,a_k\) gives
\(\tau_i=\tau_j=\tau_k=0\).

If all three segment times are zero, each primitive next-facet condition says
that the same point already lies on the next facet.  Thus a fixed point lies
in \(H_i\cap H_j\cap H_k\cap K\).  Conversely, any point of this triple
facet intersection that lies in the closed tube domain satisfies all three
next-facet equations with zero time and is fixed by the composed map.  The
action is the sum of the three segment times, hence it is \(0\) on the whole
fixed set.
\end{proof}
\end{lemma}
\end{unverified}

\begin{remark}[Claim FG-PR-4: resolver completeness for supported minimizers]%
\label{rem:fg-pr-resolver-completeness}
\textbf{Status:} proved for the idealized regular class in
Theorem~\ref{thm:fg-real-capacity-correctness}; Rust correspondence and input
validation remain separate.

\textbf{Claim.}  On the supported input class, the closed-word/tube resolver
returns the action of at least one HK2017 capacity-achieving simple minimizer
which is the associated curve of a closed-word realization for some
word~$\sigma_*\in D_K$.

\textbf{Why it matters.}  This is the highest-risk local bridge from HK2017
minimizers to the FG capacity theorem.

\textbf{Sub-obligations.}  The current claim abbreviates several separate
proof tasks:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item import the HK2017 capacity-achieving simple minimizer in the local
    notation;
  \item convert its itinerary to the closed facet word convention of
    Definition~\ref{def:flow-graph-closed-word};
  \item prove that some word~$\sigma_*\in D_K$ has a closed-word realization
    whose associated curve is that minimizer;
  \item prove that the tube resolver returns the action of that realization;
    and
  \item state which genericity failures are rejected rather than claimed.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Targeted falsifiers.}  Exact exhaustive FG search should agree with
certified HK2017/QP scalar capacity on deterministic supported F5/F6 smoke
cases and slower F7 confirmation cases.  Failures should be classified as a
wrong resolver claim, wrong supported-scope hypothesis, or implementation bug.
\end{remark}
\end{unverified}

\begin{remark}[Claim FG-PR-5: concrete searches satisfy the abstract axioms]%
\label{rem:fg-pr-concrete-search-axioms}
\textbf{Status:} proved for idealized direct exhaustive search in
Proposition~\ref{prop:fg-real-search-correctness}; cached and cutoff Rust
searches retain the implementation-evidence boundary below.

\textbf{Claim.}  Direct enumeration satisfies the abstract axioms when the
resolver is sound and complete.  Cached construction and action-cutoff search
satisfy the same axioms only when they preserve the minimum action and the
lower-bound certificates required by
Definition~\ref{def:flow-graph-capacity-search-axioms}.

\textbf{Why it matters.}  This separates the mathematical capacity theorem from
implementation scheduling and performance optimizations.

\textbf{Targeted falsifiers.}  Exact cutoff-enabled search should match
cutoff-disabled search on capacity and retained words for reviewed supported
cases.  This is implementation evidence for extensional equality, not a proof
that cutoff certificates are mathematically valid.  Retained-word tests must
compare against full exact FG resolution, not against QP word conventions.
\end{remark}
\end{unverified}

\begin{unverified}
\begin{remark}[Historical numerical-contract requirement]%
\label{rem:fg-pr-f64-accepted-output}
\textbf{Status:} historical boundary, not a current implementation claim.

\textbf{Claim.}  Any floating-point route may return a theorem-strength accepted
capacity only when every numerical result that can affect the returned action,
retained-word set, rejection decision, or lower-bound certificate is known to
agree with the corresponding exact semantic resolver outcome. Exact agreement
may be obtained by exact resolution of output-relevant indeterminacies or by a
reviewed proof that the numerical predicate cannot affect the accepted output.
Otherwise the implementation must reject or report a diagnostic outcome rather
than return a theorem-strength capacity value. The earlier binary64 prototype
did not establish this contract and was retired.

\textbf{Why it matters.}  This is the boundary between the exact mathematical
theorem and numerical implementation evidence.

\textbf{Role.} This general requirement remains useful for reviewing numerical
implementations, but it is not current FG evidence and does not create a
future-work commitment.
\end{remark}
\end{unverified}
