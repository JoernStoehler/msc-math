How the exact HKO improvement was obtained

29 October 2026 · research postmortem · r3b · [review: postmortem_review]

The run produced an explicit convex four-polytope with EHZ systolic ratio 1.0519141388294875…, above the Haim–Kislev–Ostrover pentagon-product value 1.047213595499958…. The important achievement is the combination: an explicit body, an exact global capacity calculation, an exact volume, and a readable, checked report with a reproducible companion. A promising orbit or a floating-point search would not have established that result.

The most useful hindsight is more specific than “start with the answer.” Once the ten-cut family had been found, the later finite-body theorem did not logically need the full local-variation theory or its tiny effective interval. A direct moderate-parameter certificate could have been attempted earlier. Inside that certificate, recognizing a compact low-dimensional feasibility problem could have avoided an unsuccessful Fourier–Motzkin elimination. These are different kinds of savings: the first needs a useful research hint and gives up independent results; the second needs a better algorithmic choice available at the time. Neither makes finding the family or proving complete capacity coverage trivial.

There is also a remaining allocation question. Delivery of the first report was completed, but the inspected evidence does not establish that ending new research selection at 01:07 (before final delivery at 01:22) was the best use of the authorized remaining time and quota. A concrete continuation of the successful family remained available. That is an opportunity-cost uncertainty, not evidence that further research would have succeeded. The original run took 2 h 47 min to final delivery; the dedicated moderate-body lane needed about 24 min to its first complete finite checks, reusing the already discovered family. Those are different cost scopes.

The outcome being explained

Thus capacity falls slightly, while volume falls enough to raise the ratio. The absolute gain is 0.0047005433295293…, about 0.449% of HKO’s ratio. What actually made the result possible

The distinction between discovery dependence and proof dependence is essential. The successful family came out of local-variation research. Once its twenty normals were known, however, the final finite-body proof could stand on its own.

Solid arrows here show how the work supplies the indicated result; the dashed arrow records motivation, not an additional hypothesis of the finite-body proof. The timing and original source locators are expanded in causal evidence.

1. Inherited mathematics made several cheap-looking routes unsafe

The accepted earlier HKO theorem covered local behavior with at most eleven facets. One added facet would remain in that class; ten simultaneous facet births would not. The run also inherited exact HKO arithmetic and orbit data, which made new probes cheaper. That earlier development is a real input, but its historical cost is outside this run’s measured budget.

Early orbit samples and finite occupation-measure banks were useful tests, not exhaustive descriptions of all shortest characteristics. A finite bank appeared to miss a corner of a facet and therefore admitted a separating hinge. An exact continuous family from the old certificate filled that corner: its averaged response matched the hinge’s volume contribution. This defeated that particular attempted discriminator. Conversely, sampled moment domination did not prove domination for every convex profile. Those failed routes supplied concrete reasons to change the search rather than evidence that unrestricted maximality was true or false.

The multicut audit records the finite-bank boundary; the more detailed historical notes and corner-family locators are in causal evidence.

3. The local theorem needed two completeness repairs, not more sampling

Primitive triangle-pair words recovered the HKO capacity and gave fifty winning six-label words. That was not enough to maximize hinge response: mixtures of winning triangle pairs produce seven-label families whose responses vary continuously. The successful reduction considered all 9!=362,8809!=362{,}880 cyclic ten-orders, extracted winning bipartite graphs, and covered all maximizing weights by 190 charts: fifty points and 140 intervals. Exact piecewise-quadratic optimization then found the response maximum 1/1251/125. Earlier 710 insertion-generated charts remained discovery material, not a completeness premise.

The derivative formula also involves repeated facet visits. An existence theorem for one simple shortest orbit does not by itself bound the response of every repeated maximizing orbit. A separate elimination argument used the product geometry and characteristic invariance to remove repetitions without decreasing response. The fixed-bank sensitivity theorem, this repeated-word bridge, and the complete 190-chart calculation together made the positive derivative valid. Their roles and exact finite arguments are printed in the ascent section.

An attempted broader shortcut was genuinely falsified: for an arbitrary convex facet profile, response need not be convex when weight is transferred between duplicate visits. An exact quadratic example has a strict interior maximum, above both deletion endpoints. The later valid argument used characteristic invariance to fix the duplicate segments’ profile values. Knowing this restriction earlier could have bypassed the general-convexity attempt; discovering the counterexample also supplied useful mathematical boundary information. It was not merely an implementation failure.

These were substantive mathematical contributions. They were also how the run acquired a credible family to test at finite size. Calling them “unnecessary” merely because the later certificate can be stated without them would erase their discovery role and their separate local theorem.

The float probes were candidate selectors; no float threshold became an acceptance predicate.

6. Singular families were the decisive algorithmic detour

There were 1,030 consistent singular stationary families. A negative coordinate in one particular solution does not imply that the whole affine family misses the nonnegative simplex. An initial loose constant-only bound therefore failed to certify improvement. Fourier–Motzkin elimination was then attempted, grew to about 8 GB, and was terminated without an accepted result.

The final route used the attaining witness as a threshold. Of the singular families, 984 had constant objective at or below it and needed no further positivity analysis. A cheap global containment (25/28)H⊂K(25/28)H\subset K excluded 21 whose constant objective exceeded an independent coarse ceiling. For the remaining 25, positivity was an exact feasibility problem in a bounded parameter polyhedron. The KKT kernel maps injectively to weights; normalized nonnegative weights lie in a compact simplex. Any nonempty feasible parameter polyhedron consequently has a vertex. Enumerating its possible active zero-weight constraints proved all twenty-five empty.

This is why the final leaf check could finish in about 69 seconds, where the general elimination route had become unmanageable. A first refined run was interrupted near word 2,000 and was rerun; the retained record reports exit 143 but does not establish a reason for that interruption. The final complete run, rather than the partial output, supplied acceptance evidence.

Corrections that better contemporaneous reasoning could have avoided

The record supports some concrete mistakes, although not a large quantified waste total. An exploratory radial comparison initially used the uniform body mean, about 0.7697, where the derivative requires the normalized facet mean, about 1.1545. All twenty-six sampled orbit responses still exceeded the corrected value. A separate partner-radius statistic omitted within-segment variance: its count below the comparator changed from twenty-three of twenty-six to seven. Correct measure selection and integrating the full segment polynomial would have avoided those repairs; neither diagnostic established or invalidated the final theorem. The original corrected values and source locators are retained in causal evidence.

The latter count changed the apparent frequency of low-response orbits, but both versions still had sampled responses above the comparator. Neither could certify ascent, which would require every relevant shortest-orbit response to be below the volume response. No inspected source links either mistaken diagnostic to a downstream assignment or canceled computation; claiming that the repairs would have saved an entire branch is unsupported.

An inaccurate review parenthetical also said equal repeated normals made action constant under weight transfer. They make it affine; global maximality at an interior point forces constancy. The accepted proof already used that extra argument, so this was a false explanation repaired during review, not a broken theorem later rescued. These mistakes should be distinguished from the useful finite-bank counterexample and the sound but impractical Fourier–Motzkin attempt.

B. Better completeness reasoning at the local-search stage

Minimal extra knowledge: Equality in a bilinear average of primitive triangle-pair actions requires all positively mixed pairs to be winners. Thus maximizing mixtures can be enumerated by winning bicliques across all cyclic orders; primitive six-word winners alone are insufficient.

What changes: Treat the small feasible-section bank and 710 insertion charts as probes from the start, then move directly to the complete 190-chart construction rather than trying to certify a claim from a sample. Similarly, state the repeated-word quantifier explicitly before assuming that a simple minimizer theorem controls derivative response.

Value and risk: This could shorten incomplete-bank exploration and prevent false confidence. It would not remove the exact response optimization or the repeated-word proof. The winning-biclique argument is available mathematical reasoning, though its later success does not show that it was obvious before the product structure was exploited. The record supports several useful failed discriminators, not a proven amount of wasted time. Confidence: high that the completeness issue was real; moderate on how much earlier it could have been resolved.

  1. The product supplied a structured starting point. The ten normals split into two regular-pentagon factor planes. This made characteristic closure, cut profiles, and symmetry tractable.   2. Ten coordinated facet births gave a computable direction. The multicut line chose one characteristic-invariant radial hinge on each of the ten facets, producing twenty retained/new rows. A general first-variation theorem reduces the right derivative of log systolic ratio to the difference between the normalized volume response and the largest occupation response among capacity-maximizing characteristics. The actual hinge profiles are convex and constant along their own characteristic directions. Those hypotheses permit a repeated-visit elimination: although a simple shortest characteristic exists, that fact alone would not control every maximizing occupation measure; the elimination shows a simple maximizing word has at least as large a response. Exact enumeration then covers the remaining simple words: 190 maximizing charts (50 points, 140 intervals) give maximum response 1/1251/125, while the facet-volume response is 8/625−8τ2/93758/625-8\tau^2/9375.     This establishes nonlinear Hausdorff instability, but not a useful explicit parameter or a finite-body global capacity.
