\section[HKO Local Maximality]{Local maximality of the HKO pentagon product}
\label{sec:hko-local-maximum}
Let $P$ be the regular pentagon of circumradius one, centred at the origin
and with vertex $(1,0)$. In coordinates $(q_1,q_2,p_1,p_2)$ put
$K_0=P\times R(-\pi/2)P$, with symplectic form
$\omega_0=\sum_j dq_j\wedge dp_j$.
The Haim--Kislev--Ostrover capacity formula~\cite{HaimKislevOstrover2024} for this body gives
\[
 c_{\rm EHZ}(K_0)=2\cos(\pi/10)(1+\cos(\pi/5)),\qquad
 \operatorname{sys}(K_0)=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{5}>1.
\]
Here $\operatorname{sys}(K)=c_{\rm EHZ}(K)^2/(2\operatorname{vol}_4 K)$.
The counterexample itself is due to Haim--Kislev and Ostrover; the result
proved here concerns its local maximality when the number of facets stays ten.
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:hko-ten-facet-local-maximum}
There is a Hausdorff neighborhood $\mathcal U$ of $K_0$ among convex
four-dimensional polytopes with exactly ten facets such that
$\operatorname{sys}(K)\leq\operatorname{sys}(K_0)$ for $K\in\mathcal U$.
Equality holds precisely for those bodies in $\mathcal U$ obtained from
$K_0$ by translation, positive dilation and a linear symplectic map.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{A second ten-facet local maximum}
\label{subsec:non-hko-local-maximum}

The HKO body is not the only local maximum in the ten-facet space. Consider
the row-normal presentation
\[
A_* =
\begin{pmatrix}
0&2&0&0\\
12/7&4/7&0&0\\
12/7&-4/7&0&0\\
0&-2&0&0\\
-12/7&-4/7&0&0\\
-12/7&4/7&0&0\\
0&0&4/3&2\\
0&0&-4&2\\
0&0&-4&-2\\
0&0&4/3&-2
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P_*:=\{x\in\mathbb R^4:A_*x\leq\mathbf1\}.
\]
The first six rows define a hexagon and the last four a quadrilateral in
complementary Lagrangian planes. An exact finite certificate, together with
the neighborhood argument below, proves the following.

\begin{theorem}[A non-HKO ten-facet local maximum]
\label{thm:non-hko-ten-facet-local-maximum}
The polytope $P_*$ is simple, has ten genuine facets and 24 vertices, and
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_4(P_*)=\frac12,
 \qquad c_{\rm EHZ}(P_*)=1,
 \qquad \operatorname{sys}(P_*)=1.
\]
There is a Hausdorff neighborhood $\mathcal V$ of $P_*$ among convex
four-dimensional polytopes with exactly ten genuine facets such that
\[
 \operatorname{sys}(P)\leq1\qquad(P\in\mathcal V).
\]
The maximum is non-strict. Locally, equality holds precisely on the images
of the two-parameter product family described below under translations,
positive dilations and linear symplectic maps. The two parameters remain
independent after quotienting by these symmetries.
\end{theorem}

For completeness, write
\[
 u=1-v+v^2,\quad a=\frac12-s,\quad b=\frac12+s,\quad t=v-\frac12,
\]
and let $E(v,s)=H(v,s)\times T(v)$, where
\[
\begin{split}
H(v,s)&=\operatorname{conv}\{(-a,\tfrac12),(a,\tfrac12),(b,t),
 (a,-\tfrac12),(-a,-\tfrac12),(-b,-t)\},\\
T(v)&=\operatorname{conv}\{(u,0),(0,v),(u-1,0),(0,v-1)\}.
\end{split}
\]
At $(v,s)=(1/2,1/12)$ this is $P_*$. The areas are identically $1$ and
$1/2$, and exact continuation of all product closure cases gives
\(c_{\rm EHZ}(E(v,s))=1\) and $\operatorname{sys}(E(v,s))=1$ for parameters
sufficiently close to the base point.

\begin{proof}
For a word with positive feasible HK weights and objective $Q>0$, the
Haim--Kislev formula gives the upper bound
\[
 \operatorname{sys}(P(A))\leq
 U(A):=\frac{1}{8\,\operatorname{vol}_4(P(A))\,Q(A)^2}.
\]
Thus the selected feasible sections are upper bounds for the actual ratio;
they are not lower bounds and need not remain optimizing away from the base.
On the product family, the Lagrangian splitting reduces the complete capacity
calculation to the vertices of two planar closure polytopes. Enumerating all
624 cyclic orders gives $Q_{\max}=1/2$, 12 tight cases, and a strict gap
of $1/14$ for every other case. Exact rational-function continuation of the
closure supports and the tight cases proves the capacity and equality claims
on $E(v,s)$. In this continuation every zero weight in a consistent base
closure system is identically zero, while positive and negative weights
retain their signs. The relevant support-system ranks persist, and
inconsistent systems remain inconsistent. Thus no new closure vertex appears arbitrarily near
the base; the twelve tight values remain $1/2$ identically, and the other
values retain a strict gap on a common parameter neighborhood.

For arbitrary ten-facet perturbations, 24 positive feasible sections are
continued through the full normal chart. Their gradient bank has rank 22 and
has a positive normalized relation whose smallest coefficient is
$3/36617$. The symmetry tangent space has rank 15; the two parameters of
$E(v,s)$ add two directions, and one remaining transverse direction $W$
is checked by an exact rational-function identity. At the base its only
nonzero rows, in the same one-based labelling as $A_*$, are
\[
 W_7=(1/2,1/3,0,0),\qquad
 W_8=(3/2,-1,0,0),\qquad W_9=(1,0,0,0).
\]
Along this direction, with the feasible weights continued as in the
certificate,
\[
 \operatorname{vol}_4(P(A_*+zW))=\frac12+\frac{13}{1728}z^2,
 \qquad Q_j(z)=\frac12,
 \qquad U_j''(0)=-\frac{13}{432}
\]
for every selected section. The last displayed number is the curvature of
the feasible upper sections, not an asserted second derivative of the actual
capacity.

To pass from these base-point calculations to a neighborhood, write
$A(v,s)$ for the normalized facet rows of $E(v,s)$. The certificate
continues the twenty-four contacts and $W$ rationally along this family.
Their ambient derivative matrix $G(v,s)$ annihilates the fifteen symmetry
directions, the two family directions and $W(v,s)$ identically. Those
eighteen directions remain independent near the base, while a rank-22
minor of $G$ remains nonzero. Consequently $G$ has constant rank 22 there.
This constant-rank conclusion uses identities along the family; base-point
rank alone would not suffice.

Choose a fixed $40\times22$ matrix $Y$ whose columns complete these
eighteen directions to a basis at the base. The exact chart-rank check
verifies that this is possible. The matrix $H=GY$ has full column rank.
If $\lambda_*$ is the positive base relation, then
\[
 \widetilde\lambda(v,s)
 =\bigl[I_{24}-H(H^TH)^{-1}H^T\bigr]\lambda_*,
 \qquad
 \lambda=\frac{\widetilde\lambda}{\mathbf1^T\widetilde\lambda}
\]
is a positive normalized relation after shrinking the parameter
neighborhood. Indeed it equals $\lambda_*$ at the base, and $H$ and $G$
have the same column space. Use local transverse coordinates
\[
 A(v,s,z,y)=A(v,s)+zW(v,s)+Yy,\qquad y\in\mathbb R^{22}.
\]
The feasible sections give smooth upper bounds $U_j(v,s,z,y)$ with
$U_j(v,s,0,0)=1$. Their free-weight derivatives in $z$ are chosen by the
exact certificate to give the displayed curvature at the base.

Here is the uniform estimate that controls mixed perturbations. On a small
compact parameter patch the positive relation and rank, by the same
unit-sphere argument as in Lemma~\ref{lem:hko-negative-slopes}, give
$\min_j(G_jY)y\leq-\alpha\|y\|$ for some $\alpha>0$.
Since $G_jW=0$, Taylor's theorem gives a common $C>0$ such that
\[
 \min_j(U_j-1)\leq-\alpha\|y\|+C(\|y\|^2+z^2).
\]
The weighted upper bound $F=\sum_j\lambda_j(v,s)U_j$ satisfies
$F(v,s,0,0)=1$ and $D_{(z,y)}F(v,s,0,0)=0$. Its negative $zz$ derivative
persists near the base. Hence, for some $b,C'>0$,
\[
 F-1\leq-bz^2+
 C'\bigl(\|y\|^2+|z|\|y\|+(|z|+\|y\|)^3\bigr).
\]
All constants may be chosen uniformly because these functions are rational
and regular on a neighborhood of the compact patch. Choose
$M>2C/\alpha$. If $\|y\|\geq Mz^2$ and $(z,y)\ne0$, the first estimate
is strictly negative for sufficiently small $\|y\|$. If $\|y\|<Mz^2$,
then $z\ne0$ and the positive terms in the second estimate are
$O(|z|^3)$, so it too is strictly negative for sufficiently small $|z|$.
Each estimate bounds the actual ratio from above. At $z=y=0$ that ratio
equals one by the complete product calculation.

Finally include the fifteen symmetry parameters. The differential of their
action on this chart has rank forty at the base, so the inverse function
theorem gives coordinates for every nearby row matrix. The simple-polytope
and polarity argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:hko-row-neighborhood} applies to
$P_*$ as well and turns this into a fixed-ten-facet Hausdorff neighborhood.
The strict decrease for $(z,y)\ne0$ gives precisely the asserted equality
family and its symmetry images.
\end{proof}

This result is deliberately narrower than a global theorem. It does not
prove local maximality when facets are added, uniqueness among all ten-facet
polytopes, or any new violation of Viterbo's inequality. Since HKO has 25
vertices and ratio $(3+\sqrt5)/5$, while $P_*$ has 24 vertices and ratio one,
the two bodies are not related by any invertible affine map, in particular not
by a symplectic or anti-symplectic symmetry.
