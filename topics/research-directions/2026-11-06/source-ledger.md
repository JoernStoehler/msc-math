## L. Primary literature

### L1. Haim-Kislev–Ostrover: the external counterexample

Pazit Haim-Kislev and Yaron Ostrover, **“A counterexample to Viterbo's conjecture.”** *Annals of Mathematics* 203(2), 2026, 603–622. [Publisher](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2026/203-2/p05), DOI [10.4007/annals.2026.203.2.5](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2026.203.2.5); [arXiv version read: 2405.16513v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16513v3).

**Read:** introduction, Proposition 1.4, selected discussion, the dimension-transfer argument in the proof of Theorem 1.3, and pointers to Section 3. **Use:** published pentagon-product benchmark, distinction between a product result and unrestricted bodies, and an existing higher-dimensional transfer. No independent audit of the full counterexample proof.

### L2. Rudolf: nonsmooth Minkowski billiards

Daniel Rudolf, **“The Minkowski Billiard Characterization of the EHZ-Capacity of Convex Lagrangian Products.”** *Journal of Dynamics and Differential Equations* 36, 2024, 2773–2791; first published online 2022. [Publisher and full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10884-022-10228-0), DOI [10.1007/s10884-022-10228-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10884-022-10228-0).

**Read:** Definitions 1–2 and Theorems 1, 3, 4, and 5 with surrounding explanations. **Use:** the scalar product/billiard bridge and strong normal-cone formulation. Existence of a short minimizing polygon is not a classification of every minimizer. No blanket orbit-limit theorem is imported.

### L3. Balitskiy–Mitrofanov–Polyanskii: planar translation covers

Alexey Balitskiy, Ivan Mitrofanov, and Alexander Polyanskii, **“Triangle covering problems and the Viterbo inequality in the plane.”** [arXiv:2603.12495v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12495v2), revised 19 August 2026.

**Read:** Theorems 1.2, 2.5, and 3.3; selected proof passages and Sections 4.5, 5.3, 6.1, and 7. **Use:** finite normal-triangle covers, current polygon-factor exclusions, the general-product bound, and the pentagon frontier. Loop-area estimates need their winding hypotheses. A fixed ten-direction cover theorem does not decide unrestricted placement.

### L4. Jung–Ahn: exact translation placement

Mook Kwon Jung and Hee-Kap Ahn, **“Smallest Convex Hulls of Polygons.”** *34th Annual European Symposium on Algorithms (ESA 2026)*, LIPIcs 388, 44:1–44:20. [Official publication](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.44), DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.44](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.44).

**Read:** model discussion, Theorem 3, and the parallel-movement result in Lemma 6. **Use:** a competing exact method and convex subproblems. The theorem gives a fixed-number-of-polygons complexity bound in its arithmetic model; it supplies no measured runtime for the pentagon instance. No implementation was inspected.

### L5. Chaidez–Hutchings: polytope dynamics and smoothing

Julian Chaidez and Michael Hutchings, **“Computing Reeb dynamics on four-dimensional convex polytopes.”** *Journal of Computational Dynamics* 8(4), 2021, 403–445. DOI [10.3934/jcd.2021016](https://doi.org/10.3934/jcd.2021016); [arXiv:2008.10111v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2008.10111v2).

**Read:** Definition 1.4, Theorems 1.11–1.12, and surrounding hypotheses. **Use:** conditional orbit/action/index correspondences and a boundary for proposed smoothing work. Its symplectic-polytope assumptions are distinct from the project's generic flow-graph contract and exclude some product degeneracies.

### L6. Haim-Kislev: nonsmooth Zoll properties and cuts

Pazit Haim-Kislev, **“Dynamical extensions of Zoll to nonsmooth convex bodies.”** [arXiv:2511.16644v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.16644v1), 20 November 2025.

**Read:** introduction; Theorems A–D; Propositions 1.4, 1.6, and 1.13; Example 1.12; Theorem 1.16; Conjecture 1.17 and associated discussion. **Use:** specified quotient/compactness and cut-additivity bridges. Theorem 1.16 is an imported result whose cited proof chain was not independently checked. The HKO cut plot is numerical evidence, not a certificate supplied by this exchange.

### L7. Chaidez–Tanny: finite symmetry and Finsler geometry

Julian Chaidez and Shira Tanny, **“The Pu Inequality And The Viterbo Conjecture With Finite Symmetry.”** [arXiv:2610.31906v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31906v1), 22 October 2026.

**Read:** introduction and construction overview; Theorems 8–11 and 15; Conjecture 17. **Use:** the precise right quaternion-group action, established symmetric inequality, and separate weaker-symmetry/regularity questions. The envelope argument in agenda §7.5 is an elementary deduction. No proof audit or publication claim beyond the preprint is made.

### L8. Gluskin–Ostrover: coarse pairing bounds

Efim Gluskin and Yaron Ostrover, **“Asymptotic equivalence of symplectic capacities.”** *Commentarii Mathematici Helvetici* 91, 2016, 131–144. DOI [10.4171/CMH/380](https://doi.org/10.4171/CMH/380); [author-hosted paper](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~ostrover/Research/AESC.pdf).

**Read:** Theorem 1.6, the operator-norm definition, and related distinctions involving linearized capacities. **Use:** the factor-four comparison for centrally symmetric bodies. The central-symmetry hypothesis on the upper comparison is retained; linearized width is not identified with unrestricted Gromov width.

### L9. Artstein-Avidan–Karasev–Ostrover: polar products

Shiri Artstein-Avidan, Roman Karasev, and Yaron Ostrover, **“From Symplectic Measurements to the Mahler Conjecture.”** *Duke Mathematical Journal* 163(11), 2014, 2003–2022. DOI [10.1215/00127094-2794999](https://doi.org/10.1215/00127094-2794999); [author-hosted paper](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~ostrover/Research/VM.pdf).

**Read:** introduction and main polar-product identity. **Use:** the centrally symmetric identity \(c(K\times_LK^\circ)=4\). In the plane it gives \(S=8/(\operatorname{area}(K)\operatorname{area}(K^\circ))\). No analogous identity for arbitrary nonpolar pairs is assumed.

### L10. Böröczky–Makai–Meyer–Reisner: existing planar stability

Károly J. Böröczky, Endre Makai Jr., Mathieu Meyer, and Shlomo Reisner, **“On the volume product of planar polar convex bodies—Lower estimates with stability.”** *Studia Scientiarum Mathematicarum Hungarica* 50(2), 2013, 159–198. DOI [10.1556/sscmath.50.2013.2.1235](https://doi.org/10.1556/sscmath.50.2013.2.1235); [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.01481); [author-hosted paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~makai/karcsi.pdf).

**Read depth:** abstract and result-level bibliographic check. **Use:** planar volume-product stability already exists. No exact stability modulus, constant, or equality classification is newly imported without further reading.

### L11. Artstein-Avidan–Ostrover: Minkowski variation

Shiri Artstein-Avidan and Yaron Ostrover, **“A Brunn–Minkowski Inequality for Symplectic Capacities of Convex Domains.”** *International Mathematics Research Notices* 2008, rnn044. DOI [10.1093/imrn/rnn044](https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnn044); [arXiv:0712.2631](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0712.2631).

**Read:** Theorem 1.4 and the Section 4 discussion of directional variation. **Use:** a constraint and alternative variation chart. A capacity Brunn–Minkowski inequality is not by itself a conclusion about the capacity-to-volume ratio.

### L12. Haim-Kislev: the finite polytope formula

Pazit Haim-Kislev, **“On the symplectic size of convex polytopes.”** *Geometric and Functional Analysis* 29(2), 2019, 440–463. DOI [10.1007/s00039-019-00486-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-019-00486-4); [arXiv:1712.03494](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.03494), version displayed as v3.

**Read:** Theorems 1.1 and 1.5 and the definitions needed to compare conventions. **Use:** the complete finite scalar formula and existence of a simple minimizing representation. The agenda adopts the supplied project's normalized-row and order convention. Constants and orientation should be checked when translating from a different convention.

### L13. Leipold–Vallentin: hardness with growing dimension

Karla Leipold and Frank Vallentin, **“Computing the EHZ capacity is NP-hard.”** *Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society, Series B* 11, 2024, 603–611. DOI [10.1090/bproc/250](https://doi.org/10.1090/bproc/250); [arXiv:2402.09914v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.09914v2).

**Read:** Theorem 1.1 and the reduction's dimension dependence. **Use:** a general computational obstruction. The reduction allows ambient dimension to grow and therefore does not prove hardness for four-dimensional input polytopes.

### L14. Leipold: combinatorial and optimization prior art

Karla Halla Marlene Leipold, **“Combinatorial Approaches to the EHZ Capacity of Simplices.”** Doctoral dissertation, University of Cologne, 2026. [Institutional record](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/80566/).

**Read depth:** institutional metadata and abstract only. **Use:** exact ordering and nonsmooth optimization are established prior-art directions. More detailed section-level claims in P7 require direct dissertation checking before technical reuse; this exchange did not inspect those proofs.

### L15. Haim-Kislev–Ostrover: symplectic p-products

Pazit Haim-Kislev and Yaron Ostrover, **“Remarks on symplectic capacities of p-products.”** *International Journal of Mathematics* 34, 2023, 2350021. DOI [10.1142/S0129167X23500210](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129167X23500210); [arXiv:2111.09177](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09177).

**Read depth:** abstract; the specific equal-capacity 2-product consequence was checked in L1's proof of Theorem 1.3, which cites this paper's Theorem 1.2. **Use:** higher-dimensional transfer has an existing exact benchmark. The agenda does not claim every coupling or arbitrary product shares that formula.
