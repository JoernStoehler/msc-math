My reader-response diagnosis:

The introduction has a discernible mathematical purpose and a convincing principal result. Its main weakness is that several supporting results arrive before a newcomer has the conceptual distinctions needed to understand why they belong together.

1. **The plane distinction creates an apparent contradiction at a particularly consequential point.** The HKO body is introduced as a product in “complementary \(q\)- and \(p\)-planes.” Later, after its local maximality is announced, “every product in symplectically orthogonal planes” admits an improving small rotation. Without knowing the distinction between these plane configurations, I would wonder whether that includes HKO and contradicts the main theorem. This is not a mathematical objection; it is an unresolved reading obstacle. Add the relevant distinction at the rotation result, explicitly explaining why its hypothesis excludes the HKO placement.

2. **The contribution hierarchy becomes obscured by the product-family paragraph.** The HKO theorem clearly feels central. The other local maximum strengthens that story by showing that the optimization landscape contains another kind of optimum. Then affine deformations, rotation formulas, a sharp numerical bound, symmetry restrictions, and another rotation theorem arrive in one paragraph. “Further restrictions on how a large ratio can arise” is too broad to organize these results for me. Group them around two intelligible questions—changes to factor shapes and changes to the relative placement of factors—and explain each group’s answer before giving formulas or theorem references. The exact symmetric-partner constant is less useful here than the fact that the bound is strictly below one.

3. **The empirical result does not yet explain its own importance sufficiently.** Its caveats are informative, but the paragraph spends most of its space qualifying a measurement that has not been motivated. Why should total symplectic face area be a plausible predictor in the first place? Why is balancing polygon factors an illuminating intervention? “Previously active branches” is unexplained implementation vocabulary. The ending—no reported search or random-sampling ratio exceeded one—also makes me reassess what the empirical programme accomplished. State the empirical question first, explain the candidate measurement geometrically, and then identify the principal positive outcome: for example, discovering the limits of a proposed guide, if that is the intended contribution. Explain what the search comparison establishes despite its failure to find a counterexample.

4. **The broader-significance paragraph mostly announces connections.** The cylinder sentence gives some additional embedding meaning to the capacity, so it contributes motivation. The Mahler sentence names an equivalence but leaves “polars” and the “symmetric Mahler volume-product bound” uninterpreted. As a nonspecialist, I can register importance but cannot understand it. A brief explanation of the convex-geometric quantity and what transfers across the connection would make this genuine motivation. Otherwise, the paragraph functions chiefly as a pointer to preliminaries.

5. **Ownership of one methodological contribution is ambiguous.** “We give an expanded derivation … and an implementation” clearly identifies thesis work. “For products of planar polygons, a reduction to at most three facet directions per factor makes the calculation smaller” does not tell me whether that reduction is established literature, a thesis theorem, or an implementation choice. State its provenance and contribution status directly.

6. **The AI section offers a concrete case but leaves the assessment side abstract.** The example makes the role of AI intelligible. However, “human judgment needed to direct and assess the work” does not tell me how the thesis distinguishes an agent’s produced argument from an accepted mathematical result. One sentence about the actual assessment process would help connect the AI discussion to the reader’s reliance on the mathematics. I would not infer from this excerpt alone that such assessment was absent.

The strongest motivational sequence is the opening distinction, the failed bound, the unanswered extremal questions, and the explanation that finite methods enable both exploration and proof. The local-result paragraphs predominantly announce achievements, but they also explain their significance and limitations. The product-family paragraph is largely a catalogue; the empirical paragraph is largely a qualified findings summary; the outline appropriately announces structure.

For an MSc reader without symplectic background, one plain-language orientation to what symplectic maps preserve would also help. Deferring closed characteristics to preliminaries seems reasonable because the introduction immediately supplies an operational reason to replace that definition computationally.
